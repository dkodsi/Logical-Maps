# Arithmetic Necessitism with a necessarily Peano counting type

Assume Arithmetic Necessitism at the designated point and necessary Potential Infinity at a fixed counting type $\kappa$. Then the Necessity of Arithmetic schema holds at every counting type. The two map records take $\kappa=e$ and $\kappa=t$ respectively.

In C the Peano condition $I^*_\kappa$ is equivalent to Potential Infinity. Thus it holds necessarily. Arithmetic Necessitism supplies a rigid property $N=\mathbb N_\kappa$ and guarantees that distinct current numbers remain distinct. We first show that any arithmetical formula with current numerical parameters keeps its current truth value at all accessible points. Root weak inextensibility of $N$ suffices for the quantifier step. [Frege arithmetic and its guard](../index.html#background/arithmetic)

Zero and successor are closed operations and commute with transport. An old numerical term remains numerical by persistence of $N$. True equations persist by identity, and false equations on numbers persist by necessary distinctness. A true numerical sum or product persists. This follows by Frege induction on its second argument using the respective zero and successor equations, or directly from the map's positive transport lemmas for the graphs. Suppose instead an operation equation is false. Choose its correct actual output. Its graph instance persists and that output remains distinct from the proposed wrong one. At every accessible point the Peano recursion is functional, so the original equation remains false. These arguments cover the atomic formulas in Goodsell's grammar.

Induct on formulas for all tuples of current numerical parameters simultaneously. Negation and the Boolean connectives preserve the assertion that the current sign is necessary. If a bounded universal is true, each old number necessarily satisfies its matrix with the other parameters, by the induction hypothesis. Use that matrix as the admitted tester in weak inextensibility of $N$. It must then hold of every number at every future point. If the universal is false, an actual counterexample remains numerical and continues to falsify the matrix. Thus every closed arithmetical sentence at $\kappa$ has one necessary sign. The induction is on syntax and includes all internally nonstandard numbers.

Now fix another type $\sigma$ and an arithmetical sentence $A$ there. Use the identically worded arithmetic sentence at $\kappa$. At each accessible point where $I_\sigma$ holds, $I^*_\sigma$ holds as well and both number systems satisfy their Peano conditions. Take the least binary relation containing the two zeros and closed under simultaneous successors. It is a bijection between the number properties by the two induction principles, injectivity of successor and the fact that zero is not a successor. Equivalently this is the natural-number part of the map's cross-type categoricity proof. The isomorphism respects the additive and multiplicative graphs by uniqueness of Peano recursion. It preserves and reflects every arithmetic formula, hence $A$ agrees there with the sentence at $\kappa$. The one necessary sign just proved gives $\Box(I_\sigma\to A)\lor\Box(I_\sigma\to\neg A)$, as required. [Cross-type isomorphism](../index.html#background/arithmetic/real-structures-isomorphic)
