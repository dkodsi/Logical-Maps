Finite-Type Necessitism and Analytic Necessitism give finite-type analysis

OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 29 October 2026. This strengthens the analytic bridge in GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, Finite-Type Necessitism and Analytic Non-Contingency, §4. The fixed-carrier transport and cut constructions are from Finite-type necessitism, companion proof notes, 29 October 2026, §§4,6–8.

What must be preserved

Assume FTN and existing Analytic Necessitism (ANA). Fix an admitted host \tau, a finite height n, and \Diamond\exists\mathfrak R\,\operatorname{Real}_\tau(\mathfrak R). ANA gives a particular structure \mathfrak R, with carrier X^{\tau t}, such that

\operatorname{RigidStr}(\mathfrak R)\land \Box\operatorname{Real}_\tau(\mathfrak R). \tag{1}

We will add the required powers to this carrier. No real field is moved to a more capacious host. All functions with individual values are expressed as admitted relational graphs.

Fixed carriers and power transport

Call a rigid carrier with necessarily distinct members fixed. The following elementary facts explain the modal steps used below.

  1. Currently coextensive rigid relations are identical. Persistence of one and inextensibility of the other give necessary inclusion in each direction. Necessary coextension identifies arbitrary relations in C as well, even before their rigidity is known.
  2. Fixedness has a necessary version. Rigidity persists by 4. For ND, apply inextensibility of the rigid product of a carrier with itself; necessary identity handles equal pairs, and 4 handles the necessary inequality of unequal pairs. Finite products of rigid carriers are rigid by successive applications of inextensibility.
  3. A rigid relation necessarily supported on a fixed product has stable positive and negative instances there. A positive instance persists. If the instance at a is false, every current related tuple is necessarily different from a; inextensibility applied to the complement of its haecceity gives necessary falsity. Formula induction therefore works over fixed carriers with rigid, necessarily supported primitive relations. Restricted quantifiers use carrier inextensibility in the true universal case and persistent counterexamples in the false case. Stable definable subcarriers are rigid and inherit ND.
  4. A persistent graph which is necessarily supported on, total on and functional over a rigid domain is rigid. If its current pairs necessarily satisfy Q, take the unique image of each current input. Persistence and necessary functionality give \Box\forall y(Jxy\to Qxy) for that input. Inextensibility of the domain makes this true of every possible input. Necessary support excludes stray graph pairs. Necessitating this proved conditional, using the necessary hypotheses, supplies the leading box. Ranges of rigid graphs are rigid by pulling a test predicate back along the graph.

For a rigid necessary bijection J:A\cong B between fixed carriers, define the pushforward of a rigid supported property by

T_J(R)=\lambda b\,[Bb\land\exists a(Aa\land Jab\land Ra)]. \tag{2}

The restriction of J to R is a persistent necessarily supported functional graph, so (2) is its rigid range. Pullback along the inverse shows that (2) is a necessary bijection between the canonical powers of A and B. Its graph is rigid by fact 4. If \mathsf P(A) is rigid, its image under (2) is rigid and necessarily coextensive with \mathsf P(B). Identify these carriers before inferring rigidity of the latter. Fullness transfers by pulling back an arbitrary predicate, representing it over A, and pushing the representative forward. This argument holds at every possibility and iterates at finite height. It never assumes that an arbitrary intersection of rigid relations is rigid.

The same facts restrict a tower to a fixed subcarrier K\leq A. Inside a fixed full power of A, the condition of having every member in K is stable: use fixed A, rigid incidence and rigid K. Its carrier is exactly \mathsf P(K), because current inclusion of a rigid property in persistent K implies necessary inclusion. Restrict any test predicate to K and use fullness over A to obtain fullness over K. Repeat at the next levels. Necessary distinctness passes to rigid subproperties by a persistent point of disagreement. Thus all these represented carriers are fixed.

Activation of the canonical arithmetic

The included number carrier of (1) is fixed. Successor, given by adding the distinguished one, is a persistent, necessarily supported functional graph on it and hence rigid. The real-structure axioms say necessarily that this is a Peano presentation (K,z,S), with induction over all admitted subproperties.

Here is an explicit reason this activates \Box I^*_\tau, rather than merely resembling an infinite carrier. At a current point extend successor to the whole type by the admitted relation

T(x,y)=(Kx\land Ky\land Sxy)\lor(\neg Kx\land y=x). \tag{3}

It is total and injective, and omits z. The internal finite-cardinal Dedekind lemma therefore shows that no finite Frege cardinality counts the whole type. Its finite induction removes a point from a supposed finite carrier, bypassing that point’s predecessor when necessary; the restricted or redirected graph would again inject the smaller carrier properly into itself. The base case is empty. This argument is about internal finite cardinalities and admitted graphs, not external standard natural numbers.

Consequently every internal finite cardinality is realizable: zero is realized by the empty property, and a finite realization cannot exhaust the whole type, so adding one outsider realizes its successor. This is induction proving existential witnesses, not selection of witnesses at all numbers. If two canonical successors are identical, realize their common cardinality. Removing their respective distinguished points gives predecessor realizations; the definable transposition interchanging the two points gives a common realization when they differ. The finite cardinality Exclusivity lemma identifies the predecessors. This proves successor injectivity; zero-not-successor is already a theorem of C.

These are the Dedekind, Swap and Exclusivity arguments in the existing Frege proofs. Their composition proves in C that a supplied Peano presentation at \tau implies I^*_\tau. Necessitate that closed conditional and use the boxed Peano premise from (1), obtaining \Box I^*_\tau. No optional assumption has been necessitated and no existential has been moved across a box.

A reference continuum and its finite powers

The activated \tau-source instances of FTN now give as many fixed, necessarily full canonical powers of N_\tau as the requested finite height needs. The base is fixed by [[finite-type-necessitism-implies-arithmetic-necessitism]].

Peano recursion supplies the arithmetic graphs on N_\tau; their least recursion graphs are persistent and necessarily total-functional, so fact 4 makes them rigid. Use canonical number codes for rationals. In the first power \mathsf P(N_\tau), take those represented subsets of rational codes that are nonempty proper downward-closed cuts with no greatest member. This is a fixed subcarrier X_0, by stable definition over the fixed arithmetic and its rigid incidence relation. Cut order and arithmetic have rigid, necessarily supported graphs.

At every possibility, an arbitrary admitted nonempty bounded family of cuts has the admitted union predicate on rational codes. Necessary fullness of the first power supplies its representative. The ordinary union-of-cuts argument gives the least upper bound, so the field is necessarily complete. The image of the canonical numbers is its fixed least inductive number carrier. This proves that the cut structure is fixed and necessarily real. It does not require a selected representative for every family simultaneously.

Restrict successive canonical full powers to X_0 using the restriction argument above. Thus this reference real carrier has the required finite full tower.

The reference and (1) have a rigid necessary ordered-field isomorphism. Match their Peano numbers, hence their rationals, and relate real elements exactly when they determine the same rational cut. The Peano matching is the least relation closed under the paired successors; the usual induction proves it necessarily bijective. The cut matching is a stable definition over fixed carriers and rigid arithmetic relations. Completeness and ordered-field categoricity make it necessarily bijective. Its graph is rigid by the facts above, with necessary support made explicit.

Transport the reference tower along this isomorphism and its successive power bijections. We obtain on the original carrier X

\operatorname{RepPower}_1(\mathsf P^{n+1}(X),\mathsf P^n(X)).

Together with (1), this is the chosen FTAN instance. Each instance uses finitely many FTN instances. The argument neither collects an infinite hierarchy nor chooses a sequence of fields. It needs no separate unconditional arithmetic premise: ANA’s activated witness supplied the necessary guard at exactly the required source.

The converse toward FTN requires a possible real structure at some specified type; without activation FTAN may be vacuous. It is not an unconditional reverse arrow. The support, guard and original-host restrictions remain as stated throughout.
