Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension imply Weakly Rigid Power

Original adaptation by Codex (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; written out for the database by Codex (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 23 October 2026. The direct source is the research note Modal principles in Maximalist Classicism, §16, unpaginated. It adapts the atom-pullback method in the existing Rigid Power proof, which uses Bacon’s Could the truths of mathematics have been different?, Appendix C.2, Lemma C.6, pp. 15–16 of the separately paginated appendix, and Christopher Sun’s coextensive-weak-rigidity lemma. The strengthening proved here is not attributed to those sources.

Statement and conventions

Fix a relational type \tau=\bar\sigma t, allowing the empty tuple and therefore \tau=t. Over the fixed logic C,

\mathrm{RC}_{\tau}\ \land\ \mathrm{Atomicity}_{\tau t} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{WRP}_{\tau}.

Both premises hold at the evaluation point. Here RC is Rigid Comprehension, and WRP is the W,W,W principle: for every weakly rigid F^\tau, the property

P_F:=\lambda Y^\tau\,.\, \operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(Y)\land Y\le F

is weakly rigid. It has type \tau t. This implies the all-type result recorded in the database. We do not assume BF, necessitate an optional premise, or use RC at \tau t.

Write \mathrm{WP}(A) and \mathrm{WI}(A) for weak persistence and weak inextensibility. In particular,

\mathrm{WI}(A)\ \equiv\ \forall B\,.\, \bigl(\forall\bar x\,.\,A[\bar x]\to\Box B[\bar x]\bigr) \to A\le B.

Entailment is necessary universal inclusion. C proves that weak rigidity implies persistence: apply WI to \lambda\bar x\,.\,\Box A[\bar x], using WP and modal 4. Rigidity is necessary weak rigidity, and is itself persistent. These are the conventions of the database’s Background.

We use the following C lemma. If A,B are weakly rigid and currently coextensive, then every current A-tuple necessarily satisfies B, by coextension and WP of B. WI of A yields A\le B. Interchanging A,B gives B\le A, hence A=B by antisymmetry of the relational Boolean algebra. Thus RC at \tau makes every current weakly rigid Y^\tau rigid: choose a rigid coextension and apply the lemma.

Fix a weakly rigid F^\tau and abbreviate P_F by P.

1. Weak persistence of the weak-power property

If P(Y) currently holds, RC at \tau and the preceding lemma make Y rigid. Consequently \Box\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(Y) holds. Also, Y\le F persists, by NI (or the boxed form of entailment and 4). Together these give \Box P(Y).

This proves only the required current weak persistence of P. It does not move the current universal quantifier over Y through a box.

2. Pulling back a member selected by an atom

Let W^{\tau t} be an atom with W\le P. We show that some current X^\tau satisfies

P(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad \Box\forall Y^\tau\,.\,W(Y)\to Y=X. \tag{1}

RC at \tau supplies a rigid X currently coextensive with

Q:=\lambda\bar x\,.\, F[\bar x]\land \Box\forall Y^\tau\,.\,W(Y)\to Y[\bar x].

First, every current X-tuple belongs to F and hence necessarily belongs to F, since F is persistent. WI of X, tested against F, gives X\le F. As X is rigid, this proves P(X).

Second, each current X-tuple necessarily satisfies \forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to Y[\bar x]. WI of X, tested against this relation on tuples, gives

\Box\forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to \forall\bar x\,.\,X[\bar x]\to Y[\bar x]. \tag{2}

The quantifiers have only been rearranged inside the same box.

For the reverse inclusion, fix a current F-tuple \bar x. If X[\bar x], persistence of X gives \Box X[\bar x]. If \neg X[\bar x], coextension with Q, together with F[\bar x], gives

\neg\Box\forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to Y[\bar x], \quad\text{that is}\quad W\not\le\lambda Y\,.\,Y[\bar x].

An atom lies below each element of its Boolean algebra or below its complement. Hence in this second case W\le\lambda Y\,.\,\neg Y[\bar x]. In either case,

\Box\forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to \bigl(Y[\bar x]\to X[\bar x]\bigr).

Apply WI of F to \lambda\bar x\,.\,\forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to (Y[\bar x]\to X[\bar x]). This yields

\Box\forall\bar x\,.\,F[\bar x]\to \forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to \bigl(Y[\bar x]\to X[\bar x]\bigr).

At a reached world, W(Y) implies Y\le F, because W\le P. By T, every Y-tuple there is an F-tuple. Thus

\Box\forall Y\,.\,W(Y)\to \forall\bar x\,.\,Y[\bar x]\to X[\bar x]. \tag{3}

Now take any reached world and any Y there with W(Y). It is weakly rigid at that world, directly from W\le P. The chosen X is rigid there, because its rigidity persists. Equations (2) and (3) make them coextensive there, so the C lemma gives Y=X. This establishes (1). No future RC instance or necessary Atomicity was used. The argument works with weak rigidity of the possibly new Y; this is the point that gives W,W,W.

3. Weak inextensibility of the weak-power property

Let Z^{\tau t} satisfy

\forall Y^\tau\,.\,P(Y)\to\Box Z(Y). \tag{4}

Suppose P\not\le Z. Then P\land\neg Z:=\lambda Y\,.\,P(Y)\land\neg Z(Y) is nonbottom. Atomicity at \tau t, at the evaluation point, supplies an atom W\le P\land\neg Z. In particular W\le P, so (1) supplies a current X with P(X) and necessary identity of every W-member with X.

By (4), \Box Z(X). But W is nonbottom, so it is possibly instantiated. At a world with a W-member Y, we have both Y=X and \neg Z(Y), contradicting \Box Z(X). Therefore P\le Z. This is WI of P. Section 1 gives WP of P, so P is weakly rigid, as required.

Every construction uses only the admitted relational types, and the argument also covers the nullary case by the empty-tuple conventions.

Scope and verification

The typed result uses RC only at \tau and Atomicity only at \tau t. It strengthens the W,R,W intermediate conclusion stated in the older Rigid Power write-up without changing that write-up. The database’s all-schema WRP-to-RP implication additionally uses WRP, and hence Tame Rigidity, at the higher type \tau t; this proof does not replace that step by a same-type inference.
