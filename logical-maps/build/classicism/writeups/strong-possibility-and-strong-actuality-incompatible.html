Strong Possibility excludes every strong world

Ordinary mathematical proof, reviewed for integration on 28 October 2026. The new argument is due to OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, Strong Possibility: density, universal theory, and algebraic embeddings, §3, and Strong Possibility: products, reduced products, and minimal identity algebras, §5, both 22 October 2026, unpaginated. It uses the already recorded atomless Maximalist model identified below. No human or Lean certification is claimed.

Statement and scope

Over the fixed C, pure Strong Possibility implies, at every relational type \tau, \forall w^\tau\,\neg\operatorname{SWorld}_\tau(w),\qquad \operatorname{SWorld}_\tau(w):= w\ne0_\tau\land\Box\forall Y^\tau (w\le Y\lor w\le\neg Y). Consequently Strong Possibility and Strong Actuality are incompatible. This excludes arbitrary current strong-world entities, not merely pure-named ones. It does not exclude weak atoms or Actuality, and does not settle the consistency of Strong Possibility. No BF or optional extensionality is used.

1. Universal identity reflection

Write D=\Diamond_{\ne} and H p=\exists q(q\land\Box(\Diamond q\to p)), so D p=\neg H\neg p. If U=\forall\bar x\,\theta(\bar x), where \theta is a finite Boolean combination of identities between typed terms, then C proves D U\longrightarrow U. \tag{1} Terms may themselves contain abstractions and quantifiers; it is the outer Boolean matrix that has finitely many identity atoms.

For a current counterexample tuple to U, let q be the conjunction of its currently false identity atoms’ negations (or Top if there are none). Thus q is true. The currently true identity atoms are necessary by NI. Wherever \Diamond q holds, none of the false identity atoms can be true: NI at that point would make that identity necessary and exclude every further q-point. Thus the same finite Boolean pattern, and the same counterexample tuple, persists wherever \Diamond q holds. Hence \Box(\Diamond q\to\neg U). This true q witnesses H\neg U, proving (1) by contraposition. No quantifier collection or BF occurs.

2. One admissible Strong Possibility instance

Put D_t(w)=\forall p(w\le p\lor w\le\neg p), and consider U_t:=\forall w\,[w=0\lor D_t(w)\ne\top]. \tag{2} This is a closed pure universal Boolean identity sentence. Since \Box A is A=\top, (2) says exactly that there is no strong propositional world.

The recorded model coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root has pure Maximalism and propositional Atomlessness: use its maximalism and atomlessness arguments and pass from the stated signature theory to its pure reduct. Every strong world is a weak atom by T. Thus that recorded model witnesses consistency of \operatorname{Max}(C)+U_t. The precise side condition for the pure Strong Possibility schema is therefore met: it supplies D U_t, and (1) supplies U_t.

This use of the existing model does not upgrade its verification status. The present argument is conditional on that recorded proved construction in exactly the ordinary manner of using a database lemma, and introduces no new model verdict.

3. The same instance excludes every relational strong world

For \tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k t, define admitted terms \pi(W)=\exists x_1\cdots x_k\,W[\bar x],\qquad K(p)=\lambda\bar x.p. The empty tuple gives identity maps. The H-equivalence inside the following boxes, together with C’s relational order identities, gives the unconditional C theorem W\le K(p)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\pi(W)\le p. \tag{3} In particular W=0_\tau iff \pi(W)=0_t. The universal closure of (3) may be necessitated because it is a theorem of C, not an optional hypothesis.

If W is a strong world, instantiate its necessarily quantified decision law at K(p), at each accessible point. Since \neg K(p)=K(\neg p), (3) gives \Box\forall p(\pi(W)\le p\lor\pi(W)\le\neg p). Its nonzeroness follows from the zero equivalence. Thus \pi(W) is a strong propositional world, contrary to (2).

A Strong Actuality witness is true, hence nonzero, and satisfies this same boxed decision law. The displayed incompatibility follows. No boxed Strong Possibility or boxed incompatibility is asserted here.
