The forcing/CH conjecture: strict-smallness formulation audit

Original source comparison and connecting argument by OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 23 October 2026. The model constructions and their shared arguments retain their existing attributions. This note has no Lean certificate.

1. Exact verdict and scope

The recorded conjecture asks whether the following four premises are incompatible over the map’s fixed Classicism:

\Box\mathrm{RigidComprehension},\quad \mathrm{IntensionalChoice},\quad \mathrm{ForcingPossibilism}_{<},\quad \mathrm{NonContingencyOfCH}.

Here the subscript emphasizes the existing definition: Forcing Possibilism requires representations only of complete Boolean algebras which are finite or have strictly smaller cardinality than the proposition domain. It does not change the principle’s ID or introduce a new principle.

Under metatheoretic Choice, the existing full action model full-free-monoid-glued-constants-singleton satisfies all four premises. Thus it refutes the incompatibility as currently formulated in the database. The same conclusion is supplied by three other recorded constructions: full-free-monoid-glued-constants, full-free-product-glued-constants, and full-double-retraction-glued-constants.

This is a connecting argument from existing model evidence, not a new model construction or a proof that the premises are themselves inconsistent. It does not by itself refute Bacon’s theorem under every possible reading of his intended definitions and background language.

2. Why strict smallness is decisive

Let M be the free monoid on countably infinitely many generators. Its finite words form a countably infinite set. In its full action model the propositions are all subsets of M, so their cardinality is the continuum.

In ZFC every infinite complete Boolean algebra has at least continuum many elements. To see this, first choose countably infinitely many pairwise disjoint nonzero elements. If there are infinitely many atoms, choose countably many of them. If there are finitely many atoms, their join has a nonzero atomless complement unless the algebra is finite; successive splitting in that complement gives the required sequence. Completeness sends each subset of the sequence to its join. Distinct subsets have distinct joins: an element present in one subset and absent from the other is disjoint from every member of the latter, whose join therefore lies below its complement. This is an injection from the power set of the natural numbers.

Consequently a complete Boolean algebra with fewer elements than the proposition domain in this model must be finite. A finite Boolean algebra with n atoms is isomorphic to the powerset algebra of any n distinct arrows of M. For their finite set p, the interval of propositions below p is exactly that powerset algebra, and p is not top because M is infinite. The degenerate one-element algebra is represented by p = bottom.

These are the internal conditions required by the principle, not merely external cardinality observations. At every object full domains contain a relation with any prescribed extension. Thus every external subfamily of an internal Boolean carrier is represented by a property; every external map or isomorphism is represented by a binary relation; internal Boolean completeness is external completeness; and the internal injection comparisons and Dedekind finiteness agree with their ordinary set-theoretic readings. This is exactly the scope of forcing-possibilism-countably-many-arrows.

In particular the strict principle does not demand any infinite atomless forcing algebra in this model. The expected route from that principle to CH contingency lacks the required algebra-existence/size step.

3. The other three premises

Necessary Rigid Comprehension. The full-action construction contains the intension whose extension at each arrow is the image of a given root extension along that arrow. This supplies a rigid coextension. The same construction works at every reachable object, giving the boxed schema. See arguments/full-rigid-comprehension.yaml for the full general argument.

Intensional Choice. With metatheoretic Choice, well-order each relevant domain and use the intension selecting the least witness at the target of each arrow. Full domains contain this intension. The shared argument intensional-choice-well-ordering proves necessary Intensional Choice, which implies the required unboxed instance by T. The construction condition codomain-intensions is semantic evidence, not an extra object-language axiom.

Non-contingency of CH. At every reachable object every relation on the extension of an admitted carrier is represented. A real number structure is therefore a complete ordered field in the metatheory, and its second-order relations range over every external relation on that field. Each such field is isomorphic to the metatheoretic reals; each second-order analytical sentence therefore has the same truth value in every admitted real structure at every object. This proves Necessity of Second-Order Analysis and hence its CH instance. See second-order-analysis-extensionally-full and the recorded implication from that schema to non-contingency-of-ch.

Completeness here has its standard nonempty bounded-subset meaning. Including the empty subset would prevent any ordered field from satisfying the definition and would make the analytical principles vacuous. The background’s explanatory clause has been clarified accordingly. The present model argument is not dependent on that accidental vacuity: its type-t domain has continuum cardinality, so, in the full domains, an external bijection transports the ordinary real-field structure to that carrier and all its relations are available internally. This gives actual real structures at type t with the corrected definition.

4. Correspondence with the cited theorem

Andrew Bacon’s Could the truths of mathematics have been different?, draft of 9 October 2025, main p. 27, defines smallness as finite or strictly fewer elements than there are propositions and states Theorem 6.1 immediately afterwards, with the proof deferred. Appendix A.3, appendix pp. 3–4 (PDF pp. 37–38), instead describes SmallCBA using a non-strict cardinal bound and displays the additional requirement that the representing proposition is not top. Appendix A.1 permits the full simple-type hierarchy; the map works in the relational-output hierarchy.

The current principle record intentionally adopts the strict convention, following the separate large-model handout. The discrepancy therefore needs to be reconciled with the source, rather than repaired by silently changing the database’s established definition. Nor does the fact that the non-strict principle is stronger prove that it yields the intended CH theorem.

The remaining source-level audit must fix one precise formulation and its background language, then either transfer the countermodel to that exact setting or establish the additional forcing-algebra availability needed by the intended proof. Until then, preserve the attributed theorem statement and distinguish its intended reading from the encoded incompatibility.
