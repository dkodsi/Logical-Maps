Propositional ND and Inextensible Comprehension imply Weakly Rigid Power

Original derivation by Codex (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; written out for the database by Codex (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 23 October 2026. The direct source is Necessary distinctness and Inextensible Comprehension imply Weakly Rigid Power, §§1–6, an unpaginated research note. The definitions are those of the current database; literature novelty is not asserted.

Statement and notation

For each relational type \tau=\bar\sigma t, including \tau=t, the following implication holds over the fixed logic C:

\mathrm{ND}_t\ \land\ \mathrm{IC}_{\tau t} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{WRP}_{\tau}. \tag{1}

Here IC means Inextensible Comprehension, not Intensional Choice. It supplies an inextensible coextension of each property of \tau-relations. Both premises are unboxed and hold at the evaluation point. The database record uses the full IC schema, from which each required \tau t instance follows.

Let \mathrm{WP}, \mathrm{WI} and \mathrm{WR} denote weak persistence, weak inextensibility and weak rigidity. For a relation A,

\mathrm{WI}(A):= \forall Z\,.\, \bigl(\forall\bar x\,.\,A[\bar x]\to\Box Z[\bar x]\bigr) \to A\le Z.

Inextensibility is \Box\mathrm{WI}(A); rigidity is persistence together with inextensibility, equivalently \Box\mathrm{WR}(A). Entailment A\le B is necessary universally quantified inclusion. The background C proves NI, T and 4, and that weak rigidity implies persistence. For the latter, apply WI to \lambda\bar x\,.\,\Box A[\bar x], using WP and 4.

We will say that A has necessary internal separation when

\operatorname{Sep}(A):= \Box\forall\bar a\,\bar b\,.\, \bigl(A[\bar a]\land A[\bar b]\land\bar a\ne\bar b\bigr) \to\Box(\bar a\ne\bar b). \tag{2}

Tuple equality is the conjunction of coordinate identities. All relations, products and families below have admitted types ending in t. The empty tuple uses the same conventions.

1. Consequences of current propositional ND

First, current \mathrm{ND}_t gives necessary distinctness of any two current entities, at any admitted type. If a\ne b, the proposition p:=(a=b) is false and therefore differs from \top. Propositional ND gives \Box(p\ne\top). Were a=b at a reached world, NI there would give \Box(a=b), that is p=\top, a contradiction. Coordinatewise this gives necessary distinctness of currently distinct tuples. We have not obtained a boxed universal ND assumption.

Second, let p be persistent, so \Box(p\to\Box p), and suppose \neg p currently. Then p\ne\top, and current \mathrm{ND}_t gives

\Box(p\ne\top)=\Box\neg\Box p.

Together with persistence this entails \Box\neg p. Thus an initially false persistent proposition cannot become true. We use this observation at the evaluation point, not as an optional ND premise at later worlds.

2. Inextensible coextensions of separated persistent relations

Suppose A is persistent and \operatorname{Sep}(A) holds. Let G be inextensible and currently coextensive with A. Every current G-tuple necessarily satisfies A, so WI of G gives G\le A.

At an arbitrary reached world, suppose A[\bar a]\land\neg G[\bar a]. Every G-tuple \bar b there is an A-tuple, since G\le A, and differs from \bar a. Equation (2) therefore gives \Box(\bar b\ne\bar a) for each such \bar b. WI of G is available there, because G is inextensible. Apply it to the complement of the tuple haecceity of \bar a to obtain

G\le\lambda\bar b\,.\,\bar b\ne\bar a, \quad\text{hence}\quad \Box\neg G[\bar a].

Persistence of A also gives \Box A[\bar a]. The same tuple therefore remains a witness to

q:=\exists\bar a\,.\,A[\bar a]\land\neg G[\bar a].

The reached world and witness were arbitrary, so \Box(q\to\Box q). Current coextension gives \neg q. Section 1 gives \Box\neg q, and hence A\le G. Mutual entailment yields A=G. Consequently A is both persistent and inextensible: it is rigid.

This lemma used IC only to the extent of being given the one inextensible coextension G. It used current \mathrm{ND}_t only for the initially false persistent proposition q.

3. Products give necessary internal separation

We give the product step explicitly. If A,B are weakly rigid, their product K[\bar a,\bar b]:=A[\bar a]\land B[\bar b] is weakly persistent. To prove WI of K, suppose a test relation Z is necessary on every current K-tuple:

\forall\bar a\,\bar b\,.\, A[\bar a]\land B[\bar b]\to\Box Z[\bar a,\bar b].

For a fixed current A-tuple \bar a, WI of B gives \Box\forall\bar b\,.\,B[\bar b]\to Z[\bar a,\bar b]. Thus every current A-tuple necessarily satisfies H[\bar a]:=\forall\bar b\,.\,B[\bar b]\to Z[\bar a,\bar b]. WI of A now gives

\Box\forall\bar a\,\bar b\,.\, A[\bar a]\land B[\bar b]\to Z[\bar a,\bar b],

which is K\le Z. These are two successive WI applications; no quantifier was moved from outside a box to inside one.

Now let A be weakly rigid and apply this to A\times A. Every current pair satisfies

D[\bar a,\bar b]:= \Box(\bar a=\bar b)\ \lor\ \Box(\bar a\ne\bar b),

by NI and section 1. The predicate D is persistent by 4, so each current pair necessarily satisfies D. WI of A\times A gives (A\times A)\le D. At a reached world, distinct A-tuples cannot satisfy the first disjunct, by T, and therefore satisfy the second. This is precisely \operatorname{Sep}(A).

In particular, under \mathrm{ND}_t, a weakly rigid A becomes rigid if IC at its type supplies an inextensible coextension: apply section 2, using its C-provable persistence. Thus

\mathrm{ND}_t+\mathrm{IC}_{\tau} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{TR}_{\tau}. \tag{3}

The use of IC in (3) is essential to this argument.

4. Inextensible Comprehension descends one property type

For a tuple \bar a, let \delta_{\bar a}:=\lambda\bar b\,.\,\bar b=\bar a. These tuple haecceities are rigid in C. Indeed, NI gives persistence; at any reached world a test necessary on their current instances is necessary of \bar a, and equality substitution gives the required entailment. This proves WI at every reached world. For the empty tuple, the haecceity is \top.

Given an arbitrary X^\tau, form its singleton family

J_X(Y):=\exists\bar a\,.\, X[\bar a]\land Y=\delta_{\bar a}.

IC at \tau t provides an inextensible G^{\tau t} currently coextensive with J_X. Every current member of G is rigid, and rigidity is persistent. WI of G therefore gives G\le\lambda Y\,.\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y). Likewise it gives necessary containment in the family of tuple haecceities: each current witness to Y=\delta_{\bar a} continues to witness singletonhood by NI. These are necessary containments, not a necessitated coextension.

Put

U[\bar a]:=\exists Y^\tau\,.\,G(Y)\land Y[\bar a].

We show U is inextensible. At any reached world, G is weakly inextensible and every current G-member Y is rigid, by the necessary containment just proved. Let Z be necessary on every current U-tuple. For each current G-member Y, the same test is necessary on all its current tuples. WI of Y gives Y\le Z, or \Box\forall\bar a\,.\,Y[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a]. WI of G, tested against \lambda Y\,.\,\forall\bar a\,.\,Y[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a], then gives U\le Z. Thus WI of U holds at every reached world.

At the initial world, coextension of G with J_X makes U coextensive with X: an X-tuple contributes its own haecceity, and every member of G is the haecceity of some current X-tuple. We have proved

\mathrm{IC}_{\tau t}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathrm{IC}_{\tau}. \tag{4}

This descent is a C derivation; it uses neither ND nor any choice principle. Combining (3) and (4), the premises of (1) already make every current weakly rigid F^\tau rigid.

5. The rigid-power family is rigid

Fix such a rigid F. Section 3 gives \operatorname{Sep}(F). Let

R_F:=\lambda Y^\tau\,.\, \operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\land Y\le F.

This property is persistent in C: both rigidity and entailment have necessary forms. We show \operatorname{Sep}(R_F).

At any reached world take distinct rigid X,Y\le F. Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical in C: weak persistence of each and WI of the other give mutual entailment. Hence X,Y differ on some current tuple. Suppose X[\bar a]\land\neg Y[\bar a]; the opposite case is symmetric. Every current Y-tuple is an F-tuple distinct from the F-tuple \bar a, so separation of F makes it necessarily distinct from \bar a. WI of Y against the complement of \delta_{\bar a} gives \Box\neg Y[\bar a]. Persistence of X gives \Box X[\bar a]. Thus X,Y remain distinct.

This proves (2) for R_F, including members quantified at reached worlds. IC at \tau t gives a current inextensible coextension of R_F. Section 2 makes it identical with R_F and proves R_F rigid. This establishes the typed Rigid Power conclusion under the premises of (1).

6. Bounded propagation identifies the weak-power family

It remains to handle weakly rigid subrelations arising at later worlds. We do not assume that current Tame Rigidity holds necessarily.

At any reached world v, let X^\tau be weakly rigid there and suppose X\le F. Rigidity of F and \operatorname{Sep}(F) are available at v and all its successors. Each current F-tuple \bar a has its X-membership decided necessarily. If X[\bar a], WP of X gives \Box X[\bar a]. Otherwise every current X-tuple is an F-tuple distinct from \bar a, so separation of F and WI of X give \Box\neg X[\bar a]. Thus every current F-tuple satisfies

D_X[\bar a]:=\Box X[\bar a]\lor\Box\neg X[\bar a].

By 4, this decision predicate is persistent. WI of F at v therefore gives F\le D_X. This step covers all F-tuples at later worlds, including those not represented by a tuple chosen at v.

Now take any world u accessible from v and any current test Z there with

\forall\bar a\,.\,X[\bar a]\to\Box Z[\bar a]. \tag{5}

Every current F-tuple at u satisfies D_X. In its positive case, T gives X[\bar a], and (5) gives \Box Z[\bar a]; in its negative case, \Box\neg X[\bar a] holds. In either case \Box(X[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a]). WI of F at u yields

F\le\lambda\bar a\,.\,X[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a].

Since X\le F persists, this implies X\le Z. We have proved WI of X at every such u. Weak rigidity already gives persistence of X, so X is rigid at v.

As v and its current X were arbitrary, we have proved

\Box\forall X^\tau\,.\, \bigl(\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(X)\land X\le F\bigr) \to\operatorname{Rigid}(X). \tag{6}

The converse implication holds in C by T. Therefore the weak-power property P_F:=\lambda X\,.\,\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(X)\land X\le F and R_F are necessarily coextensive, hence identical by the relational order’s antisymmetry. Section 5 made R_F rigid, so P_F is rigid and in particular weakly rigid. Together with sections 3–4 for the initial weakly rigid F, this proves (1).

Exact scope and verification

Only current \mathrm{ND}_t and IC at \tau t were assumed. Necessary separation was obtained by WI of products; necessary rigidity of future weak subrelations was obtained by the two successive WI uses within F in section 6. There is no BF step, no boxing of an optional premise, and no use of Atomicity, Boolean Completeness, Rigid Comprehension or Intensional Choice.

The proof also supplies TR at \tau and RP at \tau, but it does not establish the unrestricted implication TR + RP implies WRP: its bounded future tameness is explicitly derived from the stated premises. The all-schema implication WRP implies RP elsewhere in the database uses the higher power-property type; it is not a same-type shortcut in this proof.
