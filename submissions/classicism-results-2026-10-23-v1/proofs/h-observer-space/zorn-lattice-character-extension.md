# Zorn supplies admitted lattice and Boolean characters over H

By OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 24 October 2026. Direct source for the arguments: `misc`, this note, §§2–6, unpaginated. The existing Zorn principle and earlier observer results are attributed in §7.

The preorder Zorn instance at the type tt supplies admitted prime filters in any proposition distributive lattice. Consequently the distributive-lattice output equations exactly characterize material joint conjunction/disjunction separation under this explicit premise. The same argument supplies material separation for the Boolean reducts. It also extends every admitted partial lattice truth character. Plenitude is a separate premise for replacing these characters by pointwise homomorphisms.

## 1. Language and premises

Work over H in the fixed relational type system. Write m(x,y) and v(x,y) for two fixed admitted proposition operations with the truth tables of conjunction and disjunction. They may be the original primitives or specifically displayed derived operations. Their expansions remain fixed throughout each application.

DL(m,v) means the pointwise distributive-lattice equations: both operations are associative, commutative and idempotent, they satisfy both absorption equations, and each distributes over the other. Put

$$
x\le y\quad:\!\!\Longleftrightarrow\quad m(x,y)=x.
\tag{1}
$$
Under DL this is a partial order, m is its meet and v its join. These facts are elementary consequences of the displayed output identities. No identity between whole operation objects, C, or extensionality of predicate objects is assumed.

Use exactly the explicit preorder formula Zorn at σ=tt stated in [Zorn supplies admitted affine characters](zorn-affine-character-extension.md), §1, and recorded in [Zorn's Lemma](../../source/topics/classicism/principles/zorn-r.yaml). Its order is on admitted proposition predicates. Chains have the record's actual antisymmetry requirement; the proof below supplies upper bounds even for all comparable admitted families. The C background of the database and its equivalences between choice principles are not imported.

A filter F is an admitted nonempty upward-closed predicate closed under m. An ideal I is an admitted nonempty downward-closed predicate closed under v. All inclusions in this note mean current truth inclusion, not identity of predicates. A prime filter is a proper filter K satisfying

$$
K(v(x,y))\ \longrightarrow\ Kx\lor Ky.
\tag{2}
$$
Its upward closure gives the reverse implication. Membership in a filter likewise satisfies K(m(x,y)) iff Kx and Ky. Thus an admitted prime filter is itself a material lattice observer.

## 2. An admitted prime filter between a filter and an ideal

**Relative prime-filter theorem.** Assume DL(m,v), Zorn at tt, and admitted disjoint filter F and ideal I. There is an admitted prime filter K containing F and disjoint from I.

Call a predicate K valid when it is a filter, contains F, and is disjoint from I. Define the admitted relation on all predicates by

$$
R(K,J):=\neg\operatorname{Valid}(K)\lor
  (\operatorname{Valid}(J)\land K\subseteq J).
\tag{3}
$$
Invalid predicates lie below everything; valid predicates are ordered by inclusion and are never below invalid ones. Consequently R is reflexive and transitive. The given F is valid.

For an admitted R-comparable family X, define

$$
U(x):=Fx\lor\exists K^{tt}
  (XK\land\operatorname{Valid}(K)\land Kx).
\tag{4}
$$
This predicate is admitted by abstraction. It contains F and is nonempty, is upward closed, and omits I. For meet closure, two elements coming from valid X-members lie together in the larger member by comparability. An element of F belongs to every valid member; the case where both elements come from F uses F's closure. Thus U is valid, and R(K,U) for every X-member K. This includes the empty family and families with only invalid members.

Zorn therefore gives a maximal predicate K. It is valid: if it were invalid, R(K,F) would hold and maximality would imply R(F,K), contrary to F's validity. Hence every valid J containing K is included in K.

Suppose v(x,y) belongs to K but neither x nor y does. The admitted predicate

$$
K_x(z):=\exists u\,(Ku\land m(u,x)\le z)
\tag{5}
$$
is the filter generated by K and x. Indeed K is nonempty, so (5) contains x; it contains K because m(u,x)≤u. It is upward closed. Given witnesses u,u′ for z,z′, the witness m(u,u′) lies in K and its meet with x is below m(z,z′). Thus it is a filter. Since x is outside K, maximality forces K_x to meet I. There are therefore u in K and i in I with m(u,x)≤i, and downward closure gives m(u,x) in I. The same reasoning gives u′ in K with m(u′,y) in I.

Now

$$
w:=m(m(u,u'),v(x,y))
\tag{6}
$$
belongs to K. Distributivity expresses w as

$$
v\bigl(m(m(u,u'),x),\ m(m(u,u'),y)\bigr).
$$
Its two joinands are respectively below m(u,x) and m(u′,y), so both belong to I. Ideal closure puts w in I, contradicting disjointness. This proves primeness. Since I is nonempty, K is proper. The character produced is the admitted predicate K itself; no externally chosen prime filter is assumed to be in the domain.

## 3. Separation and the exact endpoint conditions

If p is not below q, the admitted principal filter F(x):=p≤x and principal ideal I(x):=x≤q are disjoint. Apply §2. The resulting character K accepts p and rejects q. If p≠q, antisymmetry guarantees this case or the reverse case. Thus admitted material m/v observers separate all proposition objects.

Conversely, joint material separation gives DL(m,v): push each lattice identity through an eligible observer, use the two-valued conjunction/disjunction truth tables, and apply separation. This is the truth-profile argument of [Truth profiles and connective reducts](truth-profiles-and-connective-reducts.md), §§2–4; it needs only material preservation.

Consequently, over H+Zorn at tt,

$$
\boxed{S[\mathrm{Mat}_m\land\mathrm{Mat}_v]
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad DL(m,v).}
\tag{7}
$$
Let T and B remain the fixed H Top and Bottom abbreviations, regardless of whether m,v are primitives. Write A(E):=ET and Z(E):=¬EB. There are four exact versions of (7):

| Material observer qualification | Output structure over H+Zorn at tt |
| --- | --- |
| m and v | DL(m,v) |
| A, m and v | DL(m,v), with T greatest |
| Z, m and v | DL(m,v), with B least |
| A, Z, m and v | DL(m,v), with T greatest and B least |

Necessity of the bounds follows by profiles. For sufficiency, every nonempty filter contains a greatest T. When B is least, the principal ideal used above contains B, so the separating K omits B. The same witnesses therefore meet both conditions when both bounds hold. These are truth conditions on the material observers, not claims that K(T)=T or K(B)=B as objects.

## 4. Partial character extension

An admitted partial lattice truth character consists of a nonempty admitted sublattice L and an admitted e₀:tt whose truth values preserve m and v on L. Its values outside L are unrestricted. Extension requires the same truth value on L; it does not require equality of output objects.

If e₀ is constantly false or constantly true on L, the respective constant term extends it. Otherwise define

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(x)&:=\exists u\,(Lu\land e_0u\land u\le x),\\
I(x)&:=\exists v\,(Lv\land\neg e_0v\land x\le v).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
They are nonempty. Meet preservation on L makes F a filter: meet two true witnesses inside L and use their true meet. Join preservation similarly makes I an ideal. They are disjoint. Otherwise some true u and false v in L would satisfy u≤v; since m(u,v)=u, material meet preservation of e₀ would make e₀u imply e₀v, a contradiction.

The relative prime-filter theorem now supplies K containing F and avoiding I. Each e₀-true element of L belongs to F, and each e₀-false element belongs to I. Thus K extends e₀.

There is also an exact formulation of the admission strength used here. Over H+DL(m,v), the following are equivalent:

1. Every admitted disjoint nonempty filter and ideal admit a separating admitted prime filter.
2. Every admitted partial lattice truth character extends to the full lattice.

The preceding argument proves 1⇒2. Conversely, given F,I, put L(x):=Fx∨Ix and e₀(x):=Fx. The disjoint union L is a sublattice. For a mixed pair f in F and i in I, m(f,i) belongs to I and v(f,i) to F. For a same-side pair, both operations remain on that side: use filter meet closure and upward closure, or ideal join closure and downward closure. Hence e₀ is a partial character on L. An extension's true set is a prime filter containing F and avoiding I. This proves 2⇒1.

Both principles imply the point-separation theorem, and Zorn at tt proves both. No converse from point separation alone to arbitrary partial-character extension or to Zorn is claimed.

## 5. Boolean reducts

Suppose m,v,n,0,1 satisfy the Boolean-algebra output identities and have the H truth tables of conjunction, disjunction, negation, false and true. For distinct p,q, use §3 with the bounded lattice. Its prime filter contains 1 and excludes 0. Because

$$
v(x,nx)=1,\qquad m(x,nx)=0,
$$
exactly one of x,nx belongs to K. Thus K also preserves n materially. Material joint Boolean separation is therefore equivalent to the Boolean-algebra output equations over H+Zorn at tt, with the specified operations and bounds.

Apply this to the three familiar signatures:

- **M and N:** use original conjunction and negation, derived join n(m(nx,ny)), bottom B, and top nB. The exact algebraic condition is that these form a Boolean algebra. A adds exactly T=nB; Z already holds for every eligible material observer because B=a∧¬a.
- **V and N:** use original disjunction and negation, derived meet n(v(nx,ny)), top T, and bottom nT. The exact algebraic condition is the dual Boolean-algebra structure. Z adds exactly B=nT; A already holds for every eligible observer.
- **M, V and N:** use all three original operations. Their Boolean-algebra identities have the fixed T,B as bounds, and both A and Z follow from qualification.

The necessity of each structure and alignment is the existing truth-profile theorem. For sufficiency, apply the prime-filter construction to exactly the displayed Boolean algebra and then discard any derived-operation qualification not requested. No derived operation is identified with an unmentioned primitive.

The extension theorem also respects complements when the admitted partial domain is a Boolean subalgebra containing 0 and 1 and the partial character has the correct bound values. Its lattice-character extension retains those bound values and therefore preserves complement by the preceding calculation. No new choice of extensions simultaneously for all subalgebras is asserted.

## 6. Plenitude, whole identities and the quantifier boundary

The added Zorn premise supplies material characters on infinite admitted domains. Under the separate Plenitude instance at t,t, [Plenitude normalization](plenitude-material-normalization.md), §§2–4, replaces them by at-most-two-output pointwise homomorphisms with the same truth profile. That theorem applies to all seven nonempty M/V/N signatures and every A/Z orientation. Accordingly, under H+Zorn at tt+Plenitude at t,t, the structures in §§3 and 5 also exactly characterize their pointwise separation principles. The selected output pair need not be the fixed T,B when the endpoint bounds are not imposed.

Local Functionality at t,t and t,tt upgrades the finite unary/binary preservation equations to identities of whole operations. At full relational separation scope, typed factorization requires all Functionality: FullSep[K] iff S[K]+Fun. Thus the corresponding full typed material, pointwise and whole-operation characterizations include Fun together with the indicated algebraic equations. Propositional algebra equations alone do not supply higher-type Functionality.

Prime-filter preservation concerns the specified finite operations. It does not assert preservation of arbitrary admitted meets, joins or primitive quantifiers. In particular, neither the lattice nor Boolean result by itself gives full logical observer separation, C, BF or Atomicity. Those conclusions require their separately stated qualifications and premises in the connected map. The proof of partial extension also makes no admission claim about an external family of chosen extensions.
