## Meaning of the statuses and queue stages

- **Ready argument:** a completed ordinary argument is available for formalization. This is not a fresh independent audit or a correctness guarantee.
- **Agent-audited:** a saved report explicitly records a separate-agent mathematical check. It is neither human checking nor Lean certification.
- **Proposed:** the source explicitly retains unresolved independent-verification obligations. Its statement may be formalized, but its conclusion must not become an imported axiom of another claimed proof.
- **Conditional:** retain every consistency, coding, source-theorem, or model-existence hypothesis as an explicit dependency.
- **Open:** formalize established lemmas or an exact reduction, not the conjectural conclusion.

Stages concern dependency cost, not hours: **S1** existing C proof library; **S2** represented orders, chains and fixed points; **S3** typed interpretations/action-model closure; **S4** internal numbers, reals and probability; **S5** forcing, set theory, computability and metalogical constructions; **S6** explicitly extended object language. A result can have an S1 obstruction and an S5 consistency witness. Costs are estimates from the saved arguments and current library file map, not a claim that all dependencies have been searched or built.

The certificate target is derivability in the original C, with exactly the displayed premises and type instances. The shallow gate must reject unrestricted use of Lean's `propext`, `funext` and `Classical.choice`; object-language translation and the schema bridge are separate obligations. Model proofs instead need soundness/interpretation and verified verdicts. A bare Mathlib analogue, an assumed missing lemma, a Lean file that merely elaborates, and `pmap validate` are not interchangeable with that certificate. Follow [Lean README](../../../topics/classicism/lean/README.md) and [verification policy](../../../topics/classicism/lean/VERIFICATION.md); the latter's historical numeric totals are not current verification counts.

1. Finish S1 certificates already assigned: uniform half-comprehension/evaluator kernels; ND+IC-to-WRP; atom projection/coordinates; splitting obstruction. Use unique modules and serialize expensive builds.

4. Transfer the strongest refinements through S3–S4: exact numerical core, finite-fibre probability, type ceiling and finite-class symmetrization. An external probability theorem alone leaves the internal-C bridge outstanding.

Keep S5 projects explicit in the queue: finite-basis/type-t symmetric models; Max splitter's set theory and rigidity input; SP embedding/quotient and arithmetic-complexity theorems; all effective-model and completion-space results. Break each into independently certifiable interfaces, but never present a conditional wrapper as a finished proof of the target.
