# Symmetric ideally-full model: an atomless Boolean prefix with recurrent colour subalgebras

Ordinary mathematical proof, Codex, 26 October 2026. Direct source category: `misc`. This is a new construction and argument, not a Lean certificate or a human-checked result. No literature novelty is asserted.

## 1. Statement

The strengthening is false. There is a pointed model of the fixed framework C satisfying

    RC_all + □BF_all + □RP_all + ¬WRP_et + ¬□TR_et.

Here RC is Rigid Comprehension. All positive schemas range over every admitted relational type. In particular, the model satisfies current TR, Boolean Completeness and Actuality, by the usual consequences of RC. No choice principle, C5, or necessary RC is asserted.

The key difference from the unsuccessful vector-space prefix is algebraic. In a countable atomless Boolean algebra, one nonzero element contains arbitrarily complicated finite Boolean partitions. A single pinned element in an entrance kernel can therefore absorb all the finitely many kernel pieces of each tuple's pin, with no bound on that pin's size. This permits the full higher-type lifting required for RP.

The semantic framework is Dorr's symmetric ideally-full construction: *Boolean Completeness does not imply Rigid Comprehension*, draft of 30 July 2026, Definitions 15–18, p. 8, and the finite-pinning semantics recorded in `topics/classicism/groups/symmetric-ideally-full.yaml`. The construction below is the direct source of the new verdicts. The elementary Boolean-algebra facts used in the proof are established in §2.

## 2. Elementary Boolean-algebra facts

Let B be the countable atomless Boolean algebra, presented as the free Boolean algebra on countably many generators. Its elements, including 0 and 1, will be the individuals of a world. A finite set of elements generates a finite Boolean subalgebra. Every nonzero interval [0,b], with b as its relative top, is again a countable atomless Boolean algebra.

We use the following facts.

**(a) Finite isomorphisms extend.** An isomorphism between finite subalgebras of B extends to an automorphism of B. More generally the two copies of B may be different. To see this, match the finitely many corresponding nonzero atoms of the subalgebras. Their relative intervals are countable atomless Boolean algebras. An isomorphism between two such intervals is obtained by back-and-forth: when adding an element, split the finitely many existing nonzero cells in the other algebra in the same pattern, using atomlessness. Combine the interval isomorphisms. This also proves the stated uniqueness of the countable atomless algebra and its nonzero intervals.

**(b) Finite homomorphisms extend surjectively.** If A is a finite subalgebra of B and f:A→B' is a Boolean homomorphism, it extends to a surjective Boolean homomorphism B→B', where B' is another countable atomless algebra. For each atom a of A with f(a)≠0, choose a relative Boolean-algebra isomorphism [0,a]→[0,f(a)]. For an atom with f(a)=0 use the zero map. The product of these maps is a Boolean homomorphism extending f. It is surjective because the nonzero f(a)'s partition 1 in B'.

**(c) Finite partitions lift through a surjection.** If h:B→B' is surjective, s∈B, and b_1,...,b_r is a partition of h(s), allowing zero parts, there is a partition a_1,...,a_r of s, allowing zero parts, with h(a_i)=b_i. Lift the b_i's into [0,s], make the lifts disjoint successively, and add the residual part of s to one piece. Equivalently use the finite Boolean algebra generated by arbitrary lifts and group its atoms by their images. A positive image forces its preimage piece to be nonzero.

Call ker(h) **dense** when every nonzero b∈B contains a nonzero z≤b with h(z)=0. This refers to the kernel ideal in the Boolean algebra, not to fibres of a linear map.

**(d) Dense-kernel surjections exist and are closed under the required compositions.** On a free presentation, send generators a_{2n} to the n-th generator of B', and send a_{2n+1} to 0. The resulting homomorphism is onto. If b≠0 depends on finitely many generators, a fresh odd generator a_j makes b∧a_j a nonzero kernel element below b. Thus the kernel is dense. Precomposing with an automorphism preserves density; postcomposing with any Boolean homomorphism preserves it because the new kernel contains the old one. Surjectivity is preserved when both maps are surjective.

**(e) Finite lifting by an automorphism.** Suppose h,h':B→B' are surjective, ker(h) is dense, and they agree on a finite subalgebra P. For every finite subalgebra M⊇P there is an automorphism u of B fixing P pointwise such that

    h u |_M = h'|_M.                                      (1)

For each atom s of P, let m_1,...,m_r be the atoms of M below it. We require a partition n_1,...,n_r of s into nonzero parts with h(n_i)=h'(m_i). If h(s)=0, any partition into r nonzero parts works. Otherwise reserve a nonzero kernel piece z<s, supplied by density. Split z into enough nonzero pieces for those indices whose required image is zero. On s\z lift the partition of h(s) given by the positive required images, using (c); assign any unused part of z to one positive-image piece. This gives the required nonzero partition. The correspondence m_i↦n_i is an isomorphism of finite subalgebras fixing P. Extend it by (a).

In (e), h' need not have dense kernel. In the model application both entrance maps do.

## 3. The category and the admitted domains

There is one prefix object A, with base a countable atomless Boolean algebra. Its only endomorphisms are all Boolean-algebra automorphisms; these also constitute its symmetry group.

The tail has objects V_m for each integer m≥0, and V_∞. Each base is a countable atomless Boolean algebra with a distinguished colour subalgebra C_V:

* At V_m use a free presentation on c_0,...,c_{m-1},d_0,d_1,... and let C_m be the finite subalgebra generated by the c_i's. In particular C_0={0,1}.
* At V_∞ use a free presentation on c_0,c_1,...,d_0,d_1,... and let C_∞ be the infinite subalgebra generated by the c_i's.

Between any two tail objects V,W take **all surjective Boolean homomorphisms** f:V→W such that f[C_V]⊆C_W. Tail symmetry groups consist of all Boolean automorphisms taking the colour subalgebra onto itself.

From A to each tail object take all surjective Boolean homomorphisms with dense kernel. There is no colour restriction at A. There are no arrows from the tail to A.

This is a category. Colour preservation and surjectivity are closed under composition. For an entrance followed by a tail arrow, the composite has dense kernel by §2(d). Precomposition of an entrance by an automorphism is also allowed. The chosen symmetries are invertible arrows. Arrows between the tail objects exist: assign the colour generators values in the target colour subalgebra and use infinitely many remaining d-generators to enumerate generators of the entire target algebra.

At every object use the ideal of finite subsets of its base, and the full symmetric ideally-full domains for this data. Individuals are the base Boolean-algebra elements. This is therefore a model of C by the cited construction. Evaluation is at the identity of A.

The Boolean-algebra operations are used to specify the semantic category. They need not be added to the language of C. Likewise the externally described subsets and orbit unions below are justified as admitted intensions by symmetry and finite pinning, not by an unasserted internal definability principle.

For a source entity or tuple a, write h*a for transport. Every such tuple has a finite base pin M. If two arrows with the same target agree on M, their transports of a are identical as whole entities. For a relation F write F_h for its extension at h. A symmetric profile pinned by a finite S is admitted; its transport along h is pinned by h[S].

Two general C facts will be used. An admitted exact-image profile F_h=h[D] is rigid, because every further extension is the exact image of the extension at the current point. Also, currently coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical as whole relations: each is below the other by weak persistence and weak inextensibility. Finite unions of tuple haecceities are rigid.

## 4. Boxed BF at every type

Every base arrow in the category is surjective. The finite-section argument for symmetric ideally-full models therefore makes every transport surjective at every admitted type. Here is its scope explicitly.

For h:V→W and a relational entity Y at W pinned by a finite T, choose a finite section s:T→V with h s=id_T. Define a profile X at V at an arrow j:V→U by using the extension of Y at any k:W→U satisfying k|_T=j s, and use the empty extension if there is no such k. This is well-defined because T pins Y; it is symmetric and pinned by s[T]. At j=l h such a k always exists, namely l, and the condition on T is exactly agreement with l there. Consequently h*X=Y. At individual type surjectivity is an assumption on the arrows themselves.

Thus all instances of BF hold at every object, giving □BF_all at A. In particular every universal relation is rigid.

## 5. Full Rigid Comprehension at A

We prove a stronger admission lemma. Let D be any set of tuples of A-entities at fixed admitted argument types. Suppose D is invariant under every automorphism fixing a finite subalgebra P of A pointwise. Then

    G_h := h[D]                                            (2)

is an admitted exact-image profile, pinned by P, and hence rigid.

For pinning, let h,h' have the same target and agree on P. If that target is A, both are automorphisms, and u=h^{-1}h' fixes P. If it is a tail object, both are dense-kernel surjections. Given a∈D, enlarge its finite pin to a finite subalgebra M containing P and use §2(e) to find u fixing P with h u=h' on M. In either case

    h'*a = h*(u*a) ∈ h[D].

Invariance of D supplies u*a∈D. Interchanging h and h' gives equality of the two image sets. Symmetry is immediate: g(h[D])=(gh)[D]. The same finite-pin condition holds after every further composition, so this is equality of whole transported relations, not just their present extensions. This proves admission and (2).

Now let F be any current relation at A and let P contain a finite pin of F. Its extension D=F_id is invariant under all automorphisms fixing P: such an automorphism fixes F, and the symmetry condition transports the current extension accordingly. Apply the admission lemma. G is rigid and currently coextensive with F. This is RC at every relational type, with no restriction on the types of the tuple coordinates.

Consequently current TR holds at every relational type as well.

## 6. A single kernel element normalizes every finite tuple pin

This is the extra ingredient for full RP. Fix an entrance h:A→V and a finite subalgebra P⊆A. Choose z∈ker(h) as follows. For each atom s of P with h(s)=0 include all of s in z. For each other atom choose a nonzero z_s≤s in ker(h), and include z_s. Then

    h(z)=0;
    every nonzero P-atom meets z;
    every P-atom with zero h-image lies below z.             (3)

For a live P-atom s, z∧s<s because h(s)≠0. Put S=⟨P,z⟩, a finite Boolean subalgebra.

**Normalization lemma.** For every finite subalgebra M⊇P there is an automorphism u fixing P such that h u=h on M and, in the finite subalgebra

    L=⟨u[M],S⟩,

every element l with h(l)=0 lies below z.

Proof: work separately in each atom s of P. If h(s)=0 then s≤z and there is nothing to normalize. If h(s)≠0, consider the M-atoms m_i below s. Partition s\z into pieces indexed by those i with h(m_i)≠0, with the prescribed nonzero h-images h(m_i), using §2(c). For the remaining indices partition z∧s into the required number of nonzero pieces. If there are no such indices, add all of z∧s to one positive-image piece. These choices give a partition n_i of s into nonzero parts, with h(n_i)=h(m_i), in which every part outside z has nonzero h-image. The finite isomorphism m_i↦n_i extends to an automorphism u fixing P. After adjoining z, the only zero-image atoms are inside z, proving the claim.

In particular, given any tuple a, take M to contain its pin and P. The normalized tuple b=u*a has

    h*b=h*a,

and has a finite pin L containing S on which all h-kernel elements lie below the **same fixed z**. The choice of z depends only on h and P, not on a or on the size of its pin.

## 7. Exact lifting of rigid subrelations at finite-colour targets

Let F be a rigid relation at A, h:A→V_m an entrance, and Y a rigid relation at V_m with Y≤h*F. Then there exists a rigid X at A with

    X≤F,                 h*X=Y.                            (4)

First, by §5, every root rigid F is the exact-image profile of its current extension D: apply that lemma to D, then use coextensive-weak uniqueness to identify the result with F.

Let T be a finite pin of Y. Choose a finite subalgebra P of A containing a pin of F and preimages under h of every member of T and of the finite colour subalgebra C_m. Thus T∪C_m⊆h[P]. Choose z and S as in §6.

Call a tuple b **normalized** if it has some finite subalgebra pin L⊇S such that h(l)=0 for l∈L implies l≤z. Let E be the following subset of D:

    E = union of the Aut(A/P,z)-orbits of all normalized b∈D
        for which h*b belongs to the current extension of Y.

Here Aut(A/P,z) means automorphisms fixing P and z pointwise. E is invariant under that group, and E⊆D because these automorphisms fix F. By §5 it defines a rigid root exact-image relation X, with X≤F.

We claim h[E]=Y_id. For the nontrivial inclusion, take a normalized b used in the definition, a pin L as above, and an automorphism u fixing S. The prescription

    v_0(h(l)) := h(u(l))      (l∈L)                        (5)

is a well-defined Boolean homomorphism on the finite algebra h[L]. Indeed h(l)=h(l') implies l△l'≤z by normalization; u fixes z, so h(u(l△l'))=0. It fixes h[P], hence T and C_m. By §2(b), extend v_0 to a surjective Boolean endomorphism v of V_m. It fixes C_m pointwise, so it is an allowed tail arrow; it fixes T, so v*Y=Y.

The two entrance arrows h u and v h agree on L. Therefore

    h*(u*b) = v*(h*b) ∈ Y_id,

where membership uses persistence of Y and v*Y=Y. This proves h[E]⊆Y_id.

Conversely, take y∈Y_id. Because Y≤h*F and F has its exact-image profile, choose a∈D with h*a=y. Normalize a by §6 using an automorphism fixing P. The resulting b is still in D, has h*b=y, and belongs to E. Thus Y_id⊆h[E].

Now h*X and Y are rigid and currently coextensive at V_m, so they are identical as whole relations. This proves (4) for arbitrary tuple argument types. No bound on the tuple pin, internal finiteness principle, or choice operation inside C has been used.

## 8. Finite-colour approximations of an infinite-colour target

For every finite set L of base elements of V_∞ there are a finite-colour object V_m and tail arrows

    c:V_∞→V_m,       k:V_m→V_∞

such that k is a Boolean-algebra isomorphism on the bases and k c fixes L pointwise.

To construct them, choose a finite list of c-generators and a finite list of d-generators on which all elements of L depend. Let C' be the finite subalgebra generated by that list of c-generators. Choose a base isomorphism k:V_m→V_∞ carrying C_m onto C'. This exists by §2(a), or directly by bijecting the remaining free generators.

Define a surjective Boolean endomorphism r of the base of V_∞ that fixes the listed c- and d-generators, sends the other c-generators into C' (0 suffices), and uses infinitely many unlisted d-generators to enumerate generators of the entire target algebra. Then r[C_∞]⊆C' and r fixes L. Set c=k^{-1}r. Both c and k are colour-preserving surjections, and k c=r has the required property.

The base inverse of k is used only in defining c; no assertion is made that k^{-1} alone is an allowed arrow from the infinite-colour object to V_m.

## 9. Full Rigid Power at A

Fix an arbitrary rigid root relation F, and put R_F(X)=Rigid(X)∧X≤F. This property is weakly persistent in C. To prove weak inextensibility, let Z be an arbitrary admitted root test necessarily true of every current R_F-member, and let N be a finite pin of Z. Take any arrow h:A→V and any rigid Y≤h*F at its target.

If V=A, invert the automorphism h to lift Y. If V=V_m, use the exact lift (4). In both cases obtain a current rigid X≤F with h*X=Y, so the cover hypothesis gives Z_h(Y).

If V=V_∞, let T be a finite pin of Y. Apply §8 with L=T∪h[N]. This gives c,k with r=k c fixing both sets. The composite c h is an entrance because its kernel contains the dense kernel of h. The relation c*Y is rigid and lies below (c h)*F. By §7 choose a current rigid X≤F such that

    (c h)*X=c*Y.

Set j=k c h. Then

    j*X=(k c)*Y=Y,

because k c fixes the pin T. Also j agrees with h on N, because k c fixes h[N]. The cover hypothesis at j gives Z_j(Y), and pinning of Z gives Z_h=Z_j. Hence Z_h(Y), as required.

This proves weak inextensibility of R_F against **every admitted higher-type test**. Current TR, already supplied by §5 at the type of R_F, makes R_F rigid. Thus RP holds at every relational type at A.

It also holds necessarily. Under □BF the universal relation at every type is rigid. RP at the universal relation makes the predicate R(X)=Rigid(X) rigid. Every current member F of R satisfies the persistent condition Q(F)=Rigid(R_F), by current RP. Weak inextensibility of R propagates Q to every future rigid F. This is □RP at the given type. The argument is the general lemma also recorded in RECURRENT-COLOUR-COUNTERMODEL.md §11.

## 10. A future weak but nonrigid colour relation

At V_∞ define U by the profile

    U_h = C_W       for every tail arrow h:V_∞→W.

This profile is symmetric and empty-pinned. Colour preservation gives full persistence.

To prove weak inextensibility, let Z be any admitted unary test necessarily true of every current member of C_∞, and let P be a finite pin of Z. Choose finite lists of c- and d-generators sufficient to express every element of P. For any tail arrow h:V_∞→W and any y∈C_W, choose a fresh c-generator x. Define a new homomorphism j by retaining h's values on the listed generators, setting j(x)=y, sending all other c-generators to elements of C_W, and using infinitely many remaining d-generators to generate all of W. It is a colour-preserving surjection and agrees with h on P. Since x is a current member of C_∞, the cover hypothesis gives Z_j(jx), namely Z_j(y). Pinning gives Z_h(y). This works for every h and y, so U is weakly rigid at V_∞.

It is not rigid. There is a tail arrow l:V_∞→V_0, obtained by sending all c-generators to 0 and using d-generators for surjectivity. At V_0 the transported U has the finite current extension {0,1}. The rigid relation H=δ_0∨δ_1 is necessary of both current members. But a base isomorphism k:V_0→V_∞ is an allowed colour-preserving arrow, and there (k l)*U has extension C_∞, whereas k*H has extension {0,1}. Therefore l*U is not weakly inextensible. So U is weakly rigid and nonrigid at V_∞.

There is an entrance A→V_∞ by §2(d). It follows that A fails □TR_et.

Finally take the rigid universal unary relation at A. Its current weak subrelations are rigid, by current RC/TR. The predicate Rigid is therefore a test necessarily true of every current member of its weak power. At V_∞ the future U is a weak subrelation of the universal relation but fails that test. Hence the weak power is not weakly inextensible, proving ¬WRP_et at A.

## 11. Scope and audit points

This settles the strengthening search by a countermodel. The model has full current RC and full boxed BF and RP, while WRP fails already at et. RC consequently cannot be boxed here. The earlier recurrent finite-colour model remains a separate, simpler separator without RC.

The source's actual-world atom is the proposition consisting of arrows with target A. It is empty-pinned and contains every root symmetry. Every true proposition includes these arrows by symmetry, so it witnesses Actuality. Its transport is bottom at every tail object, since there are no return arrows to A. Thus the countermodel meets the no-return restriction in RC-STRENGTHENING.md §6.

The decisive checks are (i) dense kernels for the root exact-image admission lemma, (ii) a single kernel element z that works for all finite tuple pins, (iii) the well-defined quotient prescription (5), (iv) fixing the *entire finite* target colour subalgebra when extending that prescription, and (v) using finite-colour approximation only after including both the test pin and the witness pin. None is an assumption of a uniform rigidifier or of necessary RC.

As a concrete check on the obstruction that defeated the vector prefix, consider finite Boolean-independent families of root individuals. Their images can be arbitrary finite target sets, because any missing independence patterns can be placed in a nonzero kernel interval. The target family of finite Boolean subalgebras is a natural rigid subfamily of the finite-set family. It has an old invariant lift: require the source independent family's reductions modulo the pinned z to form a finite Boolean subalgebra of [0,¬z]. Automorphisms fixing z preserve that condition. Its h-image is a finite subalgebra since h(z)=0. Conversely, any finite target subalgebra lifts to one in [0,¬z]; its finitely many elements can be made Boolean-independent by adjoining suitable parts of z, since the atomless interval [0,z] can realize every required finite Boolean pattern. There is no uniform bound on the number of those patterns. This example is explanatory; §§6–9 prove the general lifting theorem without restricting to this family.
