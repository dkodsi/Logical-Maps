1. THE THEOREM AND ITS MEANING

In the fixed relational type system of C,

  C + boxed Functional Choice -> full CoRevSep,
                              -> necessary full CoRevSep.       (1)

CoRevSep asks for one admitted, pairwise commuting family of reversible
logical observers that collectively distinguishes all distinct relations.
For the full schema, each finite list of quantifier qualifications and
each relational output type is included, as defined in
object-language-and-finite-basis.txt and joint-injective-separation.txt.
Our construction supplies the SAME endpoint-membership family for all
these instances. Each observer and its inverse preserve Boolean operations
and every admitted typed quantifier as whole-operation identities.
The same family continues to witness separation at every world: for each
finite instance the stronger conclusion is

  exists K.[Stable(K) and Box CoRevWitness_(tau,Delta)(K)],     (1a)

where CoRevWitness is the conjunction inside CoRevSep, with K left free,
and Stable(K) says every K(E) is Top or Bottom. The constructed K is common
to all the instances. This is stronger than just Box exists K.

In particular the proposed implication to CoInjSep is proved. Choice
provides more than individual distinguishing operations: it lets them be
organized into a single compatible, reversible system.

The sharper sufficient premise is

  C5 + Atomicity + an admitted well-order of the world atoms
       -> stable full CoRevSep.                                (2)

No quantified Infinity axiom is required. Internally finite atom fields
and internally infinite atom fields are handled separately. The argument
also applies to nonstandard Henkin models: an internally finite field is
not assumed to be externally standard-finite. No external family of
functions is declared admitted merely because it exists in set theory.

2. THE RIGID CLASSICAL FRAGMENT

Write T for C5+Atomicity, equivalently C+boxed Plenitude. Let

  W(w) := Atom_t(w),          At(w,p) := Box(w -> p).

T supplies C5, BF at every type, Functionality, necessary Actuality, and
Plenitude. At every evaluation point there is exactly one true atom a.
W and all identities are endpoint-valued. For an atom w, At(w,-) obeys
the classical Boolean laws and the typed quantifier law

  At(w, forall x.Fx) iff forall x.At(w,Fx).                    (3)

Moreover p=q iff every atom has the same At-value on p and q. These are
the atomic truth and representation lemmas in
../../../classicism-results-2026-10-24-v2/source/research/2026-10-22/evaluation-and-choice/structural-world-representation.md, Sections 1-4.

We do the set constructions below with admitted endpoint-valued predicates
and graphs. Their current extensions behave classically; coextensive such
predicates are identical, by BF and Intensionality. Quantifying over these
objects, or taking an impredicatively defined intersection of them, is
permitted at the indicated fixed types. C5 and BF make the resulting
predicates endpoint-valued too. All references below to least inductive
relations or families mean this internal construction, not an intersection
over all external subsets.

An arbitrary currently admitted predicate P has an endpoint-valued
representative with the same current extension: at the true atom a use
lambda x.At(a,Px). The same applies to binary relations. Thus an admitted
current well-order of W can be replaced by an endpoint-valued well-order
with the same current order. All subsequent well-order reasoning is in
the rigid fragment, over admitted rigid subsets of W.

Unique-value graphs whose output type is relational become operations by
Plenitude, extending by a fixed default outside their fields. Every output
type used below is relational (t, tt, or an iterated relational type).
There is no operation returning e and no implicit type extension.

3. TWO INTERNAL SET-THEORETIC LEMMAS

The following previously established proofs apply to any admitted rigid
field at a fixed type, not only to the full individual domain.

(a) Internal Schroeder-Bernstein: admitted injections both ways give an
admitted bijection. If f:X->Y and j:Y->X are injections, let D be the
least admitted X-subset containing X minus j[Y] and closed under j o f.
Then D=(X minus j[Y]) union j[f[D]]. Use f on D and the inverse graph of
j on X minus D. This is a bijection; its unique-value graph is an operation
when needed. See ../../../classicism-results-2026-10-24-v2/source/research/2026-10-22/evaluation-and-choice/structural-world-representation.md, Section 6.

(b) Internal infinite-square lemma: an internally infinite admitted
well-ordered field X admits an injection X times X -> X. The proof in
Section 10 of the same source is recalled to identify its assumptions.

Suppose X is a counterexample and take a least bad initial segment K:
choose the least endpoint of a bad proper segment if one exists, and
otherwise take X. Every proper initial segment is finite or has a square
injection. No proper segment is equipotent to K. K has no greatest point:
if K=L union {m}, L is infinite and L squared injects into L; two distinct
points of L give K -> L squared -> L, contradicting Schroeder-Bernstein
and the preceding assertion.

Well-order K squared by (max(x,y),x,y). Every proper predecessor set P
is contained in J squared for a proper initial segment J of K. No K
injects into P: if J is finite this contradicts finiteness heredity; if
J is infinite, the square injection of J gives K -> J and contradicts
Schroeder-Bernstein. But internal comparison of the two well-orders K
and K squared embeds one onto an initial segment of the other. Embedding
K squared into K contradicts badness. Embedding K into K squared must
have proper range, contradicting the preceding restriction on P.

Comparison is internal: take the admitted union of all initial-segment
order-isomorphisms. Least disagreement proves compatibility. If neither
side is exhausted, adjoining the two least omitted points extends the
union, contradiction. Products and pairs can be coded by haecceities.

Finiteness in these arguments is internal Frege finiteness. Its invariance
under represented bijections, injection heredity, and closure under finite
unions and products follow by successor-removal induction at fixed types;
the details are in the cited Section 10. The square proof needs the given
well-order, these finiteness lemmas, and internal Schroeder-Bernstein.
It has no remaining use of Functional Choice. Plenitude supplies any
operation needed from a unique-value graph.

4. INTERNALLY FINITE FIELDS: ONE CYCLIC PERMUTATION

Suppose W is internally finite. Internal finite-subset induction proves
that every nonempty finite subfield A has an admitted cyclic permutation
g: a permutation with no proper nonempty admitted forward-closed subset
of A. Extend g by identity outside A.

For a singleton, use identity. For the successor step take a cyclic
permutation of nonempty A, choose a point u in A, and insert a new z
into its edge u -> g(u):

  g'(u)=z,       g'(z)=g(u),       g'(v)=g(v) otherwise.

This is an admitted permutation of A union {z}. If a nonempty admitted
subset B of the enlarged field is g'-forward-closed, its old part B cap A
is nonempty: if z is its first available point, g'(z) belongs to A.
The old part is g-forward-closed, including at u since closure gives
u -> z -> g(u). Thus B cap A=A, and closure from u also includes z.
This proves cyclicity. Adjoining a point already present changes nothing.
The empty starting field is handled by the singleton step.

The induction predicate is an admitted formula asserting existence of
such a permutation. This is internal finite induction and therefore
also covers pseudofinite W.

Let M be the least admitted family of operations containing I and g and
closed under composition. Use endpoint-valued candidate families in its
definition. The usual two-stage centralizer induction proves that all
M-members commute: first show every member commutes with g; then, for
each such member, show every member commutes with it.

Every member permutes W, fixes its complement, and has an admitted inverse.
These properties contain I,g and are closed under composition. The inverse
of g is given by its unique inverse graph using Plenitude; a product's
inverse is the reverse product of its inverses. This does not presume
that the inverse already belongs to M.

For any a in W the admitted orbit

  O(v) := W(v) and exists f.[M(f) and f(a)=v]

is nonempty and g-forward-closed. Cyclicity gives O=W. Hence M is an
admitted commuting family of permutations acting transitively on W.

5. INTERNALLY INFINITE FIELDS: A BOOLEAN GROUP

Now suppose W is internally infinite. Its well-order and Section 3 give
an admitted injective pairing p:W squared -> W. Choose a!=b in W and put

  c=p(a,b),                  J(x,z)=p(p(x,z),a).

J is injective and misses c: equality p(p(x,z),a)=p(a,b) would imply a=b.

Let Fin(W) be the admitted field of internally finite endpoint-valued
subsets of W, normalized false off W. Equivalently these are the members
of the least admitted family containing the empty subset and closed under
adjoining one point. Equivalence with the Frege definition follows by
finite-cardinal induction in one direction and adjunction induction in
the other. Finite unions, subsets and symmetric differences stay finite.

Define R(A,z) as the least admitted relation of subset/code pairs containing
(empty,c) and closed under sorted append:

  R(A,z) and x in W and (forall u in A.u<x)
       -> R(A union {x}, J(x,z)).                              (4)

Every set appearing in R is an endpoint-valued subset of W and every code
belongs to W. All definitions are at fixed types: A:tt, z:t and relations
on these variables. There is no sort of external finite sequences.

Here are the two substantive coding obligations.

ARBITRARY INSERTION. Induct on R(A,z) to prove that for every x in W the
set A union {x} has some R-code. At the base append x. At a successor
A=A0 union {y}, with all A0 below y, compare x and y. If x<y, insert x
into A0 by induction and then append y. If x=y retain the existing code.
If y<x append x to the existing code. Thus the domain of R contains empty
and is closed under arbitrary adjunction. Internal finite induction gives
a code for every internally finite subset of W.

DECODING IS SINGLE-VALUED. The least-relation definition has an internal
inversion rule. If F(P) denotes the base pair plus the permitted successors
of P, closure gives F(R) subset R. Monotonicity gives F(F(R)) subset F(R),
so F(R) is also closed. Minimality yields R=F(R).

Induct on R(A,z) with the assertion that R(B,z) implies B=A. For z=c,
inversion cannot give a successor because c is outside J's range, so B is
empty. In the successor step z=J(x,u) and A=A0 union {x}, with R(A0,u).
Inversion of R(B,z) gives B=B0 union {y}, z=J(y,v), and R(B0,v).
Injectivity of J gives x=y and u=v. Induction gives A0=B0, hence A=B.

For each finite A choose its LEAST R-code in the given well-order of W.
This is an admitted unique-value graph and hence an operation by Plenitude.
Decoding uniqueness makes this an injection Fin(W) -> W. Singleton
formation supplies W -> Fin(W). Internal Schroeder-Bernstein therefore
gives an admitted bijection b:W -> Fin(W).

Symmetric difference makes Fin(W) an abelian group in which every element
is its own inverse. Its operation is an admitted lambda term, and its
identities follow from the Boolean identities on endpoint-valued predicates.
Transport along b gives an admitted group operation * on W. The inverse
of b is again a unique-value graph represented by Plenitude. No selection
principle beyond the initial well-order has been used.

The translations f_u(v)=u*v on W, extended by identity off W, are admitted
permutations. Their endpoint range predicate is an admitted family M.
They commute, act transitively, and are all involutions.

6. LIFTING PERMUTATIONS TO FULL LOGICAL OBSERVERS

The two branches produce an admitted endpoint family M of pairwise
commuting permutations of W, transitive on W. Each f has an admitted
inverse; each operation fixes nonatoms. Define its observer by the
uniform lambda term

  L_f(p) := exists w.[W(w) and w and At(f(w),p)].               (5)

Atomic pasting, or the unique-true-atom calculation, gives for every v in W

  At(v,L_f(p)) iff At(f(v),p).                                (6)

Consequently L_f preserves negation and conjunction. Using (3), it also
preserves every typed universal quantifier: at each atom v both
L_f(forall x.Fx) and forall x.L_f(Fx) have truth value
forall x.At(f(v),Fx). Atomic separation identifies the propositions, and
Functionality upgrades the universally quantified output identities to
the required whole-operation identities. The same reasoning applies to
the Boolean clauses. Existential preservation follows by duality.

Composition reverses the permutation order:

  L_f o L_h = L_(h o f),       L_I=I.

Thus commuting permutations give commuting observers. The lift of an
inverse permutation is a two-sided inverse and has all the same logical
qualifications. In particular every L_f is injective.

Define the admitted family predicate

  K(E) := exists f.[M(f) and E=L_f].                          (7)

It is endpoint-valued by C5 and BF; the uniform lambda term in (5) makes
the displayed definition internal. Let a be the currently true atom.
If p!=q, some atom v distinguishes their At-values. Transitivity supplies
f in M with f(a)=v. Formula (6) then makes L_f(p) and L_f(q) differ in
current truth. K separates propositions. Functionality supplies an
argument tuple distinguishing any two unequal relations, so the SAME K
separates all relational types. Section 6 proves (2).

The SAME K works necessarily. All membership, permutation and transitivity
facts about M are endpoint-valued, and BF makes their universal closures
necessary. At any world choose its true atom a'; transitivity from a'
repeats the separation proof just given. Whole qualification, commutation
and inverse identities persist by NI, with the same inverse witnesses;
BF collects the universally quantified clauses. This proves (1a) under
the weaker premise (2), without changing the witness family.

In the infinite branch the observers themselves are involutions. The
finite branch need not have this additional property.

7. BOXED FUNCTIONAL CHOICE AND NECESSITY

The recorded consequences of boxed Functional Choice are boxed Plenitude,
C5, Atomicity, Well-Ordering, Functionality and No Pure Contingency. In particular a well-order of the proposition type
restricts to an admitted well-order of W, supplying the premise of (2).

The necessary-Plenitude arrow is
necessary-functional-choice-r-implies-necessary-plenitude-r. The recorded
Well-Ordering chain runs through necessary Relational Choice, necessary
Actuality/Transversal, necessary Transversal Choice, and necessary
Well-Ordering. This is a use of the recorded strong Choice package, not
a claim to settle unboxed Relational Choice -> Transversal Choice.

Hence boxed Functional Choice proves stable full CoRevSep, with the same
witness family necessarily satisfying each instance, by Section 6. This
implies the necessary existential principles in (1). The existing finite
propositional/Q_t reduction also recovers all relational and quantifier
instances. No Pure Contingency provides an alternative route to the boxed
closed sentences, but is not needed for the stronger fixed-family result.
Neither route uses unrestricted necessitation on optional hypotheses.

The same argument gives necessary CoInjSep, necessary CoSep, and necessary
full Rev. The witness family is not asserted to be canonical. Its existence
and its necessary witness properties are what the theorem establishes.

8. SIGNIFICANCE, BOUNDARY AND REVIEW

Together with joint-injective-separation.txt the results give two routes:

  Actuality + CoInjSep -> C5 + Atomicity + CoRevSep;

  boxed Functional Choice -> stable CoRevSep.

The first extracts atomic reversible structure from coherent injectivity.
The second supplies that coherent structure from a familiar strong theory.
The joint observer principle records an organization of logical access to
worlds that separate individual observer principles do not express.

The finite/cofinite countermodel from this folder still shows that
C5 + necessary Atomicity + necessary Rev + necessary CoIdemSep does not
suffice. It explicitly fails even unboxed Functional Choice at (t,t):
an equivariant serial relation asks for an atom below each nonzero
proposition, but any finite-support selector fails on the complement of
its own finite support. See the companion countermodel note.

The main nontrivial work is internal finite-subset coding and the
finite/pseudofinite cyclic construction, combined with the previously
proved internal infinite-square lemma. The observer lift is short once
atomic pasting is used. The abstract set-theoretic existence of abelian
group structures is background mathematics; the claim here is the
admitted construction and its consequence for the fixed logical theory.
