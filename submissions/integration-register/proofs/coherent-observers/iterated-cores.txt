1. STATEMENT

Let M be a general pointed model of C. Let E,F:tt be admitted,
qualified at every admitted type, and satisfy the whole equations

  E^2=E,  F^2=F,  EF=FE.

Then F induces a qualified idempotent observer Fbar on Core(M,E),
and there is a canonical pointed typed logical isomorphism

  Core(Core(M,E),Fbar)  =_iso  Core(M,EF).                       (1)

No BF, Functionality, C5, Atomicity, or Choice premise is needed.
The all-type qualification is a metatheoretic specification of
these particular observers; each finite proof or translation uses
only finitely many whole laws. No quantifier over types is added.

The source construction is
../observer-quotient-general.txt,
Sections 2-8. At EVERY type it quotients normalized fixed objects
by agreement in current truth under all normalized fixed tests.
Skipping that quotient is not part of the present proof.

2. NORMALIZERS COMMUTE BEFORE ANY QUOTIENT

For observer H define r_e^H=id, r_t^H=H, and

  r_(sigma tau)^H(f)=r_tau^H composed f composed r_sigma^H.

Induction on types gives the WHOLE equations

  r_sigma^E r_sigma^F = r_sigma^F r_sigma^E = r_sigma^(EF).       (2)

At an arrow the left side becomes
r_tau^E r_tau^F f r_sigma^F r_sigma^E. Apply the inductive whole
identities on both sides of f. This is equational rewriting under
lambda, as in the source's stable-premise abstraction lemma, not
an application of current pointwise Functionality.

The common fixed domain is therefore

  T_sigma = Fix(r_sigma^E) intersect Fix(r_sigma^F)
          = Fix(r_sigma^(EF)).                                  (3)

The product EF is itself fully qualified and idempotent by direct
composition of the whole laws.

3. THE SECOND OBSERVER IS ADMITTED IN THE FIRST CORE

Write S_sigma=Fix(r_sigma^E), and q:S -> K=Core(M,E) for the
first logical quotient. The E-normalization of F at tt is

  E F E = EF.

Thus Fbar:=q_tt(EF) is an admitted operation in K. On a fixed
proposition p in S_t it has value q_t(Fp), since Ep=p and EF=FE.
The operation EF is itself E-fixed, so application congruence
of q makes this action well-defined on all classes.

Whole Boolean and quantifier preservation for Fbar follow from
those for F and the E-normalized constants. For example, an
E-fixed predicate H satisfies H r_sigma^E=H and E H=H.
Then F H is again E-fixed and

  F(Q_sigma H)=Q_sigma(F H).

Both sides lie in S_t, and q carries this equation to the core
quantifier equation. Normalizing the whole composite operations
gives the same equations at their function types; this retains
whole qualification and does not infer operation equality merely
from equality at current inputs. The Boolean checks are identical
calculations, and Fbar^2=Fbar follows from F^2=F.

The recursively induced Fbar-normalizer in K obeys

  rbar_sigma(q_sigma(x))=q_sigma(r_sigma^F(x))  (x in S_sigma).  (4)

This is proved by induction on types using (2), quotient application
and lambda compatibility. For e, both sides are q_e(x). For t
it is the displayed action of Fbar. At an arrow it is the same
input/output conjugation formula for the normalizers.

4. THE TWO LOGICAL QUOTIENTS HAVE THE SAME FINAL KERNEL

Let S' be the fixed prestructure of rbar inside K. Map

  h_sigma:T_sigma -> S'_sigma,  h_sigma(x)=q_sigma(x).

It is surjective: if q(z) is rbar-fixed, (4) gives
q(r^F z)=q(z); choose x=r^F z. Equations (2)-(3) put x in T.
No simultaneous selection of class representatives is required.

The map h preserves application, normalized constants and lambda
interpretations by (2) and the quotient congruence. It also
preserves quantifiers. For a T-fixed predicate H the original
Q_sigma H is fixed under BOTH observers; the first quotient
quantifier sends q(H) to q(Q_sigma H), and restricting to S'
does not alter that value because H is invariant under r^F.
Surjectivity at each sort gives precisely the intended ranges.
Both prestructures use original current truth, so h preserves
and reflects truth.

Now compose h with the canonical logical quotient S' -> Core(K,Fbar).
It is a sortwise-surjective, truth-preserving and truth-reflecting
logical homomorphism onto an identity-separated C model.
By the source construction's universal characterization, its
kernel is exactly agreement under all T-fixed tests. But T is the
EF-normalized prestructure by (3), so its canonical quotient is
Core(M,EF). This supplies the unique canonical isomorphism (1).

Explicitly, the isomorphism sends the final class represented by
q(x), for x in T, to the EF-core class represented by x. The
kernel argument proves independence of every choice of representative,
including at the individual and proposition types.

5. CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS

The same theorem iterates for any finite pairwise commuting family
of qualified whole-idempotent observers. Successive cores are
canonically the core of their product and do not depend on order
or parenthesization. The comparison maps agree because on common
fixed representatives they are the same map. This is a coherence
statement for the isomorphisms, not just equality of theory.

Constants in an expanded signature are normalized in each stage;
equation (2) makes their iterated and product interpretations agree.
No comparison is asserted for independently reassigned constants.

The source's current Functional Choice inheritance consequently
holds through any such finite sequence, with the original allowed
relational output types. No new RC/TC inheritance theorem is
asserted. This note does not identify cores of arbitrary observers
with the same Boolean image, or omit the all-sort quotient.
