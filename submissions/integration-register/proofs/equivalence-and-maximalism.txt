EQUIVALENCE CLOSURE AND MAXIMAL OBSERVER SEPARATION
26 October 2026

Scope: the fixed pure finitary relational language of the Classicism map.
OS means the full typed observer-separation schema, with whole operation
identities and separately finite qualification lists. Additional axioms
below mean their universally closed instances. All additions of axioms
use ordinary H-consequence unless an extra closure rule is specified.

1. Equivalence closure of an extension

Bare C already is the smallest H-theory closed under the dynamic Rule of
Equivalence. Bacon and Dorr explicitly consider and settle this question
in Classicism section 1.4 and Appendix A. The online author-hosted draft
inspected here is dated 1 July 2022; its section 1.4 begins at printed p.14.
The database normally uses the later 16 May 2023 draft's pagination.

For an extension, however, there is a real and exactly describable choice:

  EqCl(C + Gamma) = Cn_H(C + Box Gamma).                       (1)

Over C, theorem Equivalence and theorem Necessitation impose the same
closure condition. Equivalence turns a theorem P iff Top into P=Top.
Conversely, necessitate the universally closed theorem P iff Q and use
C's Intensionality to identify its lambda abstractions.

To prove (1), Equivalence closure must contain Box Gamma. In the opposite
direction, any deduction from C plus boxed sentences uses finitely many
such sentences. Discharge them, necessitate the resulting C theorem,
and use K and 4 to recover its boxed conclusion. Thus C+Box Gamma is
already Necessitation-closed and hence Equivalence-closed. This argument
does not necessitate temporary assumptions in an ordinary deduction.

Rigid Comprehension separates the two policies strictly. The existing
full Boolean-valued model B=2 x A, with A complete atomless and designated
atom a=(1,0), satisfies C5 and current Actuality, hence Rigid Comprehension.
Actuality has Boolean value a rather than Top, so necessary Actuality
fails. Since necessary Rigid Comprehension entails necessary Actuality,
necessary Rigid Comprehension fails too. Both extension policies are
consistent, but they are different theories. See dynamic-audit.txt section 4.

The methodological question is therefore whether the additional principles
are proposed as necessary laws or as current truths. Bare C's fixed-H
presentation alone does not create a weaker alternative to dynamic closure.

2. Exact maximalization conventions

For an H-theory T extending C, Max(T) adds A!=B for every closed pure
identity A=B not provable in T. Equivalently it adds Diamond P for every
closed pure P consistent with T. Its base theory is included.

Put

  L = Cn_H(C + OS),
  N = EqCl(L) = Cn_H(C + Box OS).

The answers are:

  Max(L)                         consistent;
  Max(N)                         consistent;
  Cn_H(Max(C) + Box OS)           inconsistent;
  EqCl(Max(L))                   inconsistent;
  EqCl(Max(N))                   inconsistent.

The positive claims are model constructions in ordinary ZFC. The negative
claims are finite ordinary arguments over C. Changing the reference theory
before maximalizing and closing the resulting maximalization under a rule
are different operations.

3. OS adds no new closed pure identities

The existing finitely pinned interpretation-root construction preserves an
arbitrary set-sized family of C action models, supplies full typed OS at a
new root, and makes each target reachable. Take a witness for every pure
C-consistent sentence. The resulting root satisfies Max(C)+OS.

Consequently, if C+OS proved a closed pure identity not provable in C,
this root would satisfy both that identity and its Max(C) disequality.
Therefore C+OS is conservative over C for closed pure identities, and

                Max(C+OS) = Cn_H(Max(C)+OS).                  (2)

This equational conservativity does not make OS redundant. OS supplies
new current truths, including BF. It does not identify those truths with
Top. Dynamic closure does make those identifications and is strictly
stronger. The full root and whole typed observer checks are spelled out
in maximal-os-audit.txt sections 3-4, with direct construction sources.

4. Maximalizing necessary OS is also consistent

Choose an action-model witness for every pure sentence consistent with N.
Since N is Necessitation-closed, every reachable object in each witness
satisfies N, and in particular OS. Apply the same interpretation-root
construction to this family. Old components remain unchanged; OS holds
there and at the new root. Thus OS holds globally, the root satisfies N,
and every N-consistent pure sentence remains possible there. This proves
Max(N) consistent. The argument uses the special OS-preserving root
construction, not a blanket principle about maximalizing all extensions.

By contrast, fixed Max(C) demands the possibility of a failure of BF,
since such failures are C-consistent. Necessary OS implies necessary BF.
Hence Max(C)+Box OS is inconsistent. Relative Max(N) avoids that conflict
because the BF failures are no longer consistent with its reference theory.

5. Closing the finished maximalization under Equivalence is inconsistent

This is a separate, short obstruction. Let F be the closed pure Fregean
Axiom. C proves F -> Box F: under F every true proposition equals Top,
including the proposition F itself. Thus C also proves

                    Box(F -> not Diamond not F).

Both F and not F are consistent with L and with N. A full one-world
extensional model witnesses F; a full two-world S5 model witnesses not F
and necessary OS. Therefore Max(T), for T=L or N, contains both

                     Diamond F, Diamond not F.

Adding theorem Necessitation gives Box Diamond not F. But the F-world
required by Diamond F must satisfy both Diamond not F and its negation.
Contradiction. Equivalence closure entails Necessitation closure, so it
has the same obstruction.

More generally, this proof works for every extension T of C admitting
both F and not F. It does NOT refute Max(T) without that added rule.
Indeed Max(T) proves that its own axiom Diamond not F is not necessary:
Diamond F and Box(F -> not Diamond not F) imply not Box Diamond not F.
