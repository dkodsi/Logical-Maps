# Bounded-stage localization for countable symmetric repairs

The limit uses countably many forcing coordinates but only bounded-stage symmetry supports. These are deliberately different requirements. The construction is an ordinary symmetric system formed from inverse limits; it is not claimed to be an instance of a published iteration theorem using one support ideal throughout.

## 1. Finite systems and the exact hypotheses

Work over a transitive ground W satisfying ZF + DC, with real line R and κ = ω₁ of W. The intended W is the symmetric Solovay model. At each finite stage the real line remains R and κ remains ω₁. Let

```math
S_n=(P_n,G_n,F_n)
```

be the finite symmetric systems, with their coherent initial-segment projections. The next iterand, computed in the nth symmetric model, is a countable-support product with coordinates

```math
(A,i),\qquad A\subseteq R\text{ uncountable},\quad i<\kappa.
```

The factor at (A,i) is J(A), the countable partial bijections R→A. Optional free countable-partial coloring factors can be included with the same copy arrangement. The automorphism group includes independent permutations of the κ copies for each A. The filter is generated by pointwise stabilizers of countable sets of coordinates: a supported coordinate is fixed completely. One can include the independent domain/range permutations of the companion local-diagram construction, but the arguments below do not require them.

At successor steps use hereditarily symmetric names for conditions and automorphisms, padded outside a witnessing condition by the top condition or identity as appropriate. Finite-prefix actions on later names are the usual iterated actions. In particular, an automorphism at stage n can be an HS name depending on the earlier n stages. It need not be a ground permutation.

The finite filters are countably complete. A countable family of finite-stage HS names has an HS sequence name: choose countably many support witnesses using DC and intersect them. Coordinatewise union, with top-value padding off the cone where the input sequence is descending, supplies the canonical lower-bound operation for countable descending sequences of conditions. Thus the finite posets are countably closed. The standard direct argument with countably many decisions gives no new reals. The same closure and countably complete support argument retains DC at each finite symmetric stage. Only these finite-stage facts are used below; no DC assertion about the final bounded-support model is required.

For comparison, Karagila–Schilhan, [*Towards a theory of symmetric extensions*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17338), Definition 4.4, printed pp. 11–12, uses one ideal for conditions, automorphisms, and subgroup generators, and adds a common support requirement for component names. Their construction therefore does not directly verify the mixed limit here. Their finite two-step machinery and ordinary symmetric-system semantics remain applicable.

## 2. The full inverse limits and the bounded filter

Define the forcing as the full inverse limit of the finite posets:

```math
P_\omega=\{p: p\restriction n\in P_n\text{ for every }n<\omega\}.
 \tag{1}
```

Its order is the coherent coordinatewise iteration order. Each component is an HS name over its finite prefix. There is **no** demand that one bounded-stage support fix all component names of p.

Define Gω as the inverse limit of the finite automorphism groups. Its elements are coherent tuples of actual finite-prefix automorphisms; equivalently, they are the corresponding sequences of HS finite-prefix automorphism names. Set

```math
(\pi p)\restriction n=(\pi\restriction n)(p\restriction n).
 \tag{2}
```

This gives an actual automorphism of (1). Composition and inversion are determined by their finite restrictions, so Gω is a group. Let ρₙ:Gω→Gₙ be restriction. These are surjective homomorphisms: append identity automorphism names to any finite automorphism.

Now let Fω be the filter generated by

```math
\rho_n^{-1}(H),\qquad n<\omega,\ H\in F_n.
 \tag{3}
```

This is the precise meaning of bounded-stage support. Finite intersections can be taken at the larger finite stage. Conjugation is immediate:

```math
\pi\rho_n^{-1}(H)\pi^{-1}
   =\rho_n^{-1}\bigl((\rho_n\pi)H(\rho_n\pi)^{-1}\bigr).
 \tag{4}
```

Consequently Fω is a normal filter. Arbitrarily dependent tail automorphisms cause no extra support obligations in (4). That is the benefit of defining the generators by restriction preimages instead of trying to reuse the same-ideal generator syntax.

Each Pₙ is a complete initial subforcing of Pω. If a finite condition extends p↾n, splice that stronger prefix onto p's old tail. Every subsequent component remains a valid name and the resulting condition extends p. The usual compatibility and maximal-antichain tests follow.

The ordinary hereditary symmetric-name construction for (Pω,Gω,Fω) therefore gives a ZF model Mω. Every finite symmetric model Mₙ embeds in it: inflate its names, and replace any finite support H by (3).

## 3. Countable closure does not need bounded condition supports

Suppose p₀≥p₁≥⋯ is a descending sequence in Pω belonging to W. Construct a lower bound prefix by prefix. At coordinate n, the component names pₖ(n) form an HS sequence over Pₙ, because Fₙ is countably complete. Below the lower bound already constructed for the prefix, their values form a descending sequence of countable partial graphs. Take its union; outside that cone use the top condition if the union is not a valid condition.

The result is again an HS Pₙ-name. Repeating this for all n gives a member of (1) extending every pₖ. Its component supports may be unbounded in n, which is allowed. This proves countable closure of Pω.

Using DC in W, successively decide the digits of any proposed real name and take this lower bound. Hence the full forcing extension adds no reals. In particular Mω has exactly R as its real line. No countable completeness claim for Fω is made: it normally fails and is not needed for this argument.

## 4. Relative homogeneity of the full tail

Fix m and p,q∈Pω whose m-prefixes are compatible. We construct π∈kerρₘ such that πp and q are compatible.

Take π↾m to be the identity. Suppose π↾n has been defined. In the nth symmetric model, consider the two condition names

```math
(\pi\restriction n)(p(n)),\qquad q(n).
```

The union C of their copy indices is a countable subset of κ. Choose canonically a countable ordinal β>sup C. Swap the two ordinal blocks [0,β) and [β,β+β), fixing the rest of κ. Use this same copy permutation for every target A. It is an automorphism of the whole nth product, and it moves every copy used by the first condition away from every copy used by the second.

This construction is definable from the two HS condition names, so it gives an HS name π(n). There is no need to choose one ground bound for all n, nor to give all π(n) a common bounded support. They produce a coherent member of Gω by §2.

Start with a common extension of the two m-prefixes. At every later coordinate take the union of the two transformed product conditions. Their coordinate supports are disjoint, so this union is a valid countable-support condition. These unions, with the stronger prefixes, form a condition r∈Pω extending πp and q. This proves

```math
p\restriction m\parallel q\restriction m
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists\pi\in\ker\rho_m\ (\pi p\parallel q).
 \tag{5}
```

The copy set must have uncountably many members in the finite symmetric model. Using only ω copies would not support the block argument, since a countable condition could use all of them.

## 5. Localization in the finite symmetric model

Let U∈W be any fixed ground set. Let x be an HS Pω-name forced to be a subset of check U, and choose n,H∈Fₙ such that

```math
\rho_n^{-1}(H)\leq\mathrm{sym}(x).
```

Define a Pₙ-name

```math
x_n=\{(p\restriction n,\check u):
           u\in U,\ p\Vdash_{P_\omega}\check u\in x\}.
 \tag{6}
```

Then xₙ and x have the same interpretation. One direction follows immediately by projecting a condition in the generic filter. For the other, suppose p witnesses a pair in (6) and q in the full generic forces u∉x. Their n-prefixes are compatible. By (5), some π∈kerρₙ makes πp compatible with q. But π fixes x, so πp still forces u∈x, a contradiction.

Crucially, xₙ is **hereditarily symmetric**, not merely a full Pₙ-name. If h∈H, extend h by identity names to an element of Gω. It fixes x and carries the witnesses in (6) to witnesses, so h fixes xₙ. The element names check u are ground names and hence HS. Thus xₙ∈HSₙ.

If a name is only forced to be a subset of U below a condition in the chosen generic, first replace it by its intersection with check U; this keeps the desired value and a symmetry support. No global maximum principle is used.

Therefore every subset of any fixed ground set U in Mω belongs to some finite Mₙ. In particular, this applies to subsets of R and to graphs contained in θ×R for any fixed ordinal θ. The claim is about the individual subsets, not about the whole set P(R), which need not occur at a finite stage.

## 6. Finite-stage reduction to countable dependency diagrams

There is a separate finite induction. For every finite n, every x∈Mₙ with x⊆U∈W is obtained in an extension by a finite-height system having countably many selected repair coordinates at each level. The targets of its selected coordinates are names using only the preceding selected levels. A finite tuple of such subsets can be treated together by tagging its entries.

Here are the details needed to avoid a hidden uniform-support assumption.

At the last stage work inside Mₙ₋₁. Choose one countable coordinate support C for a last-stage name for x. Choose one enumeration of C in Mₙ₋₁; equivalently, below a condition in its generic, choose one HS name for such an enumeration. Its entry names are obtained canonically. This does not choose an independent support or name for each entry.

The last-stage product is homogeneous outside C relative to its restriction to C. Indeed, independently for each A, fix the protected copy indices C_A and move the first condition's remaining copy indices into fresh indices beyond a countable ordinal bound for both conditions and C_A. Well-ordering of the copy indices makes this operation canonical, so no choice over the family of all A is required. Conditions with compatible C-restrictions can consequently be made compatible by an automorphism fixing C. Project the membership name for x to the C-subproduct as in (6).

After enumeration of C, a condition in that countable subproduct is a countable sequence of countable graphs of real pairs. It has a real code. We do not select a canonical code: use the set of **all** codes. Since no stage has added reals, every such code is in the fixed ground R. The projected name is consequently represented by a set

```math
T\subseteq R\times U
 \tag{7}
```

in Mₙ₋₁. The selected target family is represented simultaneously by

```math
A^*\subseteq\omega\times R.
 \tag{8}
```

The sequence of copy indices is retained as well. It is a countable sequence below κ, hence bounded below κ and real-codable; it is old because no reals were added. Finite supports are handled by a finite enumeration instead of padding with duplicate coordinates.

Apply the induction hypothesis to one tagged subset of a fixed ground set coding (7), (8), and the necessary index data. It gives one earlier countable dependency system for both the target definitions and the entire projected membership name. This is why merely choosing supports independently for countably many target names is unnecessary.

The genericity check is also essential. In the actual earlier local extension, the enumerated target sets and copy indices are the selected coordinates of C. Pass to a local condition forcing these coordinate labels to be distinct and the targets to be uncountable; both assertions hold in the actual local extension. Uncountability holds there because countability would persist into the larger finite symmetric model. Outside that cone the target names may be padded by R. The selected product has exactly the same conditions in the local and the larger model: its countable graphs are coded by the same reals, and membership in the selected target sets is the same. Restricting a full product generic to this subproduct is therefore a complete projection. Merging a stronger condition on C with the old condition outside C verifies completeness directly.

By induction, the preceding local generic followed by this selected product generic is generic for the advertised finite-height dependent forcing over W. Evaluating the name coded by T gives x. This supplies the actual local system needed by the companion diagram argument; it is not just a statement that x has a countable stabilizer.

Combining §§5–6, every ordinal-indexed real sequence in Mω is represented by one countable dependency system of finite height. This localization does not itself establish the balance or old-value properties of that local forcing; those are the separate arguments in [symmetric repair](necessary-os-ch-symmetric-repair.md), §§2–4.

## 7. Consequence and remaining scope

Every uncountable A⊆R in Mω appears in some Mₙ by §5. It was already uncountable there: an old countable enumeration would remain in Mω. At the next stage one of its repair coordinates supplies a bijection R→A. That individual generic bijection has a finite coordinate support and hence belongs to Mω. Thus the construction satisfies the weak continuum dichotomy

```math
\forall A\subseteq R\quad
     [A\text{ is countable}\ \lor\ A\approx R].
 \tag{9}
```

This conclusion is distinct from non-well-orderability of R. To retain the latter, apply the separately established old-value theorem for the finite-height countable local systems to the ordinal-indexed sequences localized above. The inverse-limit construction alone does not replace that theorem.

Nor does (9) construct a logical observer. A modal countermodel still requires a comparison component, admitted complete Boolean maps between the components, compatible group actions and support filters, and the full typed operation-preservation checks. Those requirements are outside this note.

## 8. The real-set conclusions and the independent-color comparison

Suppose the initial stage retains one generic countable-partial coloring c:R→2. Each color class meets every old nonempty perfect set: extend a condition by assigning the requested color at a fresh point of that perfect set. Each color class also escapes every old countable set, by the same argument. These are dense requirements in W.

No subsequent stage adds reals. Every countable set of reals in the final model has a real-coded enumeration and was therefore old. Every nonempty perfect set has an old real code as well. The two color classes remain uncountable, and neither contains a nonempty perfect subset. Thus this initial coloring remains Bernstein. Together with (9), the target has WCH and fails the perfect set property. This inference does not require DC in the final model.

The independent-color comparison in [symmetric repair](necessary-os-ch-symmetric-repair.md), §6, has a different role and passes the following audit. For a finite-height local system P_h, add a new coloring independent of every repair parameter. Given a proposed injection from its 0-cell to its 1-cell, capture an enumeration of the initial coloring domain D when choosing the small universe N. Then D is countable **in N**. Complete the repair diagram over N independently of that coloring, and complete the color diagram by assigning 0 on all of R^N outside D. The balanced old-value argument forces the proposed injection to take those old inputs to old 1-colored outputs, all of which lie in D. The reconstructed forcing relation and Replacement in N yield an injection R^N\D→D, impossible in N. Reversing the colors proves the opposite non-injection.

For an infinite symmetric comparison model, this argument requires that each alleged injection actually be evaluated in a local product P_h×Col with the same extra coloring, and that the repair target names in P_h ignore that coloring. A support statement alone does not suffice. The comparison note retains precisely these hypotheses; its conditional opposite-WCH conclusion is valid at that scope.

## 9. Homogeneity and observer separation

A later attempted modal construction synchronized repair-copy permutations along the countable raw-target orbits of within-block compression maps. Such maps may be supported, but ordinary prefix block permutations need not be supported as Boolean operations at this bounded-support ω-limit.

Here is an explicit obstruction to that inference. Let γ swap two base color blocks β and δ. Take an old uncountable proper A₀⊂R and a raw target row a whose value is A₀ or R according to one color bit in β. Its within-block compression orbit stays in β, while γa's orbit stays in δ. At any arbitrarily late repair stage, let τ swap two copies on a's orbit and fix the other target orbits. Then γτγ⁻¹ swaps copies on γa's orbit, so γ and τ do not commute; one generic graph value witnesses the difference.

Every bounded-stage support subgroup contains such sufficiently late τ. Consequently the Boolean automorphism induced by γ has no bounded symmetry support as an observer object. Forcing weak homogeneity under the full group therefore does **not** supply the required admitted observers in this design. This invalidates that proposed OS argument, not the ZF limit, localization, or real-set conclusions above. A construction with a different support arrangement needs a fresh observer-admission proof.

The required supported observer cover is proved in [small observer covers](necessary-os-ch-small-observer-cover.md) and used in the [finite-support limit](necessary-os-ch-inaccessible-countermodel.md).
