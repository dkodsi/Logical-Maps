# Exact Intensional Choice and Dedekind classification for nominal world models

22 October 2026. Original proofs developed independently in parallel by the parent audit agent and Gallin-audit agent, then combined here. Ordinary mathematics in ambient ZFC; no human verification, Lean status, database promotion, or literature-novelty claim. This refines the explicit model family in `nominal-world-orbit-choice-20261022.md`. The only original types used below are the bundle's admitted relational types.

## 1. Construction and theorem

Let A be countably infinite and G=Sym(A). Let W be a nonempty G-set all of whose elements have a finite support. Take E=N with trivial action, D_t all finitely supported subsets of W, and at every admitted original arrow take all finitely supported functions under conjugation. Propositions are evaluated by world membership. These are the previously audited C5+Atomicity models; each has the full standard ordinary E/o mathematical core. If W is infinite, D_t and every other original domain are infinite, the Frege number fields are standard, and both quantified original Infinity axioms hold with their boxes.

A world is **strong** here when every permutation fixing the world fixes every member of its least finite support. This definition concerns the chosen G-action used to construct the model; it is not an added object-language axiom. Say W is orbit-finite when it has finitely many G-orbits, and trivial when every group element fixes every world.

**Classification theorem.** For this model family in ZFC:

1. All-type Intensional Choice holds iff its single proposition-type instance holds iff either W has trivial action, or W is orbit-finite and every world is strong.
2. The original sentence DedInf_t, asserting an admitted injective nonsurjective operation t→t, holds iff W has infinitely many G-orbits.
3. Intensional Choice with individual witnesses holds for every W, whether or not the all-type schema holds. More generally the same is true for witnesses restricted to any invariant subdomain on which G acts trivially, including the full ordinary core domains.
4. Functional Choice and Relational Choice each hold, at all their admitted types, iff W has trivial action. If the action is nontrivial, each already fails with input and output type t.

Every assertion and failure in (1) and (2) holds at every designated world. The choices required by IC cover all worlds, so these are genuinely boxed positive or negative verdicts. The theorem is a classification inside this explicit semantic family, not a claim about all C5 models.

## 2. Least supports and the quotient description of one orbit

We include the support facts rather than assume them. If finite S and T support an entity x, then S∩T supports it. To see this, write C=S∩T. The group Fix(C) is generated by Fix(S) and Fix(T). First consider a transposition (a b) outside C. Unless a and b are in opposite difference sets S\T and T\S, the transposition already fixes S or fixes T. In the exceptional case choose c outside S∪T∪{a,b} and use

    (a b)=(a c)(b c)(a c).

Each factor fixes S or T. Thus every finitely supported permutation fixing C belongs to the generated subgroup. For an arbitrary g fixing C, choose a finitely supported permutation h fixing C and agreeing with g on S: the finite injection from S∪C to gS∪C extends to a permutation of their finite union and is the identity elsewhere. Then h^(-1)g fixes S. This proves the group claim and the support-intersection lemma.

Start from any finite support S0 of x. Among its finitely many subsets, repeated intersections with other finite supports produce the intersection of all finite supports; the intersection is achieved after finitely many steps. It is a support and is contained in every other finite support. Write supp(x) for this least support. Its uniqueness gives

    supp(gx)=g supp(x).

Fix a world w with support K={a1,...,ak}. Its stabilizer contains Fix(K), and every stabilizer element permutes K setwise. Let H≤Sym(k) consist of the coordinate permutations induced by stabilizer elements. Any element inducing such a permutation on K can be replaced by the permutation with that same action on K and identity outside K: their quotient fixes K pointwise and hence fixes w.

The orbit of w is therefore isomorphic to A^{#k}/H, where A^{#k} consists of ordered k-tuples of distinct atoms and H identifies the permitted coordinate permutations. A tuple b is sent to gw for any g with g(ai)=bi; this is independent of the extension because K supports w. Two tuples have the same image exactly when they differ by H. Thus w is strong iff H is trivial. In particular an orbit-finite strong W is a finite disjoint union of ordered-tuple orbits A^{#k}, possibly with duplicate orbit labels and k=0 components.

For finite S, Fix(S) has finitely many orbits on every A^{#k}: an orbit records which coordinates are which elements of S, with all remaining coordinates fresh. The same holds for its quotient by H and for any finite union. Therefore an orbit-finite W has finitely many Fix(S)-orbits for each finite S.

## 3. Sufficiency: trivial actions or finitely many strong orbits

If W has trivial action, all original type domains have trivial action by induction, and every arrow domain is the full external function domain. For an everywhere inhabited F, external Choice selects a witness at each w; its graph is admitted. Thus all-type IC holds. This case includes infinite W and hence is not subsumed by orbit-finiteness.

Now suppose W is orbit-finite and strong. Let F:σ→t be supported by S and instantiated at every world. Write W as a finite union of ordered-tuple orbits, with maximal tuple length k. There are finitely many Fix(S)-orbits of worlds. Choose a representative w_i in each and an F-witness x_i at w_i. Let T_i be a finite support of x_i, C_i the coordinate set of w_i, and E_i=T_i\(S∪C_i). Let r=max_i|E_i|.

Choose a finite ordered reservoir R disjoint from S of size r+k and an ordering of each E_i. If w lies in orbit i, prescribe a finite partial permutation that fixes S, sends the ordered coordinates of w_i to those of w, and sends the ordered E_i to the first |E_i| members of R outside the coordinates of w. The prescriptions are compatible and injective; the reservoir has enough available members. Complete this partial map to a permutation g and set h(w)=gx_i. Its value is independent of the completion, since the partial map specifies g on the support T_i.

Equivariance of F makes h(w) an F-witness. If q fixes S∪R, it preserves the selected reservoir members outside the coordinates and the relevant Fix(S)-orbit. The construction gives h(qw)=qh(w). Thus h has finite support S∪R. This argument also covers k=0 worlds and any witness type σ.

Define the original predicate G0 by

    G0(x)={w∈W:h(w)=x}.

Each value is supported by S∪R∪supp(x), and G0 as a function is supported by S∪R. It is admitted, satisfies G0≤F, and has exactly one instance at every world. We have not introduced any original function whose output is e: h is an external aid and G0 has the admitted type σt.

## 4. A nontrivial local symmetry already refutes IC at t

Suppose some world orbit O is A^{#k}/H with nontrivial H. Choose a nonsingleton orbit I of the action of H on the coordinate positions {1,...,k}. For a∈A define the support-star proposition

    p_a={w∈O:a∈supp(w)}.

Each p_a is supported by {a}; g p_a=p_(ga). The stars are pairwise distinct: for a≠b choose a k-element support containing a and excluding b. Such a world belongs to p_a but not p_b.

For w=[a1,...,ak] put U(w)={a_i:i∈I}. This is independent of the representative tuple, because I is H-invariant. Define the original property F:t→t by its worldwise fibers:

    at w∈O:  F(p) holds exactly when p=p_a for some a∈U(w);
    at w∉O:  F(p) holds exactly when p=bottom.

This definition is G-equivariant, so F is admitted with empty support. More explicitly each value F(p) is supported by supp(p), and the function itself by the empty set. Every world has an F-instance.

Suppose an IC selector G0 has finite support S. Choose w∈O with supp(w) disjoint from S. Its selected instance is p_(a_i) for some i∈I. Since I is nonsingleton and H acts transitively on I, some h∈H moves i. Realize h by a permutation g supported entirely on supp(w). It fixes S and w, but moves p_(a_i) to a distinct eligible star. Equivariance of G0 then makes both stars selected at w, contrary to uniqueness. Thus IC_t fails.

This sharpens the finite-order coding argument: no witness type above t is needed. The set U(w) is an externally specified equivariant family, not a claimed pure definable choice of a coordinate.

## 5. Infinitely many orbits plus any moving world refute IC at t

Suppose W has infinitely many G-orbits and at least one moving orbit O*. Its worlds have a common positive support size k*. Use this orbit to define the same stars p_a. For every finite B⊆A put

    p_B=union_{a∈B} p_a.

The coding B↦p_B is injective. If a∈B\C, choose a k*-element support containing a and avoiding C; the corresponding world lies in p_B but not p_C. Also supp(p_B)=B. The inclusion supp(p_B)⊆B is immediate. If a finite support T omitted a∈B, choose b outside T∪B. The transposition (a b) fixes T but changes p_B by injectivity, a contradiction. Hence B is contained in every support.

Choose distinct world orbits O_n for n=1,2,...; this uses ambient ZFC. Define a property F with the fibers

    at w∈O_n: F(p) holds iff p=p_B, |B|=n, and B∩supp(w)=empty;
    elsewhere: F(p) holds iff p=bottom.

This family is equivariant: orbit labels, finite cardinality and support-disjointness are preserved by G. Thus F is an admitted empty-supported property. Every fiber is nonempty, since each world has finite support and A is infinite.

Suppose G0 selects uniquely and has finite support S. Choose n>|S| and any w∈O_n. Its selected value is p_B with |B|=n and B disjoint from supp(w). Every permutation fixing S∪supp(w) fixes G0 and w, hence fixes the unique selected proposition. Thus S∪supp(w) supports p_B, and least-support minimality gives

    B⊆S∪supp(w).

Disjointness implies B⊆S, contradicting |B|>|S|. Therefore IC_t fails. Combined with §§3–4, this proves the entire first classification: if the action is nontrivial, IC forces finitely many orbits and trivial local stabilizer symmetries, and these conditions suffice.

The proof does not require unbounded world-support sizes. Even W=A×N with its natural action on the first coordinate fails: its infinitely many orbit labels can demand witness supports of arbitrarily large finite size. Conversely infinite trivial W satisfies IC. Mere external infinitude or countability of the set of worlds does not determine the principle.

## 6. Dedekind infinity is exactly infinitude of the orbit set

If W has infinitely many orbits, choose distinct ones O_0,O_1,... and let q_n=O_n be their characteristic propositions. These propositions are pairwise distinct and each is fixed by all G. The whole-domain map

    f(q_n)=q_(n+1),
    f(p)=p for every other proposition p

is equivariant, hence admitted at tt. It is injective and omits q_0. This verifies the original pure existential sentence DedInf_t, not merely an external injection into a subfamily.

Conversely suppose W is orbit-finite. For each finite S, its finitely many Fix(S)-orbits imply that only finitely many propositions are fixed by Fix(S): they are exactly the unions of those orbits. If an admitted injective operation f:t→t omitted r, let S support f and T support r. Every iterate f^n(r) is fixed by Fix(S∪T). They are pairwise distinct, since an equality of two iterates would, by injectivity, put r in the range of f. This contradicts the finite number of fixed propositions. Thus no such operation exists.

When W is infinite these orbit-finite models still satisfy the quantified Frege Infinity axioms. The theorem therefore preserves the distinction between Frege Infinity and operational Dedekind Infinity.

## 7. Individual witnesses and the complete four-way table

For any W and any F:e→t supported by S, its individual-witness fiber is constant on each Fix(S)-orbit of worlds: the action on E is trivial. In the specified construction E=N, take the least natural in each nonempty fiber. The resulting map h:W→E is Fix(S)-invariant, hence finitely supported, and its graph gives IC_e. Equivalently set G0(n)=F(n) minus the union of F(m) for m<n. This argument already works in ZF, without infinitely many external choices. In ZFC the orbitwise-choice argument also applies to every other trivial-action witness subdomain, including all interpreted ordinary E/o core domains, which are externally full.

The classification yields the following complete table within this family:

| World action | IC, all types | DedInf_t |
|---|---|---|
| Finitely many orbits, every world strong | holds | fails |
| Finitely many orbits, some nontrivial local support symmetry | fails at t | fails |
| Infinitely many orbits, entirely trivial action | holds | holds |
| Infinitely many orbits, some moving world | fails at t | holds |

For infinite W, examples are respectively A; unordered pairs [A]^2; N with trivial action; and A×N. All four have C5+Atomicity, both boxed quantified Frege Infinity axioms, standard natural-number fields, and the full standard ordinary mathematical core. The last example is simpler than the earlier disjoint union of all finite ordered tuples: support sizes remain uniformly one.

### Functional and Relational Choice distinguish every nontrivial action

If the action on W is trivial, every original arrow domain is externally full. At any designated world, ambient Choice selects from each nonempty fiber of an actually serial relation, and the selected operation and its relational graph are admitted. Thus both original Functional and Relational Choice hold at every point.

For the converse assume W has a moving orbit, of support size k≥1. Consider the pure original rigid relation U(p,q) which, when p is the join of exactly two distinct Boolean atoms, requires q to be an atom below p; on all other p it requires q=bottom. This is an admitted equivariant relation at ttt, and it is serial at every point. Boolean atoms in this hierarchy are exactly the singleton worlds: singletons are supported, and any nonempty proposition contains one.

Let f:t→t be a purported functional selector with finite support S. Choose worlds u,v in that moving orbit whose k-element supports are disjoint from each other and from S. They can be chosen by translating one representative twice. The permutation g swapping their corresponding support coordinates and fixing everything else exchanges u and v. It fixes S and the input proposition p={u,v}, but exchanges the only eligible outputs {u} and {v}. Since g fixes f, f(p) would have to be fixed by g, a contradiction.

For a relational selector at a designated point w0, include supp(w0) in S before choosing u,v. Then g also fixes the evaluation world, so equivariance of the selecting relation makes both eligible outputs selected if either is, contradicting its actual uniqueness. This proves failure of Relational Choice as well, without treating a world-dependent relational selector as a rigid operation. Because w0 was arbitrary, both failures hold necessarily. No assumption on orbit-finiteness was used.

Consequently the two nontrivial-action rows where IC holds or fails may all lack ordinary Choice despite having full ordinary core mathematics. In the four displayed infinite-W examples, only the trivial-action N row satisfies global Functional and Relational Choice.

Within this **infinite-world finite-support family**, the table consequently gives FC iff IC and DedInf_t together. This must not be promoted to a global implication over C or boxed Plenitude. The separately verified `countable-support-choice-countermodel-20261022.md` allows countable supports over uncountably many atom worlds and has boxed IC, DedInf_t and failure of FC_(t,t), with the same positive Infinity theory and full standard ordinary core. The support restriction is mathematically substantive.

## 8. Purity, attribution, and scope

The semantic predicates F, stars, orbit labels and shifts above need not be denoted by closed pure terms. They are admitted entities, which is exactly what universally quantified IC counterexamples and existential Dedekind sentences require. The initially chosen group G need not be the whole automorphism group of the original typed model. In particular the earlier finite-world-transposition argument still shows that all evaluation points share the same closed pure theory.

The all-type positive IC proof is uniform across admitted relational types, and the negative proofs use only σ=t. The theorem depends on taking all finitely supported subsets/functions, on E having trivial action, and, for the infinite orbit selections and full-model cases, on ambient ZFC. It must not be generalized without proof to a smaller Henkin subhierarchy, nontrivial individual action, arbitrary permutation groups, or choiceless metatheories.
