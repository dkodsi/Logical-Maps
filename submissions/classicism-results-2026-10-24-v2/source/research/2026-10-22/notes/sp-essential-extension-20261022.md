# Essential extensions of the common term algebra: a sufficient SP criterion

22 October 2026. Original ordinary mathematical deductions by OpenAI Codex, not human checked or formalized. Direct source: misc, this note. Fixed framework: Bacon–Dorr Classicism C and pure Maximalism; Strong Possibility is as in the 16 May 2023 draft, §2.6 p. 42. This note uses the new positive term-witness theorem in `.reproduction/classicism-research/sp-henkin-minimal-20261022.md`, §2.4, and the SP/existential-identity criterion in `.reproduction/classicism-research/reflection-universal-core-20261022.md`, §§4–7. Neither new theorem is attributed to the published paper.

## 1. Algebraic language and the criterion

Let N be a genuine Henkin model of pure Max and choose an actual individual a. Use the many-sorted algebraic language containing every pure term operation, including identity-valued and quantifier-valued operations, but omitting the actual-truth predicate. Let A be the algebraic reduct of N. Let B be its subalgebra of pure a-term denotations. This is the common pointed term algebra: equality of its terms is precisely C(a)-provable equality, and it embeds into every Max model after choosing an individual.

A many-sorted congruence on A is a family of equivalence relations preserved by every operation of this full algebraic signature. Say B is **essential in A** if every nonidentity congruence on A identifies two distinct elements of B. This is a precise condition on congruences, not merely on Boolean ideals of the proposition sort.

**Theorem.** If B is essential in A, then N satisfies pure Strong Possibility.

The condition permits unnamed elements; it is weaker than requiring A=B. No existence of such a genuine Henkin model is asserted.

## 2. Positive equations reflect into B

The positive term-witness theorem says that every finite existential conjunction of equations over B that is realized in a Max model is realized in B. More strongly, a solution consists of pure a-terms for which C(a) proves those equations.

For clarity, the proof is short. Write each B-parameter as a pure term in a, existentially close a and all other witnesses, and call the resulting positive-existential identity sentence E. If E is true in any Max model, C proves E: a non-theorem positive E would have E=top as a non-theorem closed identity, whose Max disequality contradicts the C-theorem E->box E. Evaluate this C-theorem E at the singleton pure-profile root. Choose witnesses and a true positive branch. Project their values to pure one-individual component profiles. The positive identities hold in every C-complete component, so they are C(a)-provable identities. They give the required witnesses in B, uniformly for the chosen a in every Max model.

This statement does not itself reflect disequalities or give witnesses for the truth of arbitrary quantified proposition terms.

## 3. Congruence chains preserve any specified finite set of disequalities

Let a finite identity diagram over B be realized by a tuple u in A. Put its matrix into disjunctive normal form and choose a true branch. Thus the branch is a finite conjunction of equations and disequations

    S_j(u) != T_j(u),  j=1,...,k.

For each j let theta_j be the congruence generated by the pair (S_j(u),T_j(u)). It is nonidentity. Essentiality gives distinct b_j,c_j in B of one common sort with b_j theta_j c_j.

Membership in a generated congruence has a finite witness: a chain from b_j to c_j, each step obtained by applying a unary polynomial operation, with finitely many parameters from A, to S_j(u) and T_j(u), in either order. Here a polynomial operation is a term operation with additional fixed parameters. This finite-chain description follows directly by closing the generating pair under symmetry, transitivity, and the finitary operations; simultaneous replacements in an n-ary operation are handled one argument at a time.

Name the finitely many extra parameters in these chains by existential variables. Record the equations linking the successive chain endpoints, as well as the original positive equations. This is a finite positive existential system over B, true in A. Section 2 gives a solution of the whole system in B.

For this new solution, S_j=T_j is impossible. If it held, every j-chain step would have equal endpoints by substitution into its polynomial operation. The chain equations would then imply b_j=c_j, contradicting their fixed distinctness in B. Hence every original disequality is preserved. We have reproduced the entire finite branch in B.

Consequently every existential finite-Boolean-identity formula over B true in A is true in B. The reverse implication holds because B is a subalgebra.

## 4. Deduction of SP and the quotient limitation

For a closed pure existential identity sentence E, if N satisfies E, section 3 gives B satisfies E. Since B embeds into every Max model, every Max model satisfies E. Conversely every Max consequence is true in N. N therefore has exactly the existential identity consequences of Max, which is equivalent to SP by the previously established criterion.

There is an easy purely algebraic way to reach essentiality. Among congruences theta on A whose restriction to B is equality, choose a maximal one by Zorn's lemma. Unions of chains remain congruences and remain trivial on B, because a pair enters a chain union at some stage. The quotient A/theta contains an isomorphic copy of B, and every nonidentity quotient congruence identifies distinct B-elements: its inverse image would otherwise enlarge theta while remaining trivial on B.

This DOES NOT construct an SP model. An algebraic quotient of the full term reduct need not admit an actual-truth interpretation with the required Henkin quantifier clauses. Even its preservation of logical term operations does not supply that truth interpretation. If such a B-preserving maximal quotient could be realized as a genuine C Henkin model, it would automatically satisfy pure Max (the pure term diagram survives) and the theorem would give SP. That is the remaining mathematical obligation, not an established closure theorem.

## 5. A simpler sufficient density condition on propositions

There is also a direct condition that does not mention higher-type congruences. Suppose that, for every nonzero possibility-fixed r in A_t, there is a nonzero q in B_t with q<=r. Then N satisfies SP.

Indeed, let P be closed pure and Max-consistent, and choose r!=bottom with r=diamond r. Choose q=Q(a) as above. Because q is a nonzero pure a-profile, unary Possibility+ makes forall x diamond Q(x) a theorem of Max. In a Max model satisfying P, choose any individual b; P and diamond Q(b) is true. Hence P and diamond Q(x) is C-consistent, and unary Possibility+ at N gives

    diamond(P and diamond Q(a)).

The actual identity q<=r, together with r=diamond r and the C monotonicity/idempotence laws for diamond, gives diamond q<=r. Therefore P and r is nonzero. This holds for every nonzero possibility-fixed r, which is exactly the identity density form of D P.

This condition only requires a nonzero pure profile below each possibility-fixed neighborhood. The pure profile need not itself be true. It is consequently implied by the fixed-a version of the earlier named-true-neighborhood criterion, but uses less than that version. The earlier criterion also allowed a to vary with the neighborhood. The present proof likewise works if q=Q(b) is allowed to use a varying actual individual b; this varying-parameter version is implied by that earlier criterion without further assumptions. Alternatively, at a singleton individual domain the distinction disappears. No necessity of this condition for SP is claimed.

A Boolean-algebraic restatement is available. Let J be the ideal generated by a nonzero possibility-fixed r, namely J={p:p<=r}. It is closed under diamond. If every such nonzero diamond-closed ideal meets B_t nontrivially, the density condition holds. More generally, any nonzero diamond-closed ideal contains diamond p for a nonzero member p, so this is equivalent to every nonzero diamond-closed ideal meeting B_t nontrivially. This is the usual essential-subalgebra condition at the Boolean algebra with diamond reduct, expressed here entirely in ideals. It is not silently identified with essentiality for the full logical term signature in §1.

## 6. The two essentiality conditions are in fact equivalent

Subsequent independent arguments by the three collaborating Codex agents establish the equivalence left unclaimed at the end of §5. Throughout this section A is a genuine Max reduct and B is its pure a-term subalgebra.

Suppose B is essential for the full logical signature, and let r!=0 satisfy diamond r=r. The congruence generated by (r,0) identifies distinct b,c in B. A finite polynomial chain witnessing this fact gives the Boolean inequality

    eq(r,0) <= eq(b,c).

Indeed each polynomial step satisfies the C-theorem of identity substitution; the identities linking successive chain endpoints have value top, and transitivity gives the displayed inequality. Thus

    0 != neq(b,c) <= not eq(r,0) = diamond r = r.

Here neq(b,c) is a pure proposition and is actually true in every Max model with the corresponding individual parameter. This proves §5's density condition, in the stronger form with an actually true pure lower bound.

Conversely, suppose the density condition holds and theta is a nonidentity full-signature congruence. Choose x!=y with x theta y. The proposition r=neq(x,y) is nonzero and diamond-fixed by Necessity of Identity, and theta identifies r with 0. Choose nonzero q in B_t with q<=r. Preservation of meet now identifies q=q and r with q and 0=0. Thus theta is nonidentity on B. This proves full essentiality.

There is also an exact ideal description. If I is a diamond-closed Boolean ideal of A_t, set

    x ~_I y iff neq(x,y) belongs to I.

The equational identity-substitution and transitivity inequalities make this a congruence for EVERY pure term operation, not just the Boolean/modal ones. At proposition sort, p~_I0 iff diamond p belongs to I iff p belongs to I. An ideal avoids B_t\{0} exactly when its congruence restricts to equality on B at every sort. If theta is a maximal B-preserving full-signature congruence, its zero-class I at proposition sort is diamond-closed; theta is contained in ~_I, and maximality makes them equal. Thus these maximal quotients can equivalently be constructed using maximal diamond-closed ideals disjoint from nonzero B_t.

## 7. What is now known about the quotient's truth interpretation

Let Q be any B-essential quotient preserving B. It satisfies all C equations because it is a quotient of a C term reduct. Restrict actual truth from a genuine Max model to B_t, obtaining a Boolean ultrafilter U_B, and extend it to a Boolean ultrafilter U on Q_t.

Every such extension has correct literal identity truth. If x!=y in Q, essentiality and a finite polynomial chain supply distinct b,c in B with eq(x,y)<=eq(b,c). The pure identity eq(b,c) is false in every Max model and hence absent from U_B. Therefore eq(x,y) is absent from U. If x=y, its identity value is top and belongs to U.

Moreover the complete operation diagram of Q, together with all its U-truth literals, is consistent with C. If a finite conjunction gamma from that diagram were inconsistent, replace its fresh constants by variables. The deduction theorem would give C proves not gamma. Necessitation is legitimate here because this is a C-theorem; the Boolean identity criterion then gives the equation gamma=0. It holds in Q, contradicting gamma's truth under U. Ordinary Henkin completeness therefore embeds this algebra-with-truth into a genuine C model R. The pure diagram makes R a Max model.

This settles the Boolean and identity-truth clauses and supplies a Henkin completion. It does NOT settle preservation of quantifier witnesses by a subsequent essential quotient. Taking another maximal B-preserving quotient of R embeds Q injectively, but can erase the truth of all newly supplied witnesses for a quantified proposition that was already true in Q. The next section gives a concrete restriction forced by essentiality. Detailed quotient and witness proofs, including finite-observation fragments that do survive, are in `.reproduction/classicism-research/sp-henkin-minimal-20261022.md`, §§16–20.

## 8. Essential genuine Max models would have no Vicinity

This is a consequence of the sufficient construction criterion, NOT an asserted consequence of SP alone.

Suppose N is a genuine Max model with essential B, and suppose an actually true proposition v is a vicinity of the actual truth ultrafilter: v<=diamond p for every actually true p. Each pure Max disequality axiom K is actually true and diamond-fixed. Therefore v<=K for every such K. Put r=diamond v. Then r!=0, r=diamond r, and r<=K for every K.

By §6 choose a nonzero pure q=Q(a) with q<=r. Hence q<=K for every pure Max axiom K. These are identities between pure a-terms, so their actual validity in Max implies their C(a)-provability. Nonzeroness of q makes C(a)+Q(a) consistent. It is a consistent recursively enumerable extension of C(a) entailing every pure Max axiom, contrary to the non-r.e.-extension theorem established earlier in the research. Thus Vicinity fails. Actuality supplies a vicinity, so Actuality fails as well.

The non-r.e.-extension theorem used here says that no consistent recursively enumerable extension of C(a) can entail pure Maximalism. Its proof dovetails enumeration of C identity proofs with enumeration of the extension's Max disequality proofs to decide the undecidable C theoremhood problem. The theorem is about recursively enumerable extensions; it makes no claim that the full, non-r.e. Max theory is inconsistent.

The ordinary singleton profile-root model has Actuality, so it cannot satisfy the essentiality/density criterion. This is a specific obstruction to that starting construction, not a refutation of Strong Possibility.

## 9. Minimal identity theory holds already in every essential quotient

The finite-diagram argument does not need to wait for Q to become a genuine Henkin model. Here is a direct proof that Q has the same existential Boolean-identity theory over B as B itself.

First suppose a finite system of equations over B has a solution in Q. The conjunction of its identity values is top. Existential-introduction inequalities are C equations, so its internal existential proposition E(a) is top in Q. This proposition is a pure a-term and therefore belongs to B; preservation of B implies that C(a) proves E(a)=top, hence E(a). The positive term-witness theorem now supplies a solution in B. For a disjunction of positive systems choose the actually satisfied algebraic branch first.

Next take a finite branch with disequalities. Essentiality supplies the finite polynomial chains from §3, and the preceding positive reflection applies to the enlarged system, reproducing all original disequalities in B. Thus B is existentially closed in Q for finite Boolean combinations of term identities.

In particular Q satisfies every universal identity sentence separately consistent with Max. The remaining obstruction is sharply confined: these algebraic universal constraints are simultaneously realizable, and identity-correct Boolean truth is available, but no proof yet makes all quantifier-valued term operations agree with that truth's actual quantification over Q. The existence of a genuine essential Max model remains unproved, and essentiality has not been shown necessary for SP.

A particularly simple truth-choice obstruction is already visible at the individual sort. Existential identity reflection over B implies Q_e=B_e={a}: a second individual would witness exists x(x!=a), which B cannot witness. Nevertheless one may start from a Max model with at least two individuals and restrict its truth to B, so that the pure proposition E_1=forall x,y(x=y) is false under U_B. Every extending ultrafilter on Q then still makes the INTERNAL proposition E_1 false, although Q's actual individual domain is singleton. Thus correct Boolean and literal-identity truth need not even satisfy this simplest universal quantifier clause. A Henkin completion supplies new individuals, and an essential quotient collapses them again. Choosing U_B with E_1 true removes this particular obstruction, but no simultaneous choice eliminating all quantified obstructions has been proved.
