**Status: neither consistency nor inconsistency of pure Strong Possibility (SP) has been proved.** The work gives an exact smaller consistency target, a sufficient algebraic construction criterion, detailed progress on its remaining witness obligation, and explicit consequences and compatible test models.

## 1. The exact consistency target

Write H=Box_ne and D=Diamond_ne. SP is equivalent to adding to Max every universal finite-Boolean-identity sentence that is separately consistent with Max. Consequently SP is consistent exactly when Max has the existential-identity disjunction property:

    Max proves E or F  =>  Max proves E, or Max proves F,

where the premise means a proof of the object-language disjunction, and E,F are closed existential formulas with finite Boolean combinations of typed identities as matrices. Terms within those identities may contain arbitrary quantifiers.

A sharper normalization is now proved:

    Con(SP) iff, for all closed pure P,Q,
       C proves (H P or H Q)  =>  Max proves P, or Max proves Q.

Every existential identity sentence E is C-equivalent to H E. Conversely

    H P iff exists r [r!=bottom and r=diamond r and r and P=r].

Thus any counterexample can be normalized to a C-theorem with two branches, each using only one proposition witness, one disequality, and two positive equations. Ordinary H-disjunction property for C is false on contingent identities; the conclusion here deliberately asks for a Max proof, which resolves those simple cases.

Proofs: `reflection-universal-core-20261022.md`, §§4–7; `sp-failed-disjunction-20261022.md`, §17. The underlying Max-reflection fact Max proves P implies Max proves H P was already in the source and earlier session notes; its rediscovery here is not claimed as new.

## 2. A sufficient algebraic construction criterion

Let B be the common algebra of pure a-term denotations in a Max model, for one chosen individual a. Equality in B is exactly C(a)-provable equality. Use the algebraic signature containing EVERY pure term operation, including identity-valued and quantifier-valued operations, but not actual truth.

A genuine Max model satisfies SP if B is essential in its full term algebra: every nonidentity congruence identifies distinct B-elements. This is equivalent to the following concrete condition:

    Every nonzero r with diamond r=r contains a nonzero q in B.

The lower bound can moreover be chosen to be an actually true, diamond-fixed pure disequality. The proof uses finite congruence chains and a positive term-witness theorem. Essentiality has NOT been shown necessary for SP.

Every Max algebra has a maximal B-preserving quotient Q, and Q is essential. Every Boolean truth ultrafilter on Q extending correct B truth is automatically correct about literal identity. Its complete operation-and-truth diagram is consistent with C, so Q embeds in a genuine Max Henkin completion. Q already satisfies all universal identity constraints of SP algebraically.

**The gap:** completing Q can add existential witnesses; taking another essential quotient can destroy their truth. No simultaneous construction has yet preserved all Henkin quantifier clauses. An algebraic quotient and a correct Boolean/identity valuation are therefore not an SP model. Concretely, essential Q has only one individual, but a valuation inherited from a two-individual Max model can still make the internal singleton sentence false; even that universal quantifier clause can fail.

Any genuine essential Max model would refute Vicinity, hence Actuality. This limits this sufficient route; it does not prove SP refutes either principle.

Proofs: `sp-essential-extension-20261022.md`, §§1–9; supporting detail in `sp-henkin-minimal-20261022.md`, §§16–21.

## 3. Exactly how far witness preservation has advanced

Positive existential equation systems over B have pure a-term witnesses. In any essential quotient, the finite congruence-chain argument extends reflection to finite Boolean identity diagrams. For a chosen new disequality s!=t, its survival through EVERY maximal B-preserving quotient is equivalent to the principal congruence generated by (s,t) already identifying distinct B-elements. This has a finite polynomial-chain certificate.

Several actual-truth witness requests also have permanent old witnesses:

- Under the singleton-individual guard, extensional first-order relational witnesses reduce to top/bottom relations.
- Dependence on finitely many old-argument truth observations reduces to finite identity tables, at arbitrary relational types.
- Finitely many direct identities and disequalities to old entities, including mutual identities among a finite tuple of witnesses, can be retained using finitely many distinct, actually false pure perturbations.
- Finite prescribed ENTITY-valued input/output graphs have explicit old interpolants whenever the necessary identity-compatibility equations hold. The interpolants can avoid any finite list of forbidden old functions while preserving those output entities exactly.

These are proved guarded C reductions before passing to quotients. They do not cover arbitrary incoming higher-type operators applied to a new witness, unbounded observations, or arbitrary quantified identity dependence.

The smaller H-clopen question is also isolated: a pure sentence and its negation both equivalent to existential identity sentences are invariant under full term-algebra embeddings between genuine C models. An appropriate genuine-model joint-embedding theorem would classify them by finite Boolean combinations of pure identities. That embedding theorem is unproved. An explicit abstract two-sort example shows why positive reflection and essential quotients alone cannot imply it; the example is not a C countermodel.

Proofs: `sp-henkin-minimal-20261022.md`, §§19–23; `sp-existential-products-20261022.md`, §§23–24.

## 4. Explicit SP consequences and compatible test constructions

Finite profile spectra exactly determine possible finite Boolean widths. In particular the pure regions

    B_(n,m) = box ND_t and box Exactly_n(e) and box Exactly_(2^m)(t)

are pointed-complete finite S5 regions. SP makes each such nonzero region a weak atom. This extends the finite Fregean cells to explicit non-Fregean examples.

For every relational input type sigma and relational output type rho, explicit CLOSED PURE, C-provably persistent operators are distinct from zero under Max but take the zero entity value on EVERY current input under SP. They give concrete failures of Functionality even for that restricted pure class. For a single INDIVIDUAL input the corresponding closed-pure assertion goes the other way: Max proves that equality at even one current argument implies equality of the two closed pure operators. Additional individual parameters and multiple individual inputs are outside that restriction. More strongly, for every admitted operator type, every current operator has arbitrarily many distinct operators agreeing with it in their ENTITY-valued outputs on all current inputs. This is a theorem for each external finite multiplicity bound, not an internal infinite-sequence claim.

The pure-profile Max root already realizes the relational ghost configurations, so they do not contradict C+Max or by themselves refute SP. Their distinction between modal nonzeroness and current witnesses is essential.

Separately, a combined finite-support retract construction makes atomlessness at every fixed relational type, failure of Actuality, failure of individual Relational Choice, and arbitrarily many individuals jointly distinctness-preservingly possible from a singleton Max source. The source retains every individually Max-consistent pure one-individual atom law at every relational type and can itself fail Relational Choice at (t,t). It is not a full SP model. This rules out several apparent conflicts among those target requirements.

Proofs: `sp-finite-profile-rank-20261022.md`; `sp-existential-products-20261022.md`, §§16–20 and §25; `sp-failed-disjunction-20261022.md`, §§14 and 18.1.
