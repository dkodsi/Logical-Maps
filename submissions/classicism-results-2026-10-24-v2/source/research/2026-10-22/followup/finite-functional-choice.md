# Full boxed Functional Choice has no finite basis over C

22 October 2026. Ordinary mathematical argument, developed in this follow-up. No claim of literature novelty, independent human checking, or formal verification. The archived notes are unchanged. Metatheory except for §7's conditional application: ordinary ZFC; the countermodel constructions use symmetric forcing extensions, not an assumption of Choice in those extensions. Section 7 states its additional consistency hypothesis explicitly.

## Exact statement

Keep C and its original relational grammar fixed: e,t, and sigma→tau only for tau≠e. Let

    F = C + {□FC_(sigma,tau) : sigma admitted, tau relational}.

“Finite axiomatization” means finitely many closed formulas with fixed types in the original language, not finitely many type schemata. Then **F is not finitely axiomatizable over C**.

This concerns equivalent axioms for F. Finite sufficient conditions imposing additional restrictions, such as the finite-domain packages in the archived notes, can still entail F. The original base types remain fixed; an optional fixed-type axiom is not treated as a schema by meta-level type substitution.

More precisely, for every finite set Delta of boxed FC instances, there is a genuine general C-model satisfying Delta and failing a further boxed instance. The construction below first gives an internally full extensional hierarchy in a symmetric ZF model. Viewed externally it is a general/Henkin hierarchy, with all required lambda and quantifier closure. Every proposition has an ordinary Boolean truth value, so boxes collapse to truth in these particular countermodels. This is a model property, not a change to C.

The renewed follow-up strengthens this in §5: writing A_0=et and A_(n+1)=A_n t, the single-axiom theories C+□Sel_(A_n) form a strictly increasing sequence whose union is F. Each stage has a countermodel with exactly that cutoff among these selector assertions.

Section 7 adds a separate type-t separation conditional on Con(ZF+AD_R). That additional metatheoretic assumption is not used in the non-finite-basis theorem or the strict cofinal hierarchy.

The later hour adds a bounded-type-order obstruction (§8), a strict input-order hierarchy allowing all outputs and retaining relative non-finite axiomatizability (§9), a cofinal presentation using only diagonal Choice (§10), incomparability of the matched input and output packages (§11), and non-finite axiomatizability even at a single fixed input type (§12).

## 1. Preserve the finitely many required domains

Work initially in a ZFC ground universe V. Form its full extensional hierarchy with D_e=omega and D_t={0,1}. Set rho_0=e and rho_(n+1)=rho_n t. Thus |D_(rho_n)|=beth_n, where beth_0=aleph_0.

Every fixed original type has cardinality bounded by some finite beth_n. Indeed, if both argument domains of an arrow are bounded by an infinite cardinal lambda, then its function domain has size at most lambda^lambda=2^lambda. Choose m so that all input/output types occurring in Delta and all their subtypes have ground cardinality at most mu=beth_m. Put kappa=mu^+, a regular ground cardinal. Here regularity uses ground Choice: a purported cofinal family of at most mu proper initial segments of kappa would express kappa as a union of at most mu sets each of size at most mu.

Force with

    P = Fn(kappa × kappa, 2, <kappa),

ordered by reverse inclusion. Conditions are partial binary functions of size less than kappa. Unions of descending chains of length less than kappa are conditions, so P is <kappa-closed. In the ZFC ground this implies that the forcing adds no sequences of ground objects of length less than kappa. In particular it adds no subsets of any ground set of cardinality at most mu.

Let G be generic. Induction on the finite type trees shows that every selected domain is unchanged in V[G]: once D_sigma,D_tau are unchanged and each has size at most mu, the graph of a function between them is a subset of a ground set of size at most mu, hence is old. The same reasoning preserves D_(rho_m) and also

    D_nu, where nu=rho_(m+1),

since D_nu is the characteristic-function copy of P(D_(rho_m)). Although |D_nu|=2^mu may be large, preserving its individual members requires only preservation of subsets of the size-mu input set. Cantor's theorem and ground Choice give a ground injection

    b : kappa → D_nu.

No GCH assumption is needed. This injection is an external set map used for coding, not an added object-language arrow.

## 2. A symmetric extension with no selector at the higher type

Let the group Sym(kappa) permute the first coordinate of P. Use the normal filter of subgroups generated by Fix(S) for finite S⊆kappa. It is a proper normal filter: finite intersections correspond to unions of finite supports, and conjugation sends Fix(S) to Fix(pi[S]). The standard symmetric-extension theorem gives

    V ⊆ N ⊆ V[G],    N transitive,    N satisfies ZF,

where every element of N has a hereditarily symmetric name fixed by Fix(S) for some finite S. No tenacity or completeness of this filter beyond the stated normal-filter hypotheses is being assumed.

Write a_i⊆kappa for the Cohen subset added in column i. Its canonical name has support {i}; the name of {a_i:i<kappa} has empty support. Distinct columns are forced distinct: below any condition, choose a second-coordinate position unused in either of two prescribed columns and assign different bits there.

Set tau=nu t=rho_(m+2). In the internally full N-hierarchy, let x_i∈D_tau be the characteristic function of b[a_i]⊆D_nu. These objects and

    A = {x_i : i<kappa}

belong to N. Their canonical names obey pi(x_i)=x_(pi(i)), and the name of A is invariant. Ground b and D_nu are fixed by all forcing automorphisms.

Explicitly, a graph name for x_i uses only ground ordered pairs (d,0) and (d,1): when d=b(alpha), its value is decided by the bit at (i,alpha), and off the range of b it is always 0. Thus its hereditary symmetry and support {i} do not require any closure of the support filter under infinite intersections. Preservation of D_nu ensures that this ground-domain graph is still a total function on the required internal domain.

**Claim.** N has no function c on its internal powerset P^N(D_tau) such that c(X)∈X for every nonempty X⊆D_tau in N.

Suppose c exists, and choose a hereditarily symmetric name dot c with finite support S. The nonempty set

    C_S = A \ {x_s : s∈S}

belongs to N and has the canonical name consisting of the names x_i for i∉S, each paired with the top condition. This name is fixed by Fix(S). In the actual generic extension, c(C_S)=x_i for some i∉S. By the ordinary forcing truth lemma there is p∈G forcing that c is a function and that c(C_S)=x_i. Choose j outside S, {i}, and the first-coordinate projection of dom(p); this is possible because that projection has size less than kappa. Let pi transpose i and j.

Then pi fixes the names of c and C_S. The forcing symmetry lemma therefore makes pi(p) force c(C_S)=x_j. Conditions p and pi(p) are compatible: column j was absent from p, the old column-i entries move to j, and every other column is unchanged. Their union would force x_i=x_j, contrary to the forced distinctness of Cohen columns and injectivity of b. This proves the claim.

This argument does not mistake forcing automorphisms for automorphisms of the transitive model N. It works on names and compatible forcing conditions. Nor does it require a condition itself to have finite support: its column set merely has size <kappa, leaving a fresh j. Functionhood of the fixed set c and the assertion that (C_S,x_i) belongs to its graph are absolute from the transitive N to V[G]: all possible failures of functionhood are witnessed by members of the transitive closure of c. These are the only assertions fed into the ordinary truth lemma. The domain of c is the fixed set P^N(D_tau), not the generally larger P^(V[G])(D_tau). No truth lemma for a claim of choice on that larger powerset is being invoked.

## 3. Verify the entire typed model and Delta

Inside N define, recursively at every admitted type,

    D_e=omega; D_t={0,1};
    D_(sigma tau) = all functions D_sigma→D_tau that belong to N.

These are sets in N. For each finite original formula, its interpretation is definable in ZF with quantifiers restricted to the relevant domain sets. Separation and Replacement in N supply its lambda abstraction, its Boolean operations, and its quantifier operations. This applies even to formulas quantifying over higher domains where new sets were added. Thus closure is not restricted to the preserved fragment. Application and equality are ordinary application and identity; beta/eta and extensionality hold. Every H theorem is sound, so H-equivalent formulas have identical abstractions, verifying precisely C's Logical Equivalence condition.

Since V⊆N⊆V[G], the domains preserved in §1 have the same values in all three universes. Their ground well-orderings belong to N. For every FC_(sigma,tau) in Delta and every serial relation U in the N-hierarchy, N can choose, for each x, the least y in the preserved well-order of D_tau satisfying U(x,y). Replacement gives the selecting function in N, hence in the appropriate admitted arrow domain. This checks all relations present in N, not just ground relations. Therefore each required FC instance holds. D_t={0,1} makes □P equivalent to P, so all of Delta holds.

At the higher output type tau from §2, the sentence

    Sel_tau := exists epsilon^((tau t)tau) forall P^(tau t)
      [(exists y^tau P(y)) → P(epsilon(P))]

fails. Otherwise Replacement in N would turn epsilon into the forbidden set-choice function c by c(X)=epsilon(chi_X), using the characteristic function of each internal subset X of D_tau. Every such chi_X is in D_(tau t).

As established in the archived gallin-comparison.md §21.2, C proves Sel_tau equivalent to FC_(tau t,tau): apply FC to U(P,y)=((exists z P(z))→P(y)); conversely apply a selector to each row of a serial relation. All displayed operation outputs are relational. Consequently this model fails FC_(tau t,tau), and also its box. No e-valued function type or type quantifier has been introduced.

The forcing construction has the usual metatheoretic interpretation. If a ground finite C+Delta proof established the failed instance, the same proof would remain valid in the forcing extension, contradicting the model just constructed. Equivalently, the forcing relation proves that there is no such proof, while finite proof codes and their correctness are absolute between V and V[G]. Thus C+Delta plus the negation of the failed instance is syntactically consistent in the ground metatheory. General BBK completeness then supplies a C-model there; the argument does not require an additional hypothesis that a countable transitive model of ZFC exists.

There is also an entirely first-order extraction route. Use the recursive many-sorted extensional Henkin theory H_0 and Gallin interpretation in archived positive-effective.md §§3–4, with a named evaluation world, the translations of Delta, and the translated negation. For the one-world constructions also include the auxiliary sentence that every world equals the named world; this retains a genuinely one-world model after extraction. The forced internal hierarchy is a model of this first-order theory. Any finite contradiction proof in the ground would still be a contradiction proof in the extension, so ordinary first-order completeness supplies a ground Henkin model. Its Gallin interpretation is a genuine C-model. This route preserves the original grammar and does not require the ground model to remain internally full or externally standard in its number objects.

## 4. Conclude non-finite axiomatizability

Suppose finitely many original sentences Gamma axiomatized F over C. Each member of Gamma has a finite derivation from C and finitely many boxed FC instances. Taking their union gives a finite Delta with C+Delta proving Gamma. The model above satisfies C+Delta and hence Gamma, but fails a boxed FC instance. This contradicts the alleged equivalence. Therefore no such finite Gamma exists.

The same proof covers finite collections of the stated polyadic forms: there are still only finitely many output types, and the least-output construction is defined on the finite input product as a set and then curried into admitted arrows. No object-language product type is needed.

## 5. A strict cofinal sequence of single-axiom theories

Let A_0=et, A_(n+1)=A_n t, and

    T_n = C + □Sel_(A_n)       (n=0,1,2,...).

Then, as deductively closed theories in the original language,

    T_0 ⊊ T_1 ⊊ T_2 ⊊ ... ,       union_n T_n = F.

### Retractions and the ascending inclusions

For every relational alpha, C proves that alpha is a retract of alpha t. The embedding is the haecceity H_x=λy^alpha.(y=x). Flatten alpha as beta_1...beta_k t and define

    r_alpha(P) = λz_1...z_k. exists y^alpha [P(y) and y[z_1,...,z_k]].

The empty tuple handles alpha=t. Substitution of H_x gives the formula exists y(y=x and y[zbar]), which H proves equivalent to x[zbar]. Logical Equivalence applied to that genuine H theorem, followed by beta/eta conversion, proves r_alpha(H_x)=x. This uses no equivalence under optional assumptions. Both displayed operation types have relational outputs.

Also t is a C-provable retract of et: use i(p)=λx^e.p and r(f)=forall x^e f(x). The retraction equation follows from the genuine H equivalence (forall x^e p)↔p in the standing nonempty-domain logic.

In general, a retraction i:alpha→beta, r:beta→alpha transfers Sel_beta to Sel_alpha: apply the beta-selector to the i-image of a nonempty alpha-property and then apply r. The original lambda term for that image is λz^beta.exists y^alpha(P(y) and z=i(y)). Thus the pure retractions above give C-theorems Sel_(A_(n+1))→Sel_(A_n) and Sel_(A_n)→Sel_t. Necessitating these discharged C theorems gives the corresponding boxed implications. Hence T_n⊆T_(n+1).

The archived exact-instance proof (gallin-comparison.md §21.3) shows C+□Sel_t entails B=C+□Plenitude. Therefore every T_n supplies all of B; B is not an additional axiom in the definition of T_n.

### Cofinality: encode each fixed type at a finite tower level

Under B there is, for every original type sigma, a pure-term injection j_sigma:sigma→A_(d(sigma)). One explicit recursive bound is

    d(e)=d(t)=0;
    d(sigma→tau)=max(d(sigma),d(tau))+3.

For e use x↦λy^e(y=x); for t use p↦λy^e p. Both are injective already in C. Lift embeddings to a common higher A_k by iterating haecceities.

For the arrow step, an ordered pair of alpha-objects has the Kuratowski code

    K(a,b) = λX^(alpha t) [X=H_a or X=U_(a,b)],
    U_(a,b) = λu^alpha [u=a or u=b].

This has type (alpha t)t and is injective on ordered pairs already in C. Here is the finite argument, without extensionality. Suppose K(a,b)=K(c,d), and apply the codes to H_a. Either H_a=H_c or H_a=U_(c,d). In the first case a=c. Applying the outer-code equality also to U_(a,b) and U_(c,d) forces U_(a,b)=U_(c,d): if they differed, each would have to equal the common singleton member H_a, a contradiction. If b=a, application of this equality at d forces d=a; if b≠a, application at b forces b=d. In the second case, H_a=U_(c,d) forces a=c=d by application at c and d. The outer equality evaluated at U_(a,b) then forces U_(a,b)=H_a, so b=a=d. Thus in both cases a=c and b=d. No inference from general coextension to identity is used.

The bound below is sufficient, not claimed optimal. One tempting shortcut is nevertheless impossible uniformly: there is no closed pure pairing term of curried type alpha→(alpha→(alpha t)) that is injective on ordered pairs throughout B, already for alpha=et. In the full one-world model with two individuals, alpha has four elements and alpha t has sixteen. Swapping the two individuals fixes four ordered pairs in alpha squared, leaving twelve nonfixed pairs. It fixes eight elements of alpha t, since a fixed subset of alpha is a union of the three orbits {bottom}, {top}, and the pair of singleton properties; only eight predicates are nonfixed. Every closed pure term is equivariant under this automorphism, and an equivariant injection must send nonfixed inputs to nonfixed outputs. Twelve cannot inject into eight. This excludes that particular pure one-level pairing shortcut, not parameterized or existential coding, and not other possible improvements to d.

Write j_sigma^k and j_tau^k for the injections lifted to the common target A_k. Put

    j_(sigma tau)(f) = λz^(A_(k+2)). exists x^sigma
      [z=K(j_sigma^k(x), j_tau^k(f(x)))].

Its output type is A_(k+3). If the codes of f and g are equal, apply that equality at z=K(j_sigma^k(x),j_tau^k(f(x))). Beta conversion makes the left membership assertion true, so the right supplies some x' with equal pair code in g's graph. Pair injectivity and the two input injections imply x'=x and f(x)=g(x). Universally generalize over x and use Functionality at the single arrow sigma→tau to infer f=g. This completes the induction. B supplies that Functionality instance; no merely material equivalence proved from B is submitted to Logical Equivalence. At lower induction stages B may already have been used for their corresponding Functionality instances.

For a relational tau, convert its injection into A_k into a retraction under B. Use the explicit relation

    R(z,y) := i(y)=z or [(not exists u^tau i(u)=z) and y=bottom_tau].

Injectivity makes R total and single-valued. The unboxed Plenitude instance with input A_k and output tau supplies an operation r:A_k→tau satisfying r(i(y))=y. This r is an existential witness supplied by Plenitude, not a claimed pure-term inverse. The output restriction is respected; no retraction to e is required. A selector epsilon on A_k now yields the tau-selector P↦r(epsilon(λz.exists y(P(y) and z=i(y)))). This last selector transfer uses the retraction equation and beta conversion, without any further Functionality or Choice assumption.

Consequently B proves Sel_(A_(d(tau)))→Sel_tau. To check the boxed transfer explicitly, let delta_1,...,delta_r be the finitely many boxed Plenitude axioms used in this derivation. Discharge these closed premises: C proves (delta_1 and ... and delta_r)→(Sel_(A_(d(tau)))→Sel_tau). Necessitate this C theorem and apply modal K. Each delta_i already has the form □P_i, so S4 supplies □delta_i from delta_i. Hence B proves □Sel_(A_(d(tau)))→□Sel_tau. This is stability of the boxed base, not necessitation of the optional unboxed selector hypothesis.

Every fixed boxed FC instance, with any admitted input sigma and relational output tau, now follows from T_(d(tau)), since Sel_tau supplies all input instances at that output type. Conversely F contains every T_n. A finite derivation from F uses only finitely many such instances, hence is contained in one T_n. Therefore the union of the ascending theories is precisely F.

### Strictness at every adjacent level

Apply §§1–3 with mu=beth_n and kappa=mu^+, without asking to preserve the large input domain of Sel_(A_n). Here

    A_n=rho_(n+1)=nu.

The entire domain D_(A_n) is unchanged, even though its cardinality is 2^mu. Its ground well-order belongs to N and remains a well-order: the ground isomorphism to an ordinal also belongs to N. In N, choosing the least member of each nonempty internal subset of D_(A_n) defines a selector by Replacement. This works also for new subsets and therefore proves Sel_(A_n) in the full internal typed hierarchy. Its box holds because truth is two-valued in this model.

The symmetric obstruction of §2 occurs exactly at

    tau=nu t=A_(n+1),

so this same model refutes Sel_(A_(n+1)) and its box. It satisfies T_n but not T_(n+1). This proves strictness for every n. By the ascending implications it satisfies all tower selectors at levels at most n and fails all those at levels greater than n.

This sharper argument needs preservation of the selector's output domain, not its property-input domain. The latter may gain new elements; the preserved well-order still chooses from every internal nonempty subset. That is why the adjacent-level separation is stronger than simply applying the earlier coarse bound to all types appearing in a selector formula.

Combining cofinality with these models also gives an explicit alternative presentation of the original finite-package construction: for a finite Delta take the maximum d(tau) of its output types, use that stage's model, and fail the next tower selector. This is a refinement of the same forcing route, not an appeal to the old nominal model as a hierarchy proof.

## 6. A finite-neighborhood strengthening

Let M_V be the ground full extensional hierarchy on e=omega, t={0,1}, and let Y_V be its complete pure theory. For **every finite Gamma⊆Y_V**, there is a C-model satisfying Gamma and failing a boxed FC instance. Gamma need not consist of Choice axioms or consequences of F.

To see this, fully elaborate the finitely many formulas in Gamma and choose m so that every type occurring in their terms, binders, and logical operators has ground cardinality at most beth_m. The preservation argument of §1 keeps all these domains unchanged. Induction on those formulas and their subterms therefore gives exactly the same denotations and truth values in the internally full N-hierarchy: applications and equality are unchanged, quantifiers range over the same sets, and lambda abstractions have the same graphs on the same domains. Thus Gamma remains true. The higher selector failure from §2 is still present. The same finite-proof/BBK argument supplies a ground general model when desired.

In the Stone space S(C) of complete consistent pure theories, S(F) is closed. The point Y_V belongs to S(F), because the ground full hierarchy satisfies Choice. Every finite basic neighborhood of Y_V nevertheless contains a completion outside S(F), by the preceding paragraph. Therefore Y_V is a **boundary point** of S(F). This gives a concrete witness to failure of openness; it is compatible with the finite-domain sufficient conditions that give S(F) nonempty interior.

More generally, for any fixed finite Theta⊆Y_V, the all-type boxed FC schema has no finite basis over C+Theta. A proposed finite basis would be derivable from Theta and finitely many FC instances Delta. Apply the finite-neighborhood construction to Theta∪Delta, which is true in M_V, and obtain a model refuting a further FC instance. This includes the Fregean Axiom as an optional preset; the enlarged target is C+Theta+F, not a redefinition of C or F. Separately, F has no finite axiomatization over B: all the one-world internal hierarchies in §§1–3 satisfy B, since Replacement realizes every internally total single-valued relation without Choice. A putative finite basis over B would use finitely many FC instances Delta, and the same B+Delta countermodel refutes it. This argument does not need a finite presentation of B.

### 6.1 The same obstruction with both boxed Infinity axioms

The finite-neighborhood construction also works for the full ground Gallin model with E=W=omega. This matters because both boxed quantified Infinity axioms hold there, whereas the one-world model above has a finite proposition domain.

Choose mu=beth_m with m≥1 large enough to bound every original domain used in the specified finite conditions. In the symmetric N use D_e=E, D_t=P^N(W), and all N-functions at the admitted arrow types. Forcing is still <kappa-closed for kappa=mu^+. It preserves P(W), because |W|<kappa, and preserves all selected higher domains by the previous graph argument. Proposition operations are the usual set operations on W; equality has value W or the empty set; and forall_sigma P has value {w: forall x∈D_sigma, w∈P(x)}. All these objects and every lambda abstraction exist by ZF in N. H is sound at every world, and pointwise equality of the proposition sets verifies Logical Equivalence, so the construction is a C-model in the original language. Box is universal truth over W.

The finite conditions keep exactly their ground proposition denotations, not just their truth at one world. Thus their truth at the chosen world, including any boxes, is preserved. The ground cardinalities of rho_0=e, rho_(r+1)=rho_r t are again beth_r for r≥1, since |D_t|=2^aleph_0. Hence D_nu at nu=rho_(m+1) is preserved and admits a ground injection of kappa, just as before.

For each Cohen column define x_i:D_nu→D_t using the two rigid values W and the empty set according to membership in b[a_i]. The finite-support argument of §2 is unchanged for these distinct x_i. If Sel_tau, tau=nu t, held at any world w, its selector would induce a set-choice function on P^N(D_tau): represent X⊆D_tau by the rigid property chi_X taking values W on X and the empty set elsewhere. The selector clause at w forces its value on chi_X to belong to X. Replacement gives the forbidden function c in N. Therefore Sel_tau fails at every world, and the corresponding boxed FC instance fails.

It follows that the complete pure theory of this full ground Gallin model is also a boundary point of S(F). The preceding finite-preset argument applies to every finite collection of its truths. In particular, **adding both boxed quantified Infinity axioms does not make full boxed Functional Choice finitely axiomatizable over that enlarged base**.

Here the quantified Infinity axiom at sigma is exactly neg exists Z(FiniteCardinality_sigma(Z) and Z(universal_sigma)), not the separate arithmetic premise I_sigma. In the full ground model, the rigid property selecting exactly the standard Frege numeral objects is available and inductive. Hence FiniteCardinality_sigma has only those standard values; conversely every standard numeral satisfies it by a finite induction. At each world the nth numeral holds precisely of properties with n-element extensions. Both D_e=omega and D_t=P(omega) are infinite, so their universal properties have no such finite cardinality at any world. This proves both boxed quantified Infinity axioms in the ground witness. The finite-formula preservation argument supplies them in the separating models. It asserts no external-standardness preservation for a later BBK reconstruction in the ground universe.

## 7. One further lemma: uniformization versus global selection

This section is separate from the symmetric-forcing proof. It gives an exact set-theoretic reading of the two Choice assertions in internally full Gallin structures and a **conditional** separation of the previously undecided type-t packages. It adds no object-language resources or axioms to C. Only its final application assumes a stronger metatheory.

**Lemma (over ZF).** In a full internal Gallin hierarchy with a nonempty world set W, write X=D_sigma and Y=D_tau, with tau relational. At any world w:

1. FC_(sigma,tau) holds iff every internal serial set relation R⊆X×Y has an internal uniformizing function f:X→Y.
2. Sel_tau holds iff Y is internally well-orderable.

All sets, powersets, relations and functions in this lemma are those of the same ZF universe used to form the hierarchy. External well-orderability of a countable presentation is irrelevant.

For (1), an object-language relation U determines the internal set relation R_w={(x,y):w∈U(x,y)} by Separation. An internal uniformizer for R_w is precisely an available operation witnessing FC at w. Conversely represent an arbitrary internal set relation R by the rigid curried relation taking value W on its pairs and the empty set elsewhere. Internal Replacement supplies all its curried functions. FC at w gives a uniformizer of R. No choice of uniformizers simultaneously for different R is asserted.

For (2), a selector epsilon at w gives a choice function on every nonempty internal subset Z⊆Y by Z↦epsilon(chi_Z), where chi_Z takes the rigid values W and the empty set. In ZF such a choice function well-orders Y: recursively choose from the unused remainder until it is empty; if the recursion continued through the Hartogs ordinal of Y, it would inject that ordinal into Y, a contradiction. Separation and transfinite recursion suffice. Conversely, a well-order of Y gives, for every internal property P, its least member true at w, using any fixed default element when there is none. Replacement makes this an available selector operation. Since the right sides of (1) and (2) do not depend on w, their truth conditions also characterize the corresponding boxed assertions.

The selecting operation in this converse may depend on w. The boxed sentence is □exists epsilon..., with the existential inside the box; no single epsilon that selects correctly at every world is being asserted.

### A conditional type-t separator

Write Reals=P(omega), let Unif(Reals) denote uniformization for all serial relations on this set, and let WO(Reals) denote its well-orderability. The lemma gives an exact implication: **any model of ZF+Unif(Reals)+not WO(Reals) yields a general C-model satisfying □FC_(t,t) and □not Sel_t**. Take E=W=omega and D_t=P(omega), identify this with the Cantor reals, and form the full internal hierarchy. Clause (1) supplies FC_(t,t) at every world, and clause (2) refutes Sel_t at every world.

For a concrete sufficient hypothesis, ZF+AD_R proves Unif(Reals)+not WO(Reals). Here AD_R is determinacy of every length-omega perfect-information game whose moves are reals. Both needed deductions can be checked directly:

- Given serial Q⊆Reals×Reals, let I first play x and II next play y; II wins exactly when Q(x,y), irrespective of later moves. A winning strategy for I is impossible, since a single witness y defeats its first move and later II moves may be fixed. Determinacy gives a strategy for II, whose response to each one-move history x is a uniformizer. This requires no simultaneous preliminary choice of the witnesses y.
- If the reals were well-orderable, enumerate the strategies for both players in binary games by a cardinal kappa=|2^omega|. A fixed binary strategy has kappa compatible runs, via the opponent's free binary choices. At each stage alpha<kappa, fewer than kappa runs have previously been marked. Choose a fresh compatible run for the listed I strategy to be losing for I, and one for the listed II strategy to be winning for I, using the proposed well-order to choose the least available runs. Transfinite recursion gives a payoff set for which neither player has a winning strategy. This contradicts binary-game determinacy, which follows from AD_R by embedding the binary moves into the reals and making the first illegal move lose.

Thus, writing T_tt=C+□FC_(t,t) and G=C+□Sel_t, the precise metatheoretic conclusion is

    Con(ZF+AD_R) implies Con(T_tt + □not Sel_t).

Since C proves G contains T_tt, this gives **T_tt strictly weaker than G conditional on Con(ZF+AD_R)**. It is a new conditional separator, not an unconditional ZFC construction and not a consequence of the earlier symmetric forcing. No consistency proof for AD_R or optimal consistency bound is supplied here. The frozen archive's statement that no separator had then been established is left unchanged.

For completeness of the transfer, no transitive model of the determinacy theory is required. Given any set model M of ZF+AD_R, use its internal typed sets and application, taking the domain of a sort externally to be the M-elements of that internal set. For each standard original formula, induction on its syntax identifies external evaluation with M's interpretation of its internal set denotation. This supplies a general C-model even when M is nonstandard or externally ill-founded. In particular M's internally absent well-order does not become an available typed object merely because the ambient ZFC universe can well-order the presentation. Ordinary first-order completeness supplies such an M under the displayed consistency assumption.

## 8. No bounded-type-order axiomatization, even with infinitely many axioms

Define the ordinary syntactic type order by

    ord(e)=ord(t)=0;
    ord(sigma→tau)=max(ord(sigma)+1, ord(tau)).

For n≥1, let L_n be the fragment of closed original formulas all of whose types, after full elaboration, have order at most n. This counts logical-constant types as well as term and binder types. The restriction n≥1 ensures closure under negation; an order-zero fragment is contained in L_1 and is covered by its conclusion. No fixed-type axiom is reinterpreted as a schema by type substitution.

**Uniform size lemma.** In the ground full hierarchy with e=omega and t={0,1}, every type sigma has |D_sigma|≤beth_(ord(sigma)). The proof is structural induction. The bases satisfy the bound. If an arrow has order k≥1, its input has size at most beth_(k−1), and its output has size at most beth_k by the induction hypothesis. Therefore its function set has size at most

    (beth_k)^(beth_(k−1)) = (2^(beth_(k−1)))^(beth_(k−1)) = beth_k.

The bound is uniform over the infinitely many types of a fixed order, including types of arbitrarily large finite arity.

There is a sharper preservation argument than the earlier graph-size estimate. Fix n≥1, put mu=beth_(n−1), and kappa=mu^+. In an arrow type of order at most n, the input has order at most n−1 and ground size at most mu<kappa. Once the output domain is preserved by structural induction, a new function on this input would be a new sequence of ground objects of length less than kappa. This is impossible. The argument does not require the output domain itself to have size less than kappa. It preserves **every** domain of order at most n simultaneously, including those of ground size beth_n.

Run §§1–3 at these mu and kappa. Every logical operation whose type occurs in L_n keeps its graph. Consequently the ground full hierarchy and the internal N-hierarchy agree on every L_n formula, with every assignment from their common relevant domains. Yet the latter fails the selector at rho_(n+1). This sharpens the original finite-list preservation bound by one order.

For a ground formulation, let Sigma_n be the set of all L_n truths of the ground full hierarchy. The forced countermodel satisfies C+Sigma_n and the displayed failure. A ground contradiction proof would use finitely many sentences of Sigma_n and remain a contradiction in the extension. First-order Henkin extraction therefore gives a ground general C-model satisfying Sigma_n and failing F. Since negation preserves the type-order bound, it agrees with the full model on the entire closed fragment L_n, including its false sentences.

It follows that **no set Gamma⊆L_n, finite or infinite, axiomatizes F over C**. If it did, every member of Gamma would hold in the ground full model, so Gamma⊆Sigma_n, and the separating model would satisfy Gamma while refuting F. This is a restriction on the actual fixed-type formulas in Gamma; it does not treat a finite list of unbounded type schemata as a bounded-type axiomatization. All statements here remain in the original finitary language.

## 9. All outputs with bounded inputs still leave a strict Choice hierarchy

For n≥0, define the deductively closed theory

    I_n = C + {□FC_(sigma,tau) : ord(sigma)≤n, tau relational}.

The output types in I_n are unrestricted. In particular I_0 includes every individual-input and every proposition-input FC instance, with any admitted relational output. These are infinite schemata, not the single sentence T_tt.

**Theorem.** In the original framework,

    I_0 ⊊ I_1 ⊊ I_2 ⊊ ... ,       union_n I_n = F.

Moreover F is not finitely axiomatizable over any I_n. The countermodels for adjacent strictness can also satisfy both boxed quantified Infinity axioms, using the infinite-world variant below. No determinacy hypothesis is used.

### Blocked Cohen columns with supports of size less than kappa

First use the one-world ground hierarchy. Set mu=beth_n, kappa=mu^+, nu=rho_(n+1), and tau=nu t. As before, D_nu is preserved and a ground injection b:kappa→D_nu exists. Replace the original forcing and support convention by

    P = Fn(kappa × kappa × kappa, 2, <kappa).

Read coordinates as (block i, column j, bit alpha). The group is the product over i<kappa of Sym(kappa), acting on j separately within each block and fixing i and alpha. The normal filter is generated by Fix(S) for S⊆kappa×kappa of size <kappa, where Fix(S) fixes each indicated column in its indicated block.

The filter is proper, normal and kappa-complete: a union of fewer than kappa supports of size less than kappa still has size less than kappa. The forcing is <kappa-closed. The symmetric extension N satisfies ZF+DC_<kappa by the standard preservation theorem for a <kappa-closed forcing and kappa-complete support filter (Karagila, Proposition 13.4, PDF p.65). In this system every condition also has a permitted support, namely its fewer than kappa columns. No unmentioned tenacity assumption is needed.

In particular N has Choice for every well-ordered family of length lambda<kappa. Finite families are handled in ZF. After reindexing an infinite family by its cardinal, obtain the consequence of DC_lambda by taking the tree of partial choice functions on initial segments of lambda, allowing complete functions as maximal nodes. A strictly increasing chain of order type the infinite cardinal lambda or a maximal node supplies a complete choice function. Closure uses unions of increasing chains; a union that already has domain lambda is itself a complete function, so the argument does not assume regularity of lambda.

All domains of input order at most n are unchanged and retain ground enumerations of lengths at most mu<kappa. Given any internally serial relation from such a domain into an arbitrary D_eta, its nonempty rows form a family indexed by one of these ground enumerations. The preceding Choice principle selects a row member, and Replacement supplies the resulting operation. This applies to every relational eta, including new, non-well-orderable higher domains. Hence the full N-hierarchy satisfies I_n. The ground preservation proof did not change when the coordinate set became kappa cubed.

### A family that defeats the next input level

Let a_(i,j)⊆kappa be the Cohen set in column (i,j), and let x_(i,j)∈D_tau be the characteristic function of b[a_(i,j)]. Distinct columns are forced to give distinct functions, by the unused-bit argument of §2. Each x_(i,j) has support {(i,j)}. The sets

    B_i = {x_(i,j): j<kappa}

and the family i↦B_i belong to N: each block is invariant under the whole group, and every child name is hereditarily symmetric. This does not assert that the column enumerations (i,j)↦x_(i,j) belong to N. They are external indexing notation; the invariant family of unenumerated sets B_i is the internal object. In contrast, the injections b and, in §11, d are actual ground functions and do belong to N.

There is no N-choice function for this kappa-indexed family. If c were one, choose a name with support S⊆kappa×kappa of size <kappa. Choose a block i not in the first-coordinate projection of S. In the actual extension c(i)=x_(i,j) for some j. Take p forcing functionhood and this evaluation. Within block i choose a different column j' untouched by p. The transposition of j and j' in that block fixes the name of c and the ground input i. It sends p to a compatible condition forcing c(i)=x_(i,j'), contradicting the forced distinction. This uses exactly the ordinary truth-lemma absoluteness checked in §2.

Represent this family by the following internal serial relation from D_nu to D_tau:

    R(x,y) iff [exists i<kappa (x=b(i) and y∈B_i)]
                  or [x is outside ran(b) and y=bottom_tau].

Here the set-theoretic index i is used only to define an internal set relation; it is not an added object-language sort or forbidden e-valued operation. The full internal hierarchy contains its rigid characteristic relation. An operation selecting from R would give i↦f(b(i)) by Replacement and hence choose from the B_i, which is impossible. Thus FC_(nu,tau) fails. Since ord(nu)=n+1, this is an axiom of I_(n+1). We have proved adjacent strictness. The ascending inclusion is immediate from the definition, and the union is F because every fixed input type has finite order and every proof uses finitely many instances.

### Robustness and exact scope

For non-finite axiomatizability over I_n, let Delta be any finite set of boxed FC instances used by a proposed finite basis. Enlarge mu to beth_m so that it bounds every input domain of order at most n and every output type of Delta, including their subtypes. Run the same block construction at kappa=mu^+. DC_<kappa gives every axiom of I_n; preserved ground well-orders of Delta's output domains give all of Delta, even for new relations on new input domains. The block family still refutes FC_(rho_(m+1),rho_(m+2)). The finite-derivation argument of §4 therefore rules out the proposed basis.

For adjacent strictness with both boxed quantified Infinity axioms, use E=W=omega and D_t=P^N(omega). In the full ground hierarchy the uniform size bound is now |D_sigma|≤beth_(ord(sigma)+1), by the same exponent calculation. Define pi_0=t and pi_(r+1)=pi_r t, so ord(pi_r)=r and |D_(pi_r)|=beth_(r+1). Take mu=beth_(n+1), kappa=mu^+, nu=pi_(n+1), and tau=nu t. All input domains of order at most n are preserved; D_nu is preserved as the functions from the size-mu D_(pi_n) into the unchanged D_t, and has ground size 2^mu≥kappa. Use a ground injection b:kappa→D_nu and the same blocks and support filter. At each world, DC_<kappa selects from the section of any serial relation with a bounded-order input. The rigid block relation fails at every world. Thus I_n holds and I_(n+1) fails in this infinite-world variant as well.

Both boxed quantified Infinity axioms hold directly inside this N-hierarchy; they need not be obtained by preserving all types in their formulas. ZF recursion forms the set of Frege numeral objects at each base sort, and the full internal predicate domain contains its rigid characteristic predicate. As in §6.1, FiniteCardinality consequently consists exactly of these numeral values. Finite induction identifies the nth value with n-element current extensions. The internal sets omega and P^N(omega) are infinite, the latter already containing all singleton subsets of omega. Their universal properties therefore have no finite cardinality at any world. This is an internal ZF argument; it does not assert external standardness for ground Henkin models subsequently obtained by completeness.

All these models are extracted back to the ambient ZFC metatheory as in §3. For the full stated pattern retain the entire countable schema I_n, □Sel_nu, and □not FC_(nu,tau), together with the two boxed Infinity sentences when used. These sentences all hold in the constructed N-hierarchy. Retaining the selector explicitly matters: I_n alone need not recover it, as §11 will show. The block construction addresses the input dimension of Choice. It does not remove the consistency hypothesis of §7's type-t separator: here low proposition domains retain well-orders and therefore already satisfy Sel_t.

## 10. Diagonal Choice is another strict single-axiom presentation

The following reduction is a C theorem and requires no optional B assumption:

    FC_(alpha t,alpha t) implies Sel_alpha       (alpha relational).

Use the pure retraction r_alpha:alpha t→alpha from §5. Given a property P of alpha-objects, let Q(P,Z) say that if P is nonempty, then Z=H_y for some y with P(y). This is a serial relation on (alpha t)×(alpha t): when P is nonempty use the haecceity of a witness, and otherwise any property Z works. Diagonal Choice yields g with Q(P,g(P)). Then P↦r_alpha(g(P)) is a selector on alpha, since r_alpha(H_y)=y. Only beta conversion and the already proved pure retraction equation are used. Necessitating the discharged C implication gives its boxed form.

Thus all-type boxed Functional Choice is equivalent over C to **the diagonal schema alone**:

    F = C + {□FC_(alpha,alpha): alpha relational}.

Each output alpha is handled by the diagonal instance at alpha t, followed by Sel_alpha→FC_(sigma,alpha) for arbitrary input sigma. This is still an unbounded type schema; it is not a finite axiomatization.

For a strict cofinal sequence of single sentences, put

    D_n = C + □FC_(A_n,A_n).

The reductions give D_n⊆T_n⊆D_(n+1). The second inclusion is strict. The one-world block model of §9 at n satisfies T_n because D_(A_n) retains its ground well-order. It refutes FC_(A_n,A_(n+1)). To check the input transfer in C explicitly, for serial R on alpha×beta put

    R'(Z,y) := [exists x^alpha (Z=H_x and R(x,y))]
                  or [not exists x^alpha Z=H_x].

This is serial on (alpha t)×beta. A selecting operation g there restricts to x↦g(H_x); injectivity of haecceities makes it select R. Hence C proves FC_(alpha t,beta)→FC_(alpha,beta), including alpha=e. Diagonal FC_(A_(n+1),A_(n+1)) would therefore imply the refuted instance. The block model refutes D_(n+1).

Consequently

    D_n ⊆ T_n ⊊ D_(n+1),
    D_0 ⊊ D_1 ⊊ D_2 ⊊ ... ,       union_n D_n = F.

The proof does not establish whether D_n=T_n for general n. The §7 determinacy model does give the conditional strictness D_0⊊T_0: with E=W=omega, A_0 is the set of functions omega→P(omega), canonically bijective in ZF with P(omega×omega) and hence with the reals. Uniformization transports along this bijection, whereas a selector would well-order the reals. This conditional observation uses exactly §7's consistency assumption.

These transfers also give an exact, rather than merely partial, spectrum for the towers in §9's models. Output Choice transfers down a pure retraction i:alpha→beta, r:beta→alpha by applying FC_(sigma,beta) to R'(x,z)=R(x,r(z)) and composing the result with r; seriality follows by using i on a witness. Combine this with the input transfer above. In the one-world block model at n,

    FC_(rho_r,rho_s) holds iff r≤n or s≤n+1       (r≥0, s≥1).

The positive cases use DC_<kappa or a preserved output well-order. Every remaining pair transfers downward to the failed corner (rho_(n+1),rho_(n+2)), so is false. In the infinite-world variant replace rho by pi and allow r,s≥0; the same equivalence holds at every world and for the boxed assertions. This classifies the displayed tower pairs only. Syntactic type order by itself does not classify all FC instances, since other types of the same order can have different sizes and retractions.

## 11. Input and output packages are incomparable at matched stages

For every n≥0, the theories I_n and T_n are incomparable. In fact T_n fails to entail even I_0, while there is a model of I_n refuting the weaker diagonal sentence of D_n. These are ordinary-ZFC constructions; §7's determinacy assumption is not used.

### Preserve T_n but defeat countable-input Choice

Start with the one-world hierarchy, mu=beth_n, kappa=mu^+, nu=A_n and tau=nu t. Use the same columns as §9 but only omega blocks:

    P = Fn(omega × kappa × kappa, 2, <kappa).

The group permutes the kappa columns separately in each block. This time the support filter is generated by **finite** sets of columns. The low output domain D_nu is still preserved and has its ground well-order, so Sel_nu and T_n hold.

The invariant nonempty sets B_i⊆D_tau, for i<omega, have no N-choice function: any candidate has support meeting only finitely many blocks; choose an untouched block and apply the same compatible-transposition proof. Since D_e=omega, the family itself is a serial relation of type e tau t with no selecting operation. Thus □FC_(e,tau), an axiom of I_0, fails. This proves T_n does not entail I_m for any m≥0.

### Preserve I_n but defeat the selector at A_n

Here use a chain-condition preservation theorem for Dependent Choice, rather than increasing the forcing closure. For n≥1 set

    mu=beth_(n−1),  kappa=mu^+,  lambda=beth_n=2^mu,
    delta=lambda^+,  nu=rho_n,  tau=nu t=A_n.

Use the forcing

    P = Fn(delta × delta × kappa, 2, <kappa),

with delta blocks, delta columns per block, and kappa bits per column. The group preserves blocks and permutes columns within them. The normal filter is generated by supports S⊆delta×delta of size <delta; it is delta-complete.

This forcing is <kappa-closed and delta-c.c. For the latter, the standard Cohen chain-condition bound is (kappa^(<kappa))^+-c.c.; here kappa≤2^mu=lambda and

    kappa^(<kappa) ≤ lambda^mu = (2^mu)^mu = lambda.

Thus it is delta-c.c. No GCH hypothesis is needed. Proposition 13.6 of Karagila's notes (PDF p.66) gives N⊨DC_<delta from this chain condition and delta-completeness of the filter. Every condition also has support of size <kappa≤lambda<delta, so the tenacity normal form used in that preservation proof is already available here.

By §8's sharper structural argument all type domains of order at most n are preserved. Their ground enumerations have ordinal lengths at most lambda<delta. Even if the forcing collapses a ground cardinal, these enumerations remain bijections from those ground ordinals, and DC_<delta supplies choice for their well-ordered rows. Therefore I_n holds for every relational output type.

There is a ground injection b:kappa→D_nu, since |D_nu|=lambda≥kappa. Code the Cohen columns as x_(i,j)∈D_tau using b and form the invariant family B_i={x_(i,j):j<delta}, indexed by i<delta. The support of a candidate choice function meets fewer than delta blocks, so an untouched block and a fresh column again contradict functionhood. A fresh column exists even after choosing the forcing condition, since that condition mentions fewer than kappa≤delta columns.

Crucially, the **ground subset** D_tau^V of the possibly enlarged D_tau^N has ground size 2^lambda≥delta. Fix a ground injection d:delta→D_tau^V. It remains an injection into D_tau^N; preservation of the whole output domain is neither assumed nor wanted. Define a serial relation on D_tau×D_tau that requires y∈B_i at input d(i), and uses bottom_tau off ran(d). A diagonal choice operation would restrict to i↦f(d(i)), contradicting the no-choice family. Hence FC_(A_n,A_n) fails, and so Sel_(A_n) fails. The model satisfies I_n and refutes both D_n and T_n.

For n=0, use kappa=lambda=omega, delta=omega_1, nu=e and tau=et=A_0. The forcing has finite conditions and is c.c.c.; the countable-support filter is omega_1-complete. The same preservation theorem gives DC, so every e-input or t-input family has a selector at every output type. The base domains omega and {0,1} are unchanged. Code each Cohen real directly in D_et, and use a ground injection omega_1→D_et^V to index the block relation. The same argument refutes FC_(et,et) and Sel_et. Thus the n=0 endpoint requires no nonexistent beth_(-1).

The incomparability claims concern these one-world models. No simultaneous proposition-Infinity claim is made for this comparison. All boxes are the original boxes, which collapse to truth in these models; no unboxed-to-boxed transfer under an optional assumption has been used.

## 12. Fixed input is fundamentally different from fixed output

Fix any admitted input type sigma and put

    J_sigma = C + {□FC_(sigma,tau): tau relational}.

**Theorem.** J_sigma is not finitely axiomatizable over C. More precisely, let A be C or B, and let Theta be any finite collection of pure truths of the full ground Gallin hierarchy E=W=omega. Then A+Theta+{□FC_(sigma,tau):tau relational} has no finite basis over A+Theta. The target includes the stated extra base; this does not assert that J_sigma by itself entails B or Theta. In particular the result survives both boxed quantified Infinity axioms. This is a theorem for each fixed sigma, not an object-language quantification over types.

This contrasts with fixing an output tau: all its input instances are equivalent over C to the one sentence □Sel_tau. The direction of the type restriction matters.

To prove the theorem, let Delta be any finite set of the boxed instances with input sigma. In the full ground Gallin hierarchy every fixed type has an infinite domain: D_e=omega, while each relational type contains the distinct constant intensions obtained from the infinitely many propositions. Choose mu=beth_m, m≥1, large enough to bound D_sigma, all output domains in Delta, their subtypes, and every type in the fully elaborated formulas of Theta. Put kappa=mu^+, nu=rho_(m+1), and tau_*=nu t.

Use the countable-block forcing from §11,

    P = Fn(omega × kappa × kappa, 2, <kappa),

with within-block column permutations and **finite** supports. All the selected ground domains remain unchanged in the symmetric ZF model N, including D_t=P(omega), D_sigma and D_nu. Their ground well-orders remain available. In particular, at every world, the least-output construction verifies all of Delta, even for relations newly present in N. The finite formulas Theta retain their entire ground proposition denotations.

Choose ground injections h:omega→D_sigma and b:kappa→D_nu. Code the columns by functions x_(i,j):D_nu→D_t using the rigid values W and the empty set according to b[a_(i,j)]. As in §11, the invariant family

    B_i={x_(i,j):j<kappa},       i<omega,

belongs to N and has no N-choice function. This is the same finite-support argument: a candidate name touches only finitely many blocks, leaving one block in which a fresh-column transposition contradicts a forced evaluation.

Define the rigid relation from D_sigma to D_(tau_*) which requires an output in B_i at input h(i), and uses bottom_(tau_*) elsewhere. It is internally serial at every world. Any operation selecting at even one world would, on the rigid rows at h(i), give the forbidden N-function i↦f(h(i)). Hence FC_(sigma,tau_*) fails at every world. The index maps h and b are ground set maps used in the semantic construction; no new object-language constants, type quantifiers or individual-valued arrows are introduced.

The full internal Gallin hierarchy also satisfies B: at each world a total single-valued relation is represented by Replacement, and this is true at every world. Both boxed quantified Infinity axioms hold by the direct internal argument at the end of §9, independently of how many formula types were preserved. Thus the construction supplies B+Theta+Delta+□not FC_(sigma,tau_*). The usual finite-proof and first-order extraction argument gives a ground general C-model of these sentences.

If a finite set Gamma axiomatized J_sigma over any of the stated bases, finite derivations of its members would use only finitely many instances Delta. The model just constructed would satisfy Gamma but refute a further axiom of J_sigma, a contradiction. Taking Theta empty proves the original assertion. This concerns equivalent bases, not finite sufficient conditions that impose additional finite-domain restrictions.

As a consequence, each I_n is itself non-finitely axiomatizable over C: the same countable-block model can preserve any finite set of its instances by preserving their output domains, yet refute an e-input instance, which belongs to I_n. More strongly, no extension C+Gamma by finitely many F-theorems can entail J_sigma. Such a Gamma is implied by some T_m, and the same construction can preserve that T_m while refuting J_sigma by also preserving D_(A_m). This does not say that J_sigma is equivalent to one of the input-order schemata.

Two corollaries make the scope explicit. First, every deductively closed intermediate theory T with J_sigma⊆T⊆F has no finite basis over C: a finite basis would consist of finitely many F-theorems entailing J_sigma, which the preceding paragraph rules out. In particular, any nonempty collection of fixed-input, all-output Choice schemata is non-finitely axiomatizable over C, even when the collection of input types itself is infinite.

Second, adjoining finitely many such complete fixed-input packages still leaves F non-finitely axiomatizable over that enlarged base. If the listed input types have order at most n, their packages are all contained in I_n. The countermodel from §9 satisfying I_n and any proposed finite set Delta of further FC instances therefore satisfies those packages and Delta while failing F. These are consequences of the existing constructions, not additional assumptions.
