# Exterior-arm posets: native-Box separation need not persist

By OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 24 October 2026. Direct source: `misc`, this note, §§1–5, unpaginated. The action-model framework and earlier finite-frame results are attributed in §6.

On finite full poset models, idempotent native-Box separation is persistent: it says that the frame has binary joins, and every future cone then has binary joins. The next stabilization bound already behaves differently. An eight-point poset has two-step separation at its root but not at a future point. A ten-point poset has two-step separation at its root while a future cone fails even unrestricted native-Box separation. The latter single model refutes persistence of every fixed finite stabilization bound greater than one.

The construction adds short exterior branches to a future cone. Root observers use those branches as preimages for upper points. When the model is restricted to the future cone, those preimages are no longer available. The whole preservation identities and iteration bounds hold at the root; the failure concerns the existence of enough qualified observers at a future point.

## 1. Qualification and the full-frame criterion

Use $`B_r`$ from [Bounded stabilization](bounded-stabilization-native-box-observers.md), §1. For each fixed external integer r≥1, the qualifying observers satisfy $`\mathrm{Good}_\Delta`$, the whole native-Box identity, and the whole stabilization identity

```math
 E^{r+1}=E^r
 \tag{1}
```

Thus B₁ is IdemBoxSep. Unindexed $`B_r`$ and BoxSep mean the full schemata over relational output types and finite external qualification lists. A boxed schema means the separately boxed closed instances. No quantifier over r is introduced into the object language.

Interpret a finite rooted poset P as a thin category, with singleton individuals and the full action-model domains. The coordinate theorem in [Idempotent native-Box observers](idempotent-native-box-observers.md), §§3–4, gives:

* D_t(x) is the powerset of ↑x, with current truth given by membership of x.
* Every Boolean observer is a coordinate reader E_f for an inflationary map f:P→P, where x≤f(x). Every such reader satisfies $`\mathrm{Good}_\Delta`$ for every finite Δ.
* The whole native-Box identity is equivalent to

```math
 f[\mathord\uparrow x]=\mathord\uparrow f(x)
 \quad\text{for every }x\in P
 \tag{2}
```

* The whole identity (1) is equivalent to fʳ⁺¹=fʳ.

At the root o, separation by such observers holds exactly when every a∈P can be read, meaning f(o)=a for some qualified f. An unread point a gives the distinct propositions ∅ and {a} with the same current truth under every observer in the class. Conversely the readers distinguish every pair of distinct subsets. Current Functionality lifts this to all relational output types.

These facts include the full composite quantifier identities. At any future x, a coordinate reader evaluates at f(x); transport from x to f(x) is onto at every type in the full thin-category model. This makes both quantifier composites agree on every future predicate input. Consequently the success statements below hold at all types and all finite lists, while the failures already occur at propositional output and Δ={t}.

The same model satisfies C and the necessary schemata BF, Functionality, ordinary OS and Atomicity_t. Our frames also have a common top, so .2 holds at every point: any nonempty future cone contained in p includes top, which every other future point can access. Hence all the constructions retain

```math
 \mathrm C+\Box\mathrm{BF}+\Box\mathrm{Functionality}
 +\Box\mathrm{OS}+\Box\mathrm{Atomicity}_t+\Box .2
 \tag{3}
```

## 2. The exterior-arm family

Fix positive external integers m,n. The frame $`A_{m,n}`$ has the following distinct points:

```math
 o,\quad v,\quad
 A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\},\quad
 B=\{b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}\},\quad
 \{z_1,\ldots,z_{n-1}\},\quad 1
 \tag{4}
```

Its order is generated by

```math
 o<v<a_j<b_i<1
 \quad(1\le j\le m,\ 0\le i<n),
 \qquad
 o<z_i<b_i<1
 \quad(1\le i<n)
 \tag{5}
```

There are no further comparisons except those required by reflexivity and transitivity. In particular each z_i is incomparable with v, every a_j, every other arm point, and every b_k for k≠i. There is no arm below the distinguished upper point b₀. The frame has m+2n+2 points.

The cone ↑v is exactly the two-antichain frame $`P_{m,n}`$: its levels are v, A, B and 1. Every other proper cone is a join-semilattice. The cone above a_j is an n-armed star with a common top; each arm cone ↑z_i and each ↑b_i is a chain.

Every point other than a member of A has a join with every point of the whole frame. For v, joining with z_i gives b_i, and the other joins are determined by comparison. For z_i, joining with v or any a_j gives b_i, while joining with a different arm or upper point gives 1. An upper point b_i joins any point below it to itself, and any point not below it to 1. The root and top give the remaining immediate cases.

Thus every target outside A has an idempotent qualified reader x↦x∨target. If m=1 or n=1, the remaining pairs also have joins. If m,n≥2, two distinct members of A have n incomparable minimal upper bounds, so they have no join. In particular the root has B₁ exactly when m=1 or n=1.

## 3. Two-step root readers

Fix a∈A. Define f_a on the whole frame by

```math
\begin{aligned}
 f_a(o)&=a,\\
 f_a(v)=f_a(a_j)&=b_0 &&(1\le j\le m),\\
 f_a(z_i)&=b_i &&(1\le i<n),\\
 f_a(b_i)&=1 &&(1\le i<n),\\
 f_a(b_0)&=b_0,\qquad f_a(1)=1
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
```

It is inflationary. Its cone images can be checked without any counting or search:

| Input x | f_a[↑x] | ↑f_a(x) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| o | {a}∪B∪{1} | ↑a |
| v or any a_j | {b₀,1} | ↑b₀ |
| z_i | {b_i,1} | ↑b_i |
| b_i with i≥1 | {1} | ↑1 |
| b₀ | {b₀,1} | ↑b₀ |
| 1 | {1} | ↑1 |

Thus (2) holds everywhere. The only possible nontrivial two-step orbits are

```math
 o\longmapsto a\longmapsto b_0\longmapsto b_0,
 \qquad
 z_i\longmapsto b_i\longmapsto 1\longmapsto 1
 \tag{7}
```

Every other orbit stabilizes sooner. Hence f_a³=f_a² as maps on the entire frame. The corresponding observer is Good at every finite list, commutes with native Box as a whole operation, and satisfies E³=E² as a whole operation.

Together with the join readers for targets outside A, these readers cover every root read point. Therefore

```math
 \boxed{\text{Every full model on }A_{m,n}
 \text{ satisfies full }B_2\text{ at }o}
 \tag{8}
```

By class inclusion, it also satisfies $`B_r`$ for every fixed external r≥2 and unrestricted BoxSep. This says that each such schema holds in the same model; it does not add an infinitary qualification formula.

The exterior arm is essential to the displayed mechanism. The reader f_a maps the entire cone ↑v into {b₀,1}; its missing upper values b_i are supplied by the exterior inputs z_i. Its restriction to ↑v is still a qualified two-step reader, but it reads b₀ there. It does not provide a reader of a at v.

## 4. Exact persistence thresholds in the family

The earlier [two-antichain calculation](bounded-stabilization-native-box-observers.md), §3, establishes at the root of $`P_{m,n}`$

```math
\begin{aligned}
 B_1&\Longleftrightarrow m=1\ \text{or}\ n=1,\\
 B_2&\Longleftrightarrow m=1\ \text{or}\ n=1\ \text{or}\ m>n,\\
 B_r\ (r\ge3)&\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{BoxSep}
 \Longleftrightarrow m=1\ \text{or}\ n\le m
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
```

For completeness, the obstruction is as follows. If m,n≥2 and f reads a lower point a from the root of $`P_{m,n}`$, no upper point can be fixed. Otherwise, fixing b forces every lower point other than a to map to b; the other upper points move to top and cannot be read. Thus all upper points map to top. The n upper values must have preimages among the m lower points, requiring n≤m for BoxSep. If f³=f², the value f(a) must already be top. Only m−1 lower inputs remain to supply the n upper values, requiring m>n. Finite surjections provide the converse readers. Height three makes every inflationary map stabilize by three steps. The join criterion gives the B₁ row.

At o in $`A_{m,n}`$, the root itself has B₂ by (8), and every proper cone other than ↑v has B₁. Therefore (9) gives the exact table

```math
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 B_1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Box B_1
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=1\ \text{or}\ n=1,\\
 \Box B_2
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=1\ \text{or}\ n=1\ \text{or}\ m>n,\\
 \Box B_r\ (r\ge3)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Box\mathrm{BoxSep}
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m=1\ \text{or}\ n\le m
\end{aligned}}
\tag{10}
```

These are evaluations in the specified finite full models. The parameters m,n and each index r are external finite integers, and boxes apply separately to the closed separation instances.

### Eight points: two steps do not persist

Take $`A_{2,2}`$. The root has B₂, while ↑v is P_{2,2}, which has B₃ but fails B₂. Hence

```math
 A_{2,2},o\models B_2+\Box B_3+\neg\Box B_2
 \tag{11}
```

The failed boxed instance can be taken at output t and Δ={t}. No minimality claim about eight points is needed.

### Ten points: even unrestricted native-Box separation fails later

Take $`A_{2,3}`$. Name its lower points a,a′, its upper points b₀,b₁,b₂, and its arm points z₁,z₂. Formula (6) gives a root reader for a; replacing only its root value by a′ gives the other exceptional reader. The whole frame has ten points.

At v, the cone P_{2,3} cannot read either lower point by any native-Box qualified observer: three upper values would need preimages among only two lower inputs. Thus BoxSep(t,{t}) fails there. Since every $`B_r`$ observer is also a BoxSep observer, the same pair ∅,{a} in D_t(v) witnesses failure of $`B_r(t,\{t\})`$ for every fixed r.

Consequently this single finite full poset model satisfies

```math
\boxed{
 B_2+\neg\Box\mathrm{BoxSep},
 \qquad
 B_r+\neg\Box B_r\quad\text{for every fixed external }r\ge2
}
\tag{12}
```

It retains the entire necessary background (3). Failure of persistence therefore does not depend on a future failure of ordinary logical separation, Barcan, Functionality, propositional Atomicity or .2.

## 5. What is and is not inherited by future cones

The finite-poset inheritance question now has an exact negative answer beyond the idempotent case:

* B₁ is cone-hereditary in the full finite-poset family, because it is equivalent to binary joins.
* For every fixed r≥2, $`B_r`$ is not cone-hereditary, even when the root already satisfies the stronger B₂.
* BoxSep is not cone-hereditary in this family either.

These statements concern full models on antisymmetric finite frames. They do not reuse the earlier preorder counterexample with a nontrivial terminal cluster. The additional exterior points explain the new failure: an observer can satisfy every whole qualification while using preimages that lie outside the future cone being evaluated. Restriction preserves the observer's equations; it need not preserve the availability of readers for all points of that cone.

No general-frame representation or general logical equivalence is inferred from the exact finite-model tables. The consequences for H are the verified nonimplications supplied by these models, with the fixed-list failures and all-type successes stated above.
